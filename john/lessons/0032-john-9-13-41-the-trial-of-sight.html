Lesson 0032 · Unit 5: Chapters 9-10

John 9:13-41: The Trial of Sight

~30 min · Passage: John 9:13-41 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?

Write down at least five observations: how many rounds of questioning there are and who is questioned, how the man's descriptions of Jesus develop, what his parents do and why, the sentence beginning "one thing I know", and the last exchange about blindness.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The Sabbath detail is withheld until now (v. 14). John tells us only after the healing that it happened on a Sabbath, letting the reader feel the shift from wonder to interrogation.
  • The Pharisees are divided (v. 16). "This man is not from God, for he does not keep the Sabbath." Others: "How can a man who is a sinner do such signs?" Division follows Jesus everywhere in this book.
  • The man's titles for Jesus climb. "The man called Jesus" (v. 11), "he is a prophet" (v. 17), "from God" (v. 33), "Lord, I believe" and worship (v. 38). Track that ladder; it is the spine of the chapter.
  • They doubt the premise (v. 18). Rather than accept the miracle, they question whether he had been blind at all, and summon the parents.
  • The parents duck (vv. 20-23). They confirm the two facts nobody can deny, then refuse the third: "Ask him; he is of age." The narrator explains: fear of being put out of the synagogue.
  • Excommunication was already agreed (v. 22). "The Jews had already agreed that if anyone should confess Jesus to be Christ, he was to be put out of the synagogue." The cost of confession is set in advance.
  • Round three opens with pressure (v. 24). "Give glory to God. We know that this man is a sinner." An oath formula used to demand a retraction.
  • His famous answer (v. 25). "Whether he is a sinner I do not know. One thing I know, that though I was blind, now I see." Testimony without pretending to expertise.
  • He turns the interrogation around (v. 27). "Do you also want to become his disciples?" Bravery with a needle in it.
  • Insult and pedigree (vv. 28-29). "You are his disciple, but we are disciples of Moses." Chapter 5:45-46 has already answered that.
  • The best theological argument in the chapter is made by the beggar (vv. 30-33). "Why, this is an amazing thing! You do not know where he comes from, and yet he opened my eyes... Never since the world began has it been heard that anyone opened the eyes of a man born blind. If this man were not from God, he could do nothing."
  • They excommunicate him (v. 34). "You were born in utter sin, and would you teach us?" They reach for the very doctrine Jesus rejected in verse 3.
  • Jesus seeks him out (v. 35). "Jesus heard that they had cast him out, and having found him he said..." He is thrown out of one place and found by the one who matters.
  • Full confession and worship (vv. 36-38). "Who is he, sir, that I may believe in him?" Then, "Lord, I believe", and he worshiped him. Jesus receives the worship.
  • The verdict (v. 39). "For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind."
  • The closing sting (v. 41). "If you were blind, you would have no guilt; but now that you say, 'We see,' your guilt remains."

Interpretation

Why is the beggar the hero of the chapter?

He answered, "Whether he is a sinner I do not know. One thing I know, that though I was blind, now I see." John 9:25, ESV

John gives this uneducated man the strongest logic and the best lines in the chapter, and it is deliberate. Consider what he does under pressure that no one else does. He tells the truth simply and repeatedly, without embellishing it. He refuses to speculate beyond what he knows. He reasons from what he has actually experienced to a conclusion that follows: an unprecedented act requires an explanation, and God does not answer sinners' prayers in this way, so this man must be from God. And he pays for it, losing his place in the community his parents were desperate to keep. Meanwhile the trained experts assert, insult, threaten, and finally expel. The chapter's argument is that spiritual sight is not a function of education or standing. The man who can see least at the start ends up seeing most, and the men certain of their vision end blind. There is also a practical model of witness here for anyone who feels unqualified. You are not required to answer every objection. You are required to tell the truth about what Christ has done, admit the limits of what you know, and be willing for it to cost something.

What does "for judgment I came" mean?

Jesus said, "For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind." John 9:39, ESV

Set beside 3:17, "God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world", this looks like a contradiction, and the resolution is important. His purpose in coming was salvation; the inevitable effect of his coming is division, because light both reveals and exposes. The same sun that opens a healthy eye is unbearable to a diseased one. So "those who do not see" are people who know they are blind, and they receive sight; "those who see" are people confident of their own vision, and their confidence is the disease. Verse 41 spells it out: claimed sight is what makes guilt remain. This is why the whole chapter runs on a double meaning, physical blindness healed and spiritual blindness hardened, and it is why the last people to be helped by Jesus are usually the ones who feel no need of him. Take the warning personally. The Pharisees were not villains in a costume; they were serious religious people who had studied their Bibles and were sure they could see. The only protection against their end is the beggar's posture: I was blind, and everything I have, I was given.

Application

  • About God He seeks out the man who was thrown out. The excommunication was the setup for the personal revelation, and the worship in verse 38 is received rather than refused.
  • About you Verse 22 names a real cost: confession can lose you a community. The parents chose safety; the son chose the truth and got Christ. That trade is still on offer in various forms.
  • Do Write your own "one thing I know" sentence: one concrete thing Christ has done in you that you would not retract under pressure. Keep it short enough to say in ten seconds.
