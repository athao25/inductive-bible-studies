Lesson 0055 · Unit 8: Chapters 18-21

John 19:31-42: Pierced and Buried

~25 min · Passage: John 19:31-42 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 19:31-42 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: why the legs were to be broken and why his were not, what came out when the spear went in, the two Scriptures quoted, what verse 35 claims about the witness, and who buries him.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • A religious motive for a brutal request (v. 31). The bodies must come down before the Sabbath, "for that Sabbath was a high day", so they ask for the legs to be broken to hasten death.
  • Breaking legs was standard. It prevented the victim from pushing up to breathe, which brought suffocation quickly.
  • He is already dead (v. 33). The soldiers, professionals at this, see it and skip him. Any theory that Jesus merely fainted has to get past an execution detail whose lives depended on certainty.
  • The spear, and what came out (v. 34). "One of the soldiers pierced his side with a spear, and at once there came out blood and water." A medically striking detail, reported by someone standing there.
  • An explicit testimony claim (v. 35). "He who saw it has borne witness, and his testimony is true, and he knows that he is telling the truth, that you also may believe." John stops the narrative to say he was there.
  • First Scripture: not one bone broken (v. 36). Exodus 12:46 and Numbers 9:12 command it of the Passover lamb; Psalm 34:20 says it of the righteous man.
  • Second Scripture: they will look on him (v. 37). Zechariah 12:10, "they will look on me, on him whom they have pierced", where the speaker is the LORD.
  • Joseph of Arimathea (v. 38). "A disciple of Jesus, but secretly, for fear of the Jews." He asks Pilate for the body, which is a public act.
  • Nicodemus, one last time (v. 39). "Nicodemus also, who earlier had come to Jesus by night." He brings about seventy-five pounds of myrrh and aloes.
  • A royal quantity (v. 39). That much spice is a burial for a king, and it is enormously expensive.
  • Bound in linen with the spices (v. 40). "According to the burial custom of the Jews."
  • A new tomb in a garden (v. 41). Never used, so no confusion about who rose. And a garden, which is where this story began in a different sense.
  • Haste (v. 42). "Because of the Jewish day of Preparation, since the tomb was close at hand." The women will come back after the Sabbath with more spices.

Interpretation

Why does John make so much of the unbroken bones and the pierced side?

For these things took place that the Scripture might be fulfilled: "Not one of his bones will be broken." And again another Scripture says, "They will look on him whom they have pierced." John 19:36-37, ESV

Two prophecies are fulfilled by two decisions made by bored soldiers who had no interest in Scripture. They chose not to break his legs, which preserved the Passover lamb requirement; and one of them chose to make sure with a spear, which fulfilled Zechariah. Neither choice was in anyone's control but God's. That is why John pauses in verse 35 to say he watched it: this is evidence offered so "that you also may believe." Then feel the weight of the first quotation. From 1:29 the book has called him the Lamb of God, and here, at the hour the Passover lambs are being killed, the requirement that no bone be broken is kept in his body. The second quotation is even bolder in context. In Zechariah 12:10 the LORD says "they will look on me, on him whom they have pierced", and John applies it to Jesus without comment, which is one more quiet claim of deity in a Gospel full of them. As for the blood and water, John reports it as fact rather than explaining it; medically it is consistent with a pierced heart or pleural fluid, and it establishes beyond doubt that he was dead. Some see a symbol here as well, since blood speaks of atonement and water of the Spirit and cleansing (compare 7:38-39), but the primary function is testimony.

What happens to the secret disciples?

Joseph and Nicodemus are the two men John has told us were afraid. Joseph is "a disciple of Jesus, but secretly, for fear of the Jews", and Nicodemus first appears coming by night. On the day everyone else scatters, these two go to the Roman governor to claim the body of a man executed for sedition, and carry seventy-five pounds of burial spices through the city in daylight. There is no way to do that quietly. Whatever they had been, on the day of the crucifixion they came out. Two observations are worth keeping. First, notice when it happened: not at a triumph but at the apparent end. The cross that scattered the bold drew out the timid. Second, track Nicodemus across the whole Gospel, since John clearly wants you to: a night visit and confusion in chapter 3, a nervous appeal for due process in chapter 7, and a hundred pounds of spices in chapter 19. That is a slow conversion, and John records every step of it without impatience. If you are praying for someone who has been circling the faith for years, the pattern in this Gospel is longer than a season.

Application

  • About God The Passover lamb requirement is kept in the body of his Son by soldiers who knew nothing about it. His providence extends to the smallest details of the worst day in history.
  • About you Fear does not have the last word about anyone's discipleship. Joseph and Nicodemus prove it, publicly, at the worst possible moment.
  • Do Write down the name of one person you have quietly written off as never going to believe. Pray for them by name every day this week, and read John 19:39 while you do it.
