Unit 1 Recap: The Word Made Flesh

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating John 1-2

Six lessons, two chapters, and the foundation of the whole Gospel is laid. Today you retrieve it, connect it, and say it in your own words. Retrieval feels harder than rereading; that difficulty is the point, and it is what makes the memory stick.

Say it without looking

Before any quiz, take paper and try these three from memory. Then check yourself against the answers below.

  1. Recite John 1:14 (memory verse 1).
  2. List as many of the titles given to Jesus in chapters 1-2 as you can. There were roughly ten in chapter 1 alone.
  3. In one sentence each: what did the sign at Cana announce, and what did the temple saying claim?
Check your answers
(1) "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth." (2) The Word, God, the Light, the Lamb of God, the Son of God, Rabbi, the Messiah/Christ, the one Moses and the prophets wrote of, the King of Israel, the Son of Man; chapter 2 adds the implicit claim in "my Father's house". (3) Cana: the age of purification-management gives way to the abundance and joy of the messianic age, glory manifested. Temple: God's dwelling on earth is now his body, destroyed by them and raised by him in three days.

Recall: the whole unit

The unit in one frame

Watch how deliberately John built these two chapters. The prologue (1:1-18) told you who Jesus is from eternity: the Word who was with God and was God, life and light, tabernacling in flesh, exegeting the Father. Then the witness (1:19-34) told you who he is from Scripture: the Lord whose way is prepared, the Lamb God provides, the Spirit-anointed Son of God. Then the disciples (1:35-51) told you who he is by confession: Rabbi, Messiah, King of Israel, and he himself added Son of Man, the ladder between heaven and earth. Then two acted claims: at Cana (2:1-12), the purification jars fill with wedding wine, glory manifested, the old order brimming over into joy; at the temple (2:13-25), the Son claims his Father's house and names his own body the true temple. Words, witness, confession, sign, claim: five converging lines, all drawn before a single sermon is preached in this Gospel.

And two darker threads are already laid down for the chapters ahead. "His own people did not receive him" (1:11) waits behind every coming controversy. And "he knew what was in man" (2:25) stands as John's warning about sign-faith, handing us directly to Nicodemus, the impressive religious man who is about to be told that what is in man requires nothing less than a new birth.
