Lesson 0013 · Unit 2: Chapters 3-4

John 4:27-45: The Harvest

~25 min · Passage: John 4:27-45 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 4:27-45 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: what the disciples marvel at and what they refuse to ask, what the woman leaves at the well, how she words her testimony, what Jesus calls his food, and what the villagers finally say.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • They marvel but stay silent (v. 27). "They marveled that he was talking with a woman, but no one said, 'What do you seek?'" Disapproval kept politely unspoken. John records the human texture.
  • The water jar is left behind (v. 28). The thing she came for is abandoned. Small detail, large meaning: the errand is forgotten because the errand has been answered.
  • Her testimony is a question, not a lecture (v. 29). "Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did. Can this be the Christ?" She invites rather than argues, and she leads with the thing she once hid.
  • "Come and see" again (v. 29). The same words Philip used in 1:46. John's model of witness is repeatedly an invitation to look.
  • They came out to him (v. 30). Present tense in the Greek narrative, a moving crowd in the middle distance while the disciples are still talking about lunch.
  • Food they do not know about (vv. 31-33). Another literal misunderstanding: "Has anyone brought him something to eat?" The pattern of chapters 3 and 4 continues.
  • His food defined (v. 34). "My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to accomplish his work." Sent-ness is the driving category of this Gospel, and "accomplish" anticipates "It is finished" (19:30).
  • The proverb overturned (v. 35). "Do you not say, 'There are yet four months, then comes the harvest'? Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes... the fields are white for harvest." A crowd of Samaritans is walking toward them right then.
  • Sower and reaper together (vv. 36-38). "One sows and another reaps"; "others have labored, and you have entered into their labor." Ministry is a relay, and joy is shared at the end of it.
  • Two stages of faith in the village (vv. 39-42). Many believed "because of the woman's testimony", then more believed "because of his word", and they tell her so plainly.
  • The title at the end (v. 42). "We know that this is indeed the Savior of the world." Said by Samaritans, the half-outsiders, which is exactly the point of 3:16's "world".
  • The proverb about a prophet's hometown (vv. 43-45). Galileans welcome him, but the narrator notes why: "having seen all that he had done in Jerusalem at the feast." Sign-driven welcome again, which sets up the next lesson.

Interpretation

What kind of witness does John hold up?

So the woman left her water jar and went away into town and said to the people, "Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did. Can this be the Christ?" John 4:28-29, ESV

Notice everything she does not have. No training, no standing, no reputation worth trading on, and a past that would have disqualified her in most people's eyes. What she has is an encounter and a question. She points away from herself to a person, uses the worst fact about her life as the evidence, and invites people to look for themselves. And it works: verse 39 says many believed because of her testimony. Then verse 42 shows the healthy next step. Their faith moves from her word to his: "it is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves." That is not a rebuke of her; it is the goal of all witness. Your job is to get people to Jesus, not to keep them dependent on your account of him. If you have been waiting to feel qualified before you speak about Christ, this woman has just taken that excuse off the table.

Why is the harvest "now"?

Look, I tell you, lift up your eyes, and see that the fields are white for harvest. John 4:35, ESV

The disciples had a four-months-yet mentality: real work later, lunch now. Jesus tells them to lift their eyes, and the field walking toward them is Samaritan. The urgency is not manufactured; it is a matter of seeing what is actually there. Then he protects them from two opposite errors. Against pride, verse 38: "others have labored, and you have entered into their labor." Someone prepared this ground: the prophets, the Baptist, a woman with a water jar, perhaps the person who first told you the gospel. Against discouragement, verse 36: "sower and reaper may rejoice together." If you sow and never see the harvest, you have not missed the joy; it is stored up and shared. That combination is a mercy for anyone who has taught a class, raised children in the faith, or answered the same question for years without visible result. And it is worth noticing what Jesus calls this labor in verse 34: food. Not duty, not depletion. Doing the Father's will is what feeds him.

Application

  • About God The Father is seeking worshipers (4:23) and the Son calls that seeking his food. What God is doing in the world is not a side project; it sustains the Son himself.
  • About you Your worst chapter can be the doorway of your testimony, as it was for her. And your unfinished work is not wasted; you may be sowing for a reaper you never meet.
  • Do Write one sentence of testimony in her shape: what he did in your life, then a question that invites. Say it to one person this week. Keep it under thirty seconds and do not defend yourself in it.

Check yourself

Before next lesson
Read John 4:46-54 in your ESV, twice, and jot five observations. Watch: who the man is and what he asks, what Jesus says about signs and wonders, what he does not do (note the distance), what the man does after the promise, and how the servants' report is timed.
Primary source
In your MacArthur Study Bible (Olive Tree), read the notes on 4:34 (his food) and 4:35-38 (harvest, sower and reaper). Five minutes. Ask one question of the notes: what exactly is the "labor" others had already done in Samaria?
Church prompt
Ask an older believer at church who sowed the seed in their life, and whether that person ever saw the harvest. Then tell them who sowed in yours. It is a short conversation that tends to go somewhere.

Questions welcome, always: "who are the 'others' who labored?", "why did the Galileans' welcome not count for much?", "is 'Savior of the world' a universalist claim?" Ask your teacher.
