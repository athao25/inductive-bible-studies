Lesson 0024 · Unit 4: Chapters 7-8

John 7:1-24: The Feast of Booths

~25 min · Passage: John 7:1-24 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 7:1-24 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: what his brothers advise and what the narrator says about them, what Jesus says about time, how he explains where his teaching comes from, what test he gives for knowing it, and what he says about judging.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Six months have passed (v. 1-2). From Passover (6:4) to Booths (Tabernacles) in the autumn. John's calendar keeps moving.
  • The threat is explicit (v. 1). "He would not go about in Judea, because the Jews were seeking to kill him." Chapter 5 has consequences.
  • Booths matters for what follows (v. 2). The feast recalled the wilderness tents, and by the first century it included daily water pouring from Siloam and the lighting of huge lamps in the temple court. Chapter 7's water claim and chapter 8's light claim both land inside that liturgy.
  • His brothers push publicity (vv. 3-4). "Show yourself to the world." Sound marketing advice, offered by men who want a spectacle.
  • The narrator's aside (v. 5). "For not even his brothers believed in him." James and Jude, later leaders in the church, are unbelievers at this point. Family faith can come late.
  • Two kinds of time (v. 6). "My time has not yet come, but your time is always here." He lives on an appointed schedule; they live on impulse.
  • Why the world hates him (v. 7). "Because I testify about it that its works are evil." The world cannot hate his brothers because it has no quarrel with them.
  • He goes, but privately (vv. 8-10). Not on their terms and not in their parade. The distinction is between refusing their agenda and refusing the feast.
  • The crowd is muttering in factions (vv. 11-13). "He is a good man"; "no, he is leading the people astray"; and nobody speaks openly "for fear of the Jews".
  • He teaches midway through the feast (v. 14). Public, in the temple, at the height of the crowds.
  • The credential question again (v. 15). "How is it that this man has learning, when he has never studied?" No rabbinic pedigree.
  • The source of the teaching (v. 16). "My teaching is not mine, but his who sent me."
  • The test anyone can run (v. 17). "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God." Willingness precedes certainty.
  • The motive test (v. 18). Whoever speaks on his own seeks his own glory; whoever seeks the glory of the sender is true.
  • The counterattack (vv. 19-23). "Has not Moses given you the law? Yet none of you keeps the law." Then the circumcision argument: you circumcise on the Sabbath to keep Moses' law, so why be angry that I made a man's whole body well?
  • The closing rule (v. 24). "Do not judge by appearances, but judge with right judgment." Not a ban on judging; a demand that it be done properly.

Interpretation

Why refuse his brothers' advice?

Jesus said to them, "My time has not yet come, but your time is always here." John 7:6, ESV

Their strategy was reasonable by every human standard: you have a following in Galilee, go where the crowds are, build the platform. What made it wrong was the assumption underneath, that visibility is always good and timing is always now. Jesus lives by an appointed hour, so he cannot be moved by opportunity, pressure, or family expectation. It is worth noticing whose voices are pushing here. Not enemies. Brothers, who mean it, and who do not believe. The nearest people to us often give the most confident bad advice, and John flags in verse 5 that sincerity is no guarantee of insight. There is also comfort buried in this paragraph for anyone with unbelieving family. His own brothers mocked him during his ministry, and two of them wrote New Testament letters after the resurrection. Keep praying; the timeline is not yours.

How do you know whether the teaching is from God?

If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God or whether I am speaking on my own authority. John 7:17, ESV

This is one of the most useful verses in the Gospel for anyone dealing with doubt, and it inverts the usual order. We assume the sequence is: get convinced, then obey. Jesus says willingness to do God's will is the condition of recognizing God's voice. That is not anti-intellectual, and it does not mean evidence is irrelevant. It means moral posture governs perception, which is precisely what 3:19-21 and 5:44 already said: people avoid the light because of works, and cannot believe because they trade in each other's approval. So when someone says they cannot accept Christianity, the honest follow-up question is not always about evidence. It is often, "if it were true, would you want it?" Ask yourself that too, especially about the parts of Scripture you resist. Then verse 24 gives the companion rule: judge, but not by appearances. His opponents had judged the healing at Bethesda by its optics on a Sabbath rather than by what it was: a whole man made well by the God who never stops working. Surface judgment is lazy judgment, and it is still the common failing of religious people.

Application

  • About God He is not driven by opportunity. The Son moves on the Father's schedule even when the crowds, the family, and the moment all say now.
  • About you If you are stuck on a question of faith, run verse 17 honestly. Ask whether the obstacle is evidence or willingness, and be specific about what you would have to give up if it were true.
  • Do Take one snap judgment you have made recently about a person or a situation, and redo it by verse 24: what were the appearances, and what would right judgment require you to find out first?
