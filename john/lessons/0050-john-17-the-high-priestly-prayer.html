John 17: The High Priestly Prayer

~30 min · Passage: John 17:1-26 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 17 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: the three sections and who each is about, what he asks for himself, what he asks for the disciples, what he asks for future believers, and how many times some form of "give" appears.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The hour, addressed in prayer (v. 1). "Father, the hour has come; glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you." The clock that started at 2:4 has run out.
  • Authority over all flesh, for a purpose (v. 2). "To give eternal life to all whom you have given him." Universal authority aimed at a particular people.
  • Eternal life defined (v. 3). "That they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." Relational knowledge, not duration alone.
  • Work finished before the cross (v. 4). "Having accomplished the work that you gave me to do." He speaks of it as done.
  • Preexistent glory (v. 5). "Glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed." A claim no creature could make.
  • "Yours they were, and you gave them to me" (v. 6). The language of a people given by the Father to the Son, repeated throughout (vv. 2, 6, 9, 11, 12, 24).
  • He prays not for the world (v. 9). "I am not praying for the world but for those whom you have given me." Specific intercession, which does not contradict God's love for the world in 3:16.
  • Kept and guarded (v. 12). "I have guarded them, and not one of them has been lost except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled."
  • Joy is a request (v. 13). "That they may have my joy fulfilled in themselves."
  • Not taken out, but kept from the evil one (v. 15). The definitive answer to Christian withdrawal from the world.
  • Sanctified in the truth (v. 17). "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth." Set apart by means of the word.
  • Sent as he was sent (v. 18). "As you sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world."
  • He prays for us (v. 20). "I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word." That is every Christian since.
  • Unity with a purpose (vv. 21-23). "That they may all be one... so that the world may believe that you have sent me." The unity is modeled on the Father and Son and is missionary in effect.
  • The final request (v. 24). "Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory."
  • The last line (v. 26). "That the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them."

Interpretation

What does this prayer reveal about salvation?

I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world. Yours they were, and you gave them to me, and they have kept your word. John 17:6, ESV

Count the giving. The Father gives the Son authority (v. 2), gives him a people (vv. 2, 6, 9, 24), gives him words to speak (v. 8), gives him glory (v. 22), and gives him work to finish (v. 4). This is the same structure as chapter 6 and chapter 10, now in the form of a prayer, which is a very different thing from a debate. Election, in John, is not a cold abstraction argued at a distance; it is Jesus speaking to his Father about people he is hours from dying for and naming them as a gift he received. Notice too that verse 9, "I am not praying for the world", is not a denial of God's love toward the world but a description of what this particular prayer is doing: interceding for those given him. And keep verse 20 in view, because it is the most personal verse in the chapter for you. He prayed for people who would believe through the apostles' word, which means that on the night before he died, Jesus prayed for you by category if not by name. Read the prayer again with that in mind and notice what he asked for: keeping, joy, protection from the evil one, sanctification in the truth, unity, and finally that you would be with him and see his glory.

What kind of unity does he pray for?

That they may all be one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me. John 17:21, ESV

Three things define it. Its model is the Father and the Son, which means the unity is real and personal rather than institutional. Its ground is being "in us", so it exists because believers share the same life, not because they signed the same agreement. And its purpose is evangelistic: "so that the world may believe." That last clause is why divisions among Christians are not merely unpleasant but evidentially damaging. Two guardrails. First, this is not a call to unity at the expense of truth, since verse 17 in the same prayer asks that they be sanctified in the truth, and a unity that discards the word is not the unity he requested. Second, it is not a demand for uniformity of practice or a single earthly organization; the New Testament shows local churches with real differences and real fellowship. What is required is that Christians who share the same Lord actually love each other visibly, which is 13:35 again. Ask the practical question this raises. Is there a believer or a congregation you speak about in a way that would make this prayer harder to answer?

Application

  • About God The Son's last recorded act before Gethsemane is intercession for his people, and the last thing he asks is that they be with him and see his glory. That is what he wants for you.
  • About you Verse 15 forbids escape. You are not asked to leave the world but to be kept in it, sanctified by the word and sent into it as he was sent.
  • Do Pray through John 17 this week, one section a day, turning each request into a prayer for yourself and your church. It is the most direct way to learn what Christ actually wants for Christians.
