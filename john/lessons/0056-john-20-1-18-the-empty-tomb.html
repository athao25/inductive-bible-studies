Lesson 0056 · Unit 8: Chapters 18-21

John 20:1-18: The Empty Tomb

~25 min · Passage: John 20:1-18 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 20:1-18 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: who comes first and what she concludes, who runs and who believes, exactly how the linen cloths are described, how Mary finally recognizes him, and what message he sends through her.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Early, while still dark (v. 1). John's light and darkness motif is still running on the morning it ends.
  • Mary Magdalene comes first (v. 1). In a culture where a woman's testimony carried little legal weight, all four Gospels make women the first witnesses. Nobody inventing a story would do that.
  • Her conclusion is theft, not resurrection (v. 2). "They have taken the Lord out of the tomb, and we do not know where they have laid him." Expectation shapes interpretation.
  • The race (vv. 3-4). "Both of them were running together, but the other disciple outran Peter." A detail with a smile in it, written by the man who won.
  • He looks in but waits (v. 5). Then Peter, characteristically, goes straight inside (v. 6).
  • The cloths are lying there (vv. 6-7). The linen cloths lying, and "the face cloth, which had been on Jesus' head, not lying with the linen cloths but folded up in a place by itself."
  • Grave robbers do not undress a body (vv. 6-7). Nor do they leave seventy-five pounds of spice-soaked wrappings behind, tidily. The scene is orderly, which is the opposite of theft.
  • "He saw and believed" (v. 8). Of the other disciple, on the strength of the evidence, before any appearance.
  • They did not yet understand the Scripture (v. 9). Belief came before comprehension, and the Old Testament connections were made later.
  • They go home (v. 10). Mary stays.
  • Two angels, and a strange calm (vv. 11-13). She speaks to them without amazement, still on the theft theory. Grief has narrowed her world.
  • She mistakes him for the gardener (vv. 14-15). Ironic on several levels, and the tomb was in a garden (19:41).
  • One word (v. 16). "Mary." Recognition comes through her name, which is exactly what 10:3 promised: he calls his own sheep by name.
  • "Do not cling to me" (v. 17). Not a rebuke of affection but a redirection: the relationship is changing, and he has not yet ascended.
  • The message is a family announcement (v. 17). "Go to my brothers and say to them, 'I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.'" The first time he calls them brothers.
  • The first preacher of the resurrection (v. 18). "Mary Magdalene went and announced to the disciples, 'I have seen the Lord.'"

Interpretation

What did the other disciple see that made him believe?

Then the other disciple, who had reached the tomb first, also went in, and he saw and believed. John 20:8, ESV

He had not seen Jesus. What he saw was cloth. John describes it with unusual care: the linen wrappings lying there, and the face cloth folded up in a place by itself. Think about what each possible explanation would leave behind. Thieves in a hurry take the body wrapped, or if they unwrap it, they leave a mess and probably steal the spices, which were worth a fortune. A revived victim of crucifixion, weak from blood loss, does not extricate himself from tight, spice-soaked wrappings and then tidy the head cloth. The scene is undisturbed, and the body is gone from inside the wrappings. That is what he saw, and it was enough. Two things follow for us. First, John clearly regards the physical evidence as reasonable ground for faith, which is consistent with 20:31: he is writing so that readers may believe on the basis of testimony they can weigh. Second, verse 9 keeps it honest: they believed before they understood the Scriptures. Faith does not require having every piece assembled first.

Why does Jesus reveal himself to Mary this way?

Jesus said to her, "Mary." She turned and said to him in Aramaic, "Rabboni!" (which means Teacher). John 20:16, ESV

She had looked straight at him and seen a gardener. Then he says one word, her name, and she knows him instantly. That is the shepherd of chapter 10 in action: "he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out... the sheep follow him, for they know his voice." Notice the order of the whole resurrection chapter, too. The risen Christ appears first not to Pilate, not to Caiaphas, not to the council, but to a woman from whom he had cast out seven demons (Luke 8:2), weeping alone in a garden. That is entirely in character for this Gospel, which gave the first "I am he" to a Samaritan woman at a well. Then verse 17 does two things at once. "Do not cling to me" tells her that the old form of the relationship is ending; he is not returning to resume walking the roads of Galilee, but ascending to the Father. And "my brothers... my Father and your Father, my God and your God" tells her what the new form is. The men who abandoned him three days ago are called brothers, and his Father is now theirs. That is what the cross purchased, announced in one sentence to the person least likely to be believed by anybody.

Application

  • About God He comes to the grieving before he comes to the powerful, and he calls people by name.
  • About you Mary's error is instructive: she was standing in front of the answer and could not see it because she had already decided what must have happened. Grief and assumption both narrow vision.
  • Do Say aloud, as a fact rather than a feeling, "his Father is my Father, his God is my God." Then tell one person this week, in one sentence, that Jesus is risen. Mary's whole commission was to go and say it.
