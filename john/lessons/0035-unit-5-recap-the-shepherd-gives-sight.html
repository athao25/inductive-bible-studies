Unit 5 Recap: The Shepherd Gives Sight

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating John 9-10

One sign and one discourse, joined at the hip. A man born blind is given sight and thrown out by the shepherds of Israel; the true Shepherd finds him, and then explains in front of them exactly what has happened. Today you retrieve it and put it in your own words.

Say it without looking

  1. Trace the four titles the healed man gives Jesus, in order.
  2. State the three marks and the three gifts of the sheep in 10:27-28.
  3. Recite memory verses 1 through 5 in order (1:14; 3:16; 6:35; 8:12; 10:27-28).
Check your answers
(1) "The man called Jesus" (9:11), "he is a prophet" (9:17), "from God" (9:33), "Lord, I believe" with worship (9:38). (2) Marks: they hear his voice, he knows them, they follow him. Gifts: eternal life, they will never perish, no one will snatch them from his hand, with the Father's hand named as well.

Recall: the whole unit

The unit in one frame

Chapter 9 is a story about seeing, told twice. On the surface a blind beggar receives eyes he never had, made from clay and washed off in a pool named Sent. Underneath, a man who knew nothing at the start ends on his face worshiping, while men certain of their sight talk themselves into blindness. The interrogations are the mechanism: four rounds of pressure, and every round the beggar sees more clearly and the experts see less. The chapter ends with the verdict of 9:39-41, that the ones who admit blindness receive sight, and the ones who claim sight keep their guilt.

Chapter 10 explains it. The men who cast out that sheep are the false shepherds of Ezekiel 34, and Jesus takes for himself the role God claimed there: I will seek them out myself. He is the door, so access to God has one entrance; he is the good shepherd, so the flock is kept by an owner rather than an employee; he lays down his life for the sheep, deliberately and with authority to take it up again; he has other sheep to bring, so the flock will be one across every division. At Dedication they demand plainness, and get it: my sheep are in my hand and my Father's hand, and I and the Father are one. Then stones, again, and a quiet ending across the Jordan where many believed.
