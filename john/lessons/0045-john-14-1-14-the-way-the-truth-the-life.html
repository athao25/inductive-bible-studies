John 14:1-14: The Way, the Truth, the Life

~25 min · Passage: John 14:1-14 (ESV, read first!) · Memory verse 7 activates

If you have not yet read John 14:1-14 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: the command about their hearts, what the Father's house promise says and does not say, Thomas' question, the sixth "I am" saying, Philip's request and the answer, and the promise about greater works.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • A command, not a suggestion (v. 1). "Let not your hearts be troubled." Spoken to men who have just heard about a betrayer, a departure, and a denial.
  • Believing in him is set parallel to believing in God (v. 1). "Believe in God; believe also in me." Another quiet claim to deity.
  • The Father's house has room (v. 2). "In my Father's house are many rooms. If it were not so, would I have told you that I go to prepare a place for you?"
  • The promise is personal presence (v. 3). "I will come again and will take you to myself, that where I am you may be also." The point of heaven, stated as him.
  • Thomas admits confusion (v. 5). "Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?" His honesty gets the greatest answer in the book.
  • The sixth "I am" (v. 6). "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." One claim, four clauses, and the last one is exclusive.
  • Knowing him is knowing the Father (v. 7). "From now on you do know him and have seen him."
  • Philip wants a theophany (v. 8). "Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us." A reasonable request in Old Testament terms.
  • The answer is stunning (v. 9). "Have I been with you so long, and you still do not know me, Philip? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father."
  • Mutual indwelling (vv. 10-11). "I am in the Father and the Father is in me." And the fallback: "Believe on account of the works themselves."
  • Greater works promised (v. 12). "Whoever believes in me will also do the works that I do; and greater works than these will he do, because I am going to the Father."
  • Asking in his name (vv. 13-14). "Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son." The purpose clause is the key to the promise.

Interpretation

What does "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" claim?

Jesus said to him, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." John 14:6, ESV

Thomas asked for directions and got a person. Read the three titles as an answer to three questions. How do we get there? He is the way, not a teacher of the way. What is real? He is the truth, not merely a truthful speaker. What do we need? He is the life, not a supplier of it. The Gospel has been saying each of these separately (1:4; 8:32; 11:25) and here they are gathered into one sentence. Then comes the clause that makes this the most offensive verse in the modern world: "no one comes to the Father except through me." Three things are worth saying carefully about it. First, exclusivity is not arrogance if the claim is true; a doctor who says there is one cure is either wrong or kind, but not proud. Second, the verse is not narrow about who may come, only about how; "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (6:37) is the same speaker. Third, notice that the exclusivity is inseparable from the incarnation. If God has really come and really died to open the way, then to say there are other ways is to say the cross was unnecessary, which is not generosity but insult. Hold the verse the way John does: as comfort for troubled hearts first, and as a boundary second.

What are the "greater works"?

Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever believes in me will also do the works that I do; and greater works than these will he do, because I am going to the Father. John 14:12, ESV

Greater in what sense? Not in raw power, since nobody has outdone raising a four-day-dead man. The reason given is the key: "because I am going to the Father." His departure inaugurates the age of the Spirit and of worldwide mission. During his earthly ministry he was one man in one region speaking one language, and the recorded results were mostly measured in individuals. After Pentecost, ordinary believers preached and three thousand were converted in a day, and the message crossed the empire within a generation. Greater in extent, in scope, and in effect: raising the spiritually dead through the gospel in every nation is a greater work than any single healing, because it accomplishes the thing all the signs pointed to. This reading also keeps the promise from becoming a burden or a boast. It is not a guarantee that every believer will perform miracles on demand, and it does not fail if you do not. It is a promise that what Christ began will not be smaller after his ascension, and that you have a place in the work. Then verses 13 and 14 attach the engine: prayer in his name, meaning prayer consistent with who he is and aimed at the Father's glory in the Son, which is exactly why the promise is not a blank check.

Memory verse 7

Jesus said to him, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." John 14:6, ESV

Say it aloud three times today, once tomorrow, and again at week's end. This is the verse you will be asked about most often by people outside the faith, so learn it in full rather than in half; the second sentence is the one that gets quoted at you. Schedule at memory verses.

Application

  • About God "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father." If you want to know what God is like, you are not left to speculation. Read the Gospels slowly.
  • About you The cure for a troubled heart in this passage is not distraction or resolve; it is believing specific things about him and where he is going. Anxiety is answered with content.
  • Do Write out one thing currently troubling your heart. Beside it write verses 1 to 3 in your own words, addressed to yourself. Then pray one request "in his name", meaning shaped by his glory rather than only your relief.
