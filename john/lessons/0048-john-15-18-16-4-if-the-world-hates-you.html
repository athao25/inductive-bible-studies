John 15:18-16:4: If the World Hates You

~25 min · Passage: John 15:18-16:4 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 15:18 to 16:4 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: the reason given for the world's hatred, the psalm quoted, what removes any excuse, the double witness of the Spirit and the disciples, what is predicted about synagogues, and why he tells them beforehand.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Expectation set (v. 18). "If the world hates you, know that it has hated me before it hated you." Not if in the sense of doubt; it is a matter of course.
  • The reason is election out of the world (v. 19). "Because you are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you." Belonging elsewhere provokes it.
  • The servant and master principle (v. 20). "If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you. If they kept my word, they will also keep yours." Both halves are promised.
  • It is ignorance of the Father (v. 21). "All these things they will do to you on account of my name, because they do not know him who sent me."
  • Words remove excuse (v. 22). "If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have been guilty of sin, but now they have no excuse."
  • Hating him is hating the Father (v. 23). The same logic as honoring the Son in 5:23.
  • Works remove excuse too (v. 24). "If I had not done among them the works that no one else did..."
  • Hatred without a cause (v. 25). Quoting Psalm 35:19 or 69:4, and calling it "their Law". Even the hatred is scripturally anticipated.
  • A double witness (vv. 26-27). "The Spirit of truth... will bear witness about me. And you also will bear witness, because you have been with me from the beginning."
  • The purpose of the warning (16:1). "I have said all these things to you to keep you from falling away." Forewarned is fortified.
  • Two specific predictions (16:2). Put out of the synagogues, and "whoever kills you will think he is offering service to God." Religious sincerity as the engine of persecution.
  • The root cause repeated (16:3). "And they will do these things because they have not known the Father, nor me."
  • Why not earlier (16:4). "I did not say these things to you from the beginning, because I was with you." His presence had been their shield.

Interpretation

Why does the world hate Christians?

If you were of the world, the world would love you as its own; but because you are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world, therefore the world hates you. John 15:19, ESV

The reason given is not that Christians are obnoxious, though sometimes we are, and John is careful to specify. It is a matter of belonging: you have been chosen out, so you no longer fit, and the difference itself is an accusation. Add 3:20, where the light is hated because it exposes works, and 7:7, where the world hates Jesus because he testifies that its works are evil. That is the mechanism. Two guardrails matter here. First, this is not permission to be disliked for the wrong reasons. Peter is blunt about it: suffer as a Christian, not as a busybody or a criminal (1 Pet 4:15-16). If people find you insufferable rather than finding Christ offensive, verse 19 is not your verse. Second, the passage does not say everyone will hate you. Verse 20 has two halves, and the second one promises that some will keep your word just as some kept his. The Gospel that says the world will hate them is the same Gospel that says whoever comes to him is never cast out. Expect both, and do not become bitter when the first happens or smug when the second does.

Why warn them in advance?

I have said all these things to you to keep you from falling away. John 16:1, ESV

Unexpected suffering shakes faith in a way that expected suffering does not. If you have been told that following Christ guarantees comfort, the first serious cost feels like evidence that the whole thing was false. So he tells them, plainly and in detail, hours before it starts: hatred, expulsion, and eventually death at the hands of people who believe they are serving God. That last prediction deserves a pause. Verse 2 says the killers will think they are offering worship, which Paul later confirms from personal experience (Acts 26:9-11), and which has been repeated in every century since. Sincerity is not a virtue by itself; it is only as good as its object. The pastoral value of the whole paragraph is in verse 4: "when their hour comes you may remember that I told them to you." That is the function of forewarning. It converts a crisis into a confirmation. If you are suffering for Christ today, this passage is not an explanation of why God has failed you; it is proof that he told you and that you are exactly where he said his followers would be.

Application

  • About God He is honest with his people. There is no fine print in this discourse, and the promise of the Spirit sits in the same paragraph as the promise of hatred.
  • About you Test any opposition you face against verse 19 and 1 Peter 4:15-16. Is the offense Christ, or is it you?
  • Do Pray this week by name for one believer you know of who is under real pressure for their faith, whether across the world or across your office. Then send them a message saying so.
