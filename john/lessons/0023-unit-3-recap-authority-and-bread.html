Unit 3 Recap: Authority and Bread

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating John 5-6

Two chapters that turned the temperature up. Chapter 5 put Jesus on trial for claiming equality with God, and he answered by claiming more. Chapter 6 fed thousands and then preached them away. Today you retrieve it and say it in your own words.

Say it without looking

  1. List the four witnesses of John 5.
  2. State the promises of 6:37-40 in your own words, in three sentences.
  3. Recite memory verses 1, 2, and 3 in order (1:14; 3:16; 6:35).
Check your answers
(1) John the Baptist, the works the Father gave him to do, the Father himself, and the Scriptures, with Moses named as prosecutor at the end. (2) Everyone the Father gives to the Son will come; anyone who comes is never cast out; the Son's assignment is to lose none of them and to raise them on the last day.

Recall: the whole unit

The unit in one frame

Chapter 5 is about authority. A man paralyzed for thirty-eight years is healed on a Sabbath by a Christ who did not wait to be asked; the objection over a carried bedroll escalates into a charge of blasphemy when Jesus says "My Father is working until now, and I am working". His defense claims the two things that belong to God alone, giving life and executing judgment, and demands that he be honored exactly as the Father is honored. Then he produces four witnesses, all of which the court already accepted, and turns their own hero into their accuser: Moses wrote of me.

Chapter 6 is about bread, which is to say about what a person actually lives on. He feeds a wilderness crowd at Passover, refuses the crown they offer, walks to his disciples across a dark sea saying "I am", and then explains the sign: he is the true manna, and the manna their fathers ate could not keep anyone alive. When the crowd wants a supply, he offers a person. When they want a list of works, he gives one work, believing. When they grumble at his ordinariness, he tells them that no one comes unless the Father draws, and that everyone drawn will be raised. When they recoil at flesh and blood, he intensifies rather than softens, and the crowd leaves. Twelve remain, one of them a traitor, and Peter says the sentence the chapter was written to produce: "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life."

The sovereignty verses of chapter 6, in one place

VerseWhat it says
6:29Believing is the work of God
6:37All the Father gives will come; whoever comes is never cast out
6:39The Son's will: lose nothing, raise it up
6:44No one can come unless the Father draws; that person is raised
6:45All taught by God; everyone who learns from the Father comes
6:65No one can come unless it is granted by the Father

Read them straight through once a year. They are the clearest single block on this subject in the Gospels, and they are addressed to a crowd that is walking away, which is exactly why they function as comfort rather than speculation.
