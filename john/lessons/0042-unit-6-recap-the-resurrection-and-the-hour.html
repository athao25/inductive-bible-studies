Unit 6 Recap: The Resurrection and the Hour

~30 min · No new passage · Consolidating John 11-12 and the Book of Signs

This recap closes two chapters and half a Gospel. Chapters 1 to 12 are the Book of Signs; chapters 13 to 21 are the Book of Glory. Today you consolidate the hinge.

Say it without looking

  1. List the seven signs with chapter numbers.
  2. Explain in three sentences why Jesus delayed two days before going to Bethany.
  3. Recite memory verses 1 through 6 in order (1:14; 3:16; 6:35; 8:12; 10:27-28; 11:25-26).
Check your answers
(1) Water into wine (2), the official's son (4), the invalid at Bethesda (5), feeding the five thousand (6), walking on the sea (6), the man born blind (9), and the raising of Lazarus (11). (2) The delay was caused by love, not despite it; it was aimed at the glory of God and at their believing; and an earlier arrival would have produced a healing instead of the sign that displays him as the resurrection and the life.

Recall: the whole unit

The unit in one frame

Chapter 11 is the seventh and greatest sign. A friend dies while Jesus deliberately waits; two sisters meet him with the same reproach; and to the one who believes in a resurrection on the last day he says that the last day is standing in front of her. He weeps, he is angry at death, and then he calls a four-day-dead man out of a tomb by name. The immediate result is a formal decision to kill him, and a high priest who states the doctrine of substitution while intending only politics.

Chapter 12 turns everything toward the cross. Mary pours out a year's wages on his feet and is defended against a thief's principled objection; a crowd hails him as a nationalist king and he answers with a donkey and Zechariah; Greeks arrive and the hour finally comes. Then the seed must fall into the ground, the soul is troubled and does not ask to be spared, the ruler of this world is cast out, and the lifting up will draw a people from everywhere. The Book of Signs ends with a verdict, Isaiah quoted twice to explain both the rejection and its cause, secret believers who love human glory too much, and a closing summary in which every word Jesus spoke is traced back to the Father.

Notice the progression: from a wedding to a grave, from private guests to a public tomb, and from a want to a corpse. The signs escalate until only one thing is left to conquer, which is exactly where the Book of Glory begins.
