Lesson 0001 · Orientation

How to Study Scripture

~25 min · No passage prerequisite · The one skill every other lesson builds on

Before John, the tool. This course uses inductive study: you draw meaning out of the text instead of reading your assumptions into it. Today you learn the three steps and practice them on two verses of John. If you completed the Matthew course, treat this as your annual sharpening; the method is the same, but the practice text is new and John will stretch it in new directions.

Why method matters

Scripture is God speaking (2 Tim 3:16). If God has spoken, then the meaning of a passage is not up to us; our job is to hear what he actually said. That conviction, common to MacArthur, White, and Durbin and to the whole Reformed tradition, is why they all preach expositionally: verse by verse, letting the text set the agenda. You are about to do the same thing at a personal scale, 20 minutes at a time, for 21 chapters.

The three steps

StepQuestionDiscipline
1. ObservationWhat does it say?Look long. List facts: who, what, when, repeated words, connectors, contrasts.
2. InterpretationWhat does it mean?What did the author intend the original audience to understand? Context first; Scripture interprets Scripture.
3. ApplicationWhat must change?What does this reveal about God? What in me must be confessed, believed, obeyed?

The order is not decoration. Skip observation and your interpretation floats free of the text. Skip interpretation and your application applies something the passage never said. The full cheat sheet lives at reference: inductive method; it is your daily template from tomorrow on.

One rule for your helps
You own the MacArthur Study Bible in Olive Tree. Excellent tool, wrong first move. Always make your own observations before opening any note or commentary. Helps confirm and correct your reading; they must never replace it.

Practice: John 20:30-31

Two verses, and not random ones: this is the author telling you why he wrote the book you are about to study. Read them slowly, twice. Then, before scrolling on, write down at least four observations on paper.

Now Jesus did many other signs in the presence of the disciples, which are not written in this book; but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name. John 20:30-31, ESV

Done writing? Compare with what careful observation surfaces:

  • The author admits selection: "many other signs... are not written in this book". John chose what to include and what to leave out. Every episode in this Gospel is therefore on purpose; nothing is filler.
  • The miracles have a name: John calls them "signs", not wonders or works of power. A sign points beyond itself. Watch for what each one points to.
  • There were witnesses: the signs were done "in the presence of the disciples". John is claiming eyewitness testimony, not legend collected later.
  • The book has a stated aim: "these are written so that you may believe". The target is you, the reader. This is not a neutral biography; it is a sworn deposition seeking a verdict.
  • Two titles carry the claim: "the Christ, the Son of God". One is Israel's promised Messiah; the other is a claim about his relation to God. The whole book argues both.
  • Believing has a payoff: "that by believing you may have life in his name". Belief is not the finish line; life is. And the life comes "in his name", through the person, not the information.

Notice what happened: none of that required a commentary. It required slowing down. That is the skill this whole course trains.

A word about "simple" passages
John's vocabulary is the simplest in the New Testament; first-year Greek students start here. Do not be fooled. His plainest sentences carry his deepest freight, and the danger in John is not boredom but overfamiliarity: verses so well known you no longer see them. The cure is the same discipline you just practiced: look long, and write down what is actually there.
