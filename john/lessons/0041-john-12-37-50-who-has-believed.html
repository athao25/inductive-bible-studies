Lesson 0041 · Unit 6: Chapters 11-12

John 12:37-50: Who Has Believed

~25 min · Passage: John 12:37-50 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 12:37-50 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: the summary John gives of the public ministry, the two Isaiah quotations and what each explains, the group who believed secretly and why, and the main claims of the closing summary in verses 44 to 50.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The verdict on the Book of Signs (v. 37). "Though he had done so many signs before them, they still did not believe in him." Chapters 1 to 12 close with unbelief.
  • Isaiah 53:1 explains the response (v. 38). "Lord, who has believed what he heard from us?" The rejection was foretold, and in the very chapter about the suffering servant.
  • Isaiah 6:10 explains the cause (v. 40). "He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart." John says "therefore they could not believe" (v. 39).
  • The astonishing claim about Isaiah (v. 41). "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory and spoke of him." The glory Isaiah saw in the temple in chapter 6 was the glory of Christ.
  • Secret believers among the rulers (v. 42). "Nevertheless, many even of the authorities believed in him, but for fear of the Pharisees they did not confess it."
  • The diagnosis is blunt (v. 43). "For they loved the glory that comes from man more than the glory that comes from God." Compare 5:44.
  • A closing summary, not a new scene (vv. 44-50). No location or audience is given; John gathers the public message into a final statement.
  • Believing in him is believing in the Father (vv. 44-45). "Whoever believes in me, believes not in me but in him who sent me. And whoever sees me sees him who sent me."
  • Light again (v. 46). "I have come into the world as light, so that whoever believes in me may not remain in darkness."
  • His purpose is salvation (v. 47). "I did not come to judge the world but to save the world." Consistent with 3:17.
  • The word itself will judge (v. 48). "The word that I have spoken will judge him on the last day." Rejected truth becomes the standard of judgment.
  • Everything traced to the Father (vv. 49-50). "For I have not spoken on my own authority... And I know that his commandment is eternal life."

Interpretation

How can John say they "could not believe"?

Therefore they could not believe. For again Isaiah said, "He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart, lest they see with their eyes, and understand with their heart, and turn, and I would heal them." John 12:39-40, ESV

This is one of the hardest paragraphs in the Gospel, and it needs to be handled carefully rather than hurried past. Four observations keep it in balance. First, John does not offer this as an excuse for the unbelievers, because the immediately preceding verses hold them responsible: they saw the signs and would not believe. Both things are said in the same breath. Second, judicial hardening in Scripture is always a response to prior, willing resistance. Pharaoh hardened his own heart before God hardened it, and Romans 1 describes God giving people over to what they already wanted. Nobody is hardened out of neutrality. Third, note where the quotation comes from. Isaiah 6 is the passage where a prophet is commissioned to preach to a people who will not hear, and Isaiah 53 is the passage where the servant is despised and rejected. John's point is that the rejection of Christ was not a surprise or a failure but exactly what the prophets said would happen, which means the cross is not plan B. Fourth, keep verse 42 in view. Even here, in the darkest verdict in the book, "many even of the authorities believed". The hardening is not universal, and the Gospel that says "they could not believe" also says "whoever comes to me I will never cast out". Both sentences are in the same book, written by the same man, for the same purpose.

What is wrong with secret faith?

For they loved the glory that comes from man more than the glory that comes from God. John 12:43, ESV

John does not tell us whether these rulers were saved, and it is worth resisting the urge to settle it. What he does tell us is the mechanism, and it is the same one from 5:44: a life funded by human approval cannot pay the price of confession. Two of these men, Nicodemus and Joseph of Arimathea, will step into the open at the burial, which suggests silence is not always permanent. But the New Testament does not treat confession as optional (Rom 10:9-10; Matt 10:32-33), and John's phrasing here is a warning rather than a description of a valid option. Do not read it as an attack on introverts or on prudence; the issue is not volume but allegiance. The question the verse presses is simple: whose approval is currently funding your sense of worth, and what would it cost you to say plainly that you belong to Christ in the room where it would be least welcome? Then read verses 44 to 50 as the answer to why it is worth it. Believing in him is believing in the Father, seeing him is seeing the Father, and his word is eternal life. There is nothing on the other side of the ledger.

Application

  • About God He was not defeated by unbelief. It was foretold in the prophets, and Isaiah saw the glory of Christ centuries before the manger.
  • About you Verse 48 says the word you have heard will be the standard on the last day. Reading John is therefore not a neutral hobby, which is a serious thought and also a hopeful one.
  • Do Read Isaiah 6:1-10 and Isaiah 53 in one sitting this week with John 12:37-41 open. Write one sentence on what it means that Isaiah "saw his glory".
