Lesson 0017 · Unit 3: Chapters 5-6

John 5:19-29: The Son Gives Life

~25 min · Passage: John 5:19-29 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 5:19-29 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: what the Son cannot do alone, what the Father shows him and why, the two prerogatives handed to the Son, what verse 24 promises in present tense, and the two resurrections of verses 28-29.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • This is a defense, not a new topic (v. 19). They charged him with making himself equal with God (v. 18). His answer expands the claim rather than denying it.
  • "The Son can do nothing of his own accord" (v. 19). Not weakness but perfect unity of action: he never acts independently of the Father, which is exactly what a mere man would be unable to say.
  • "Whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise" (v. 19). Not some things. All things, in the same way.
  • Love is the reason for revelation (v. 20). "For the Father loves the Son and shows him all that he himself is doing." The relationship is affectionate before it is functional.
  • Greater works are promised (v. 20). "Greater works than these, so that you may marvel." He has just healed a paralytic; raising the dead is coming.
  • Two divine prerogatives claimed (vv. 21-22). Giving life to whom he will, and all judgment. In the Old Testament both belong to God alone (Deut 32:39; Gen 18:25).
  • "To whom he will" (v. 21). The Son's life-giving is sovereign and discriminating, matching 3:8 and anticipating 6:37-39.
  • The purpose clause (v. 23). "That all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father." And the sting: whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him. There is no honoring God while bypassing Christ.
  • Verse 24 is a chain of three verbs. Hears, believes, has. And three results: eternal life now, no judgment, already passed from death to life. The tense work here is the whole point.
  • "An hour is coming, and is now here" (v. 25). Present-tense resurrection: the spiritually dead hear the Son's voice and live. This is regeneration language.
  • Life in himself (v. 26). "For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself." Self-existent life, possessed rather than borrowed moment by moment.
  • Judgment tied to "Son of Man" (v. 27). Daniel 7:13-14 stands behind it: the one who receives dominion and judges.
  • A second, future hour (vv. 28-29). "All who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out." Bodily resurrection of everyone, to life or to judgment. Notice: everyone hears the same voice.

Interpretation

How can Jesus be equal with God and dependent on him?

So Jesus said to them, "Truly, truly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise." John 5:19, ESV

Read carelessly, verse 19 sounds like a demotion, and groups that deny Christ's deity quote it constantly. Read in context it is the opposite. First, the whole speech answers a charge of equality by claiming more, not less: he gives life to whom he will, receives all judgment, and is to be honored exactly as the Father is. Second, "can do nothing of his own accord" is a statement of inseparable operation, the point being that there is no scenario in which the Son acts on a separate agenda. A creature could not say "whatever the Father does, the Son does likewise" without blasphemy. This is the shape of orthodox Trinitarian teaching: one God, three persons, undivided in works, with an eternal order of relation in which the Son is from the Father and is sent by the Father while being fully God with him. Verse 23 supplies the practical test, and it is uncomfortably clear. Any religion, any spirituality, any polite theism that honors God while withholding equal honor from the Son does not honor God at all. There is no back door to the Father.

Two hours, two resurrections

Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life. John 5:24, ESV

Verse 24 is one of the most concentrated promises in Scripture, and its verbs decide everything. "Has": present possession, not a probability. "Does not come into judgment": the verdict is settled, matching 3:18. "Has passed from death to life": a completed crossing, already behind the believer. That is the first resurrection, described in verse 25 as an hour that "is now here", when the spiritually dead hear the Son's voice and live. It is happening today, one person at a time, wherever the word of Christ raises someone who was dead in sin. Then verses 28-29 add a second hour, still future, when "all who are in the tombs" hear the same voice and come out bodily, some to life and some to judgment. John's habit of pulling the future into the present never dissolves the future; it guarantees it. Note the point of comfort and the point of soberness in the same paragraph: the voice that will one day empty every grave is the voice you can hear today in the gospel, and the response you give to it now is the resurrection you get then. Note also that verse 29 speaks of "those who have done good" and "those who have done evil". John is not contradicting salvation by grace through faith. He is doing what the whole New Testament does: works are the evidence that gets displayed at the judgment, never the currency that purchases the life.

Application

  • About God The Father loves the Son and shows him everything; the Son has life in himself and gives it freely. The life you need is not a substance he dispenses but a person he is.
  • About you If you have heard and believed, verse 24 says your crossing is already made. Assurance in this Gospel is not a feeling you generate; it is a sentence you learn to believe.
  • Do Memorize verse 24 this week, or at least write it on a card where you will see it daily. When accusation comes, read the three verbs out loud in order: hears, believes, has passed.
