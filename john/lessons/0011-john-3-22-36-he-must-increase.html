Lesson 0011 · Unit 2: Chapters 3-4

John 3:22-36: He Must Increase

~25 min · Passage: John 3:22-36 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 3:22-36 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: what the complaint to the Baptist is, what image he chooses for himself, what he says must happen, and how verse 36 ends the chapter with two abiding realities.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Two baptizing ministries overlap (vv. 22-23). "John also was baptizing at Aenon near Salim, because water was plentiful there." Historical detail from an eyewitness, and the setup for the rivalry.
  • The narrator dates it (v. 24). "For John had not yet been put in prison." John's Gospel quietly assumes readers know the Synoptic account and fills the gap before it.
  • The trigger is a dispute over purification (v. 25). Ritual washing arguments again. Chapter 2 filled purification jars; chapter 3 records people quarreling about them.
  • The complaint is about market share (v. 26). "He who was with you... look, he is baptizing, and all are going to him." The disciples are counting heads and losing.
  • The Baptist's first answer is theology of gift (v. 27). "A person cannot receive even one thing unless it is given him from heaven." He answers a jealousy question with sovereignty.
  • He restates his job (v. 28). "I am not the Christ, but I have been sent before him." Third time in the book he says who he is not (1:20-21; 1:23; here).
  • The wedding image (v. 29). Bridegroom, bride, and the friend of the bridegroom whose whole joy is the sound of the groom's voice. The best man does not marry the bride.
  • The sentence the whole passage is remembered for (v. 30). "He must increase, but I must decrease." Both verbs are necessity, not preference.
  • Above and earthly (v. 31). "He who comes from above is above all." The Nicodemus vocabulary returns: from above, from heaven, from the earth.
  • Testimony rejected, testimony received (vv. 32-33). "No one receives his testimony", then "whoever receives it sets his seal to this, that God is true". Hyperbole for the general response, with the exception named immediately.
  • The Spirit without measure (v. 34). "For he gives the Spirit without measure." The prophets received in portions; the Son does not.
  • Father, Son, all things (v. 35). "The Father loves the Son and has given all things into his hand." A summary sentence John will echo at 13:3 and 17:2.
  • Verse 36 is two present realities (v. 36). "Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him." Note "obey" as the opposite of "believe", and note "remains": wrath is the standing condition, not a new sentence.

Interpretation

How does the Baptist stay joyful while losing his crowd?

The one who has the bride is the bridegroom. The friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices greatly at the bridegroom's voice. Therefore this joy of mine is now complete. He must increase, but I must decrease. John 3:29-30, ESV

Two convictions hold him steady. First, verse 27: nothing anyone has, including a following, was ever theirs to begin with. If your ministry, gifts, and influence are all given from heaven, then a shrinking share is a reassignment, not a robbery. Second, verse 29: he knows his role in the story. In a Jewish wedding the friend of the bridegroom made the arrangements and then stepped aside; his joy came from the groom's happiness, not from the bride's attention. And there is a deeper current here. In the prophets, the bridegroom of Israel is the Lord (Isa 62:5; Hos 2:19-20). By taking the friend's role, the Baptist has just placed Jesus in God's role. That is the pattern for anyone in Christian service: the measure of health is not whether people are drawn to you but whether you can hear his voice and be glad it is his. "He must increase, but I must decrease" is not resignation. It is the sound of a man whose joy is "now complete" precisely because he is not the center.

Why does verse 36 pair "believe" with "obey"?

Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him. John 3:36, ESV

The verse sets up a contrast and then swaps the expected word: the opposite of believing turns out to be disobeying. That is not sloppy writing. In this Gospel, faith is never bare assent to information; it is the whole person receiving and submitting to the Son, so refusing him is disobedience to a command God has actually given (compare 1 John 3:23, "this is his commandment, that we believe"). Two further things deserve weight. First, "has": eternal life is present tense, already possessed, which matches 5:24. Second, "remains": the wrath of God is not something that arrives when a person finally crosses a line; it is the settled condition under which everyone stands until they are in the Son. That is a hard sentence, and John lets it end the chapter that contains 3:16 without softening either one. The same paragraph holds the freest possible offer and the most sober possible warning, and a study that keeps only one of them is not reading John.

Application

  • About God The Father loves the Son and has put all things in his hand (v. 35). The universe is not neutral territory; everything is already given to Christ.
  • About you Test yourself with verse 27 the next time someone else is preferred over you. If nothing you hold was self-earned, envy loses its logic. And test yourself with verse 30: is there any area where you need to get smaller for him to be seen?
  • Do Name one place where you have been quietly competing (at church, at work, online). Do one concrete act this week that increases the other person and decreases you: a public word of credit, a stepped-back role, a genuine congratulation.

Primary source
In your MacArthur Study Bible (Olive Tree), read the notes on 3:29-30 (the friend of the bridegroom) and 3:36 (believe, obey, wrath remains). Five minutes. Ask one question: why does the chapter end here rather than at verse 21?
Church prompt
Ask your pastor or a ministry leader how they handle it when a person or program they serve grows past them. The Baptist's answer is in verses 27 and 29; it is worth hearing how someone lives it.

Questions welcome, always: "is verse 31 the Baptist speaking or John commenting?", "what does 'without measure' mean?", "how can wrath 'remain' on someone God loves?" Ask your teacher.
