John 10:22-42: I and the Father Are One

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The Feast of Dedication, in winter (v. 22). Hanukkah, commemorating the cleansing and rededication of the temple after Antiochus defiled it. A feast about a false shepherd defeated and God's house restored, which is a fitting backdrop.
  • "It was winter" (v. 22). John notes weather rarely. Some read it as atmosphere for the coldness of the exchange.
  • They demand plainness (v. 24). "How long will you keep us in suspense? If you are the Christ, tell us plainly."
  • He says he has (v. 25). "I told you, and you do not believe. The works that I do in my Father's name bear witness about me."
  • The reason for unbelief is stunning (v. 26). "But you do not believe because you are not among my sheep." Not: you are not my sheep because you do not believe. The order runs the other way.
  • The marks and the gifts (vv. 27-28). Hear, are known, follow; eternal life, never perish, never snatched.
  • Two hands (vv. 28-29). "No one will snatch them out of my hand"; "no one is able to snatch them out of the Father's hand." Double grip.
  • The claim (v. 30). "I and the Father are one." The word for "one" is neuter, meaning one thing rather than one person: united in essence and action, not collapsed into a single identity.
  • Stones again (v. 31). The second stoning attempt in three chapters.
  • They state the charge (v. 33). "For blasphemy, because you, being a man, make yourself God." His hearers, again, are the clearest commentators.
  • The Psalm 82 argument (vv. 34-36). He quotes "I said, you are gods", spoken to unjust human judges, and argues from lesser to greater: if Scripture could call them gods, the charge cannot be automatic blasphemy when the one consecrated and sent by the Father says "I am the Son of God."
  • "Scripture cannot be broken" (v. 35). A parenthesis with enormous weight: Jesus' own view of the Bible, staked on a single phrase in an obscure psalm.
  • The evidence test (vv. 37-38). "If I am not doing the works of my Father, then do not believe me; but if I do them... believe the works, that you may know and understand that the Father is in me and I am in the Father."
  • Escape and retreat (vv. 39-40). He goes across the Jordan to where John was baptizing at the first.
  • A quiet, hopeful ending (vv. 41-42). "John did no sign, but everything that John said about this man was true. And many believed in him there."

Interpretation

What exactly does "I and the Father are one" claim?

I and the Father are one. John 10:30, ESV

Precision matters here, because the verse gets pulled in two wrong directions. It does not mean the Father and the Son are one person under two names, which the Greek rules out: "one" is neuter, one thing, not one person. Nor does it mean merely that they agree, like two colleagues with a shared goal, which the reaction rules out: nobody stones a rabbi for agreeing with God. The context tells you what unity is in view. Verses 28 and 29 have just said that no one snatches the sheep from the Son's hand and no one snatches them from the Father's hand, and verse 30 grounds the second promise in the first: the two hands are one grip because the two persons are one God. That is the Trinitarian shape the church later put into creeds, and it is here in a winter portico argument: distinct persons ("I and the Father"), one being ("are one"). Verse 38 says the same thing from another angle, mutual indwelling, "the Father is in me and I am in the Father".

How should we read his use of Psalm 82?

This is one of the more puzzling arguments in the Gospels, and it is often misread as Jesus backing down, as if he were saying "relax, everyone is a god in some sense". He is not. It is a rabbinic argument from the lesser to the greater. If the word of God, which cannot be broken, could apply the term "gods" to corrupt human judges who represented God's authority and were about to be judged for failing, then the term cannot be blasphemous by itself. So the charge collapses at the level of vocabulary, and the real question becomes the one he immediately puts back on the table: look at the works, and see whether the Father is in me. Two things are worth carrying away. First, notice the throwaway clause in verse 35, "Scripture cannot be broken". Jesus rests an argument on the precise wording of a single verse in a minor psalm, which tells you what he thought about the reliability of the whole text. That is worth more to your Bible-reading than the argument it appears in. Second, notice that he ends by inviting evidence rather than demanding submission: believe the works. He is confident enough in the record to let it be examined.

Application

  • About God Your security rests in two hands, not in your grip on either of them. That is the plain teaching of verses 28 and 29, and the reason it holds is verse 30.
  • About you Verse 26 reorders your instincts: believing is the evidence of belonging, not the cause of it. If you believe, that is not something you produced.
  • Do Memorize 10:27-28 if you have not yet. Then write down one fear about your future or your perseverance and answer it, in writing, with the promise of verse 29.
