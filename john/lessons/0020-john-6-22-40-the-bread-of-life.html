John 6:22-40: The Bread of Life

~30 min · Passage: John 6:22-40 (ESV, read first!) · Memory verse 3 activates

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 6:22-40 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: why Jesus says they are seeking him, what single work God requires, how they use Moses against him, the wording of the first "I am" saying, and every promise you can find in verses 37 to 40.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • They hunt him down (vv. 22-25). A whole boat operation to find him, and their opening question is logistical: "Rabbi, when did you come here?"
  • He ignores the question and names the motive (v. 26). "You are seeking me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate your fill of the loaves." They saw miracles but missed the sign in them.
  • Two kinds of food (v. 27). "Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give to you."
  • They hear "work" and ask for a list (v. 28). "What must we do, to be doing the works of God?" Plural: give us the tasks.
  • He gives one work, and it is a gift (v. 29). "This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent." Singular, and the "work" turns out to be receiving.
  • They ask for a sign the day after a sign (vv. 30-31). "Then what sign do you do?" And they quote manna: "He gave them bread from heaven to eat." Rabbinic expectation held that the Messiah would renew the manna.
  • He corrects three things about their proof text (v. 32). Not Moses but my Father; not gave but gives, present tense; not that bread but the true bread.
  • The bread is a person who comes down (v. 33). "The bread of God is he who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world."
  • They still want a supply (v. 34). "Sir, give us this bread always." Compare the Samaritan woman in 4:15. The literal misunderstanding pattern again.
  • The first "I am" saying (v. 35). "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst." Note the parallel: coming equals believing.
  • Seeing without believing (v. 36). "You have seen me and yet do not believe." Evidence has never been the bottleneck.
  • The chain of certainties (v. 37). "All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out." A giving, a coming, and a keeping.
  • The Son's purpose is the Father's will (vv. 38-39). "That I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day."
  • Repeated four times: the last day (vv. 39, 40, 44, 54). Resurrection is the promised terminus, stated like a refrain.
  • The will of the Father restated in terms of the responder (v. 40). "Everyone who looks on the Son and believes in him should have eternal life."

Interpretation

Why does the "work of God" turn out to be believing?

Jesus answered them, "This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent." John 6:29, ESV

The crowd asks the question every religious instinct asks: what is the list? Jesus collapses the plural into a singular and puts at the center the one activity that is not an achievement. Believing is not a work you perform to earn the food; it is the act of receiving the one who gives it. The grammar of the phrase even allows the deeper sense that this is the work God does, which fits the rest of the chapter, where coming to Christ is possible only because the Father gives and draws (vv. 37, 44, 65). Hold both together: you must believe, and no one believes apart from grace. Then watch what the true bread claim does to their manna argument. Manna was daily, temporary, given in the wilderness to people who all died anyway (v. 49). The true bread comes down once, gives life to the world, and raises the eater on the last day. Their proof text becomes his platform. This is a good example of how Jesus reads the Old Testament: not as a collection of achievements to admire but as a set of pictures whose original is standing in front of them.

What do verses 37 to 40 guarantee?

All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out. John 6:37, ESV

Read the sentence in two halves, because it is doing two jobs. The first half is the doctrine of sovereign grace at its plainest: there is a group the Father gives to the Son, and every one of them will come. Not may, not might if conditions cooperate. That is why Jesus is not anxious in the middle of a chapter where the crowd is about to desert him; the outcome does not depend on the crowd. The second half is pastoral in a way no one can wriggle out of: whoever comes will never be cast out. You cannot use the first half to keep yourself away, because the only way to discover whether you were given is to come, and the promise to anyone who comes is absolute. Then verses 38 to 40 pile on the security. The Son's assignment is to lose nothing and to raise it up at the last day, which means losing a believer would be a failure of the Son to do the Father's will. This is why John's Gospel is a favorite of the Reformed tradition, and MacArthur, White, and Durbin all press these verses the same way: election is not a cold doctrine here but the ground of assurance. Note finally the balance John keeps. Verse 37 says the Father gives; verse 40 says everyone who looks and believes has life. Both are the will of the Father in the same paragraph, and a study that keeps only one of them has stopped reading.

Memory verse 3

Jesus said to them, "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst." John 6:35, ESV

Say it aloud three times today, once tomorrow, and again at week's end. Notice while you learn it that "comes to me" and "believes in me" are the same act said twice, and that the promises answer both hunger and thirst, which quietly sweeps up chapter 4 as well. Full schedule at memory verses.

Application

  • About God He gives the true bread, and he gives the people who eat it. From first to last the initiative is his, and his stated intention is to lose none of them.
  • About you Test your motive with verse 26. Much seeking of God is really seeking of loaves: peace, order, a manageable life. Those are not evil, but they are not him.
  • Do Write out 6:37 by hand and put it where you keep your Bible. When you doubt whether you would be received, read the second half aloud. Then say the first half and let it take the weight off your shoulders.

Church prompt
Ask someone at church what they make of "all that the Father gives me will come to me". Then ask how they would use the second half of the verse to comfort a person afraid they are not chosen. That pairing is the pastoral heart of the chapter.

Questions welcome, always: "if the Father gives, why preach?", "how do I know I am among those given?", "what does 'the last day' mean here?" Ask your teacher.
