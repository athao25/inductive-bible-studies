John 11:1-16: This Illness Is for Glory

~25 min · Passage: John 11:1-16 (ESV, read first!)

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • A named family (vv. 1-2). Lazarus, Mary, and Martha of Bethany. John identifies Mary by an anointing he has not yet narrated (12:1-8), assuming his readers know the story.
  • The message contains no request (v. 3). "Lord, he whom you love is ill." They state the fact and the relationship, and leave the response to him. That is a model prayer.
  • The verdict on the illness (v. 4). "This illness does not lead to death. It is for the glory of God, so that the Son of God may be glorified through it." Lazarus does die, so "does not lead to death" means death is not the outcome or the point.
  • The most difficult "so" in the Gospel (vv. 5-6). "Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus. So, when he heard that Lazarus was ill, he stayed two days longer in the place where he was." The delay is caused by the love, not despite it.
  • The disciples remember the stones (v. 8). "Rabbi, the Jews were just now seeking to stone you, and are you going there again?" Chapter 10 is fresh.
  • Twelve hours in the day (vv. 9-10). Nobody stumbles while the light lasts. There is an allotted span for his work and no danger inside it.
  • Sleep as a euphemism (vv. 11-13). "Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep, but I go to awaken him." They misunderstand, so John explains: "Jesus had spoken of his death."
  • Plain speech, and a strange gladness (vv. 14-15). "Lazarus has died, and for your sake I am glad that I was not there, so that you may believe."
  • He knew from a distance (vv. 11, 14). Nobody brought news of the death; he simply states it.
  • Thomas, loyal and gloomy (v. 16). "Let us also go, that we may die with him." Courage without hope, which is exactly who Thomas is in this Gospel.

Interpretation

Why does love delay?

Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus. So, when he heard that Lazarus was ill, he stayed two days longer in the place where he was. John 11:5-6, ESV

Read that connective slowly, because everything in this chapter hangs on it. Not "yet he stayed", which is how we would write it, but "so he stayed". The delay is an expression of love, not an exception to it. Two days of silence while a friend dies and two sisters watch the road. John wants you to feel that, because you have felt it: the prayer that got no answer while the situation got worse. And he wants you to know the reason before you watch the scene, so you can carry it into your own waiting. The reason is verse 4 and verse 15: the glory of God, and "so that you may believe". Had Jesus arrived on day one, Lazarus would have been healed and everyone would have gone home with a story like several other stories. Because he waited, they got the seventh sign, the greatest of them all, and an unmistakable statement about who he is. That does not make the two days painless. It makes them purposeful. Notice what the delay is not: it is not indifference, not distraction, and not weakness. He knows the diagnosis, the death, and the timetable, and he loves them all the way through it. When God seems late, the options are not only "he does not care" and "he cannot act". This chapter adds a third, which is the one that turns out to be true more often than we expect.

What does "does not lead to death" mean when he dies?

Jesus is not making a medical prediction that fails. He is naming the destination rather than the route. Lazarus will pass through death, but death is not where this illness terminates; glory is. That way of speaking runs through the whole chapter and is the reason he can call the death "sleep". Sleep implies waking, and for the believer that is exactly what death is in this Gospel: not annihilation, not a final state, but a condition from which the voice of the Son will call (5:28-29; 11:43). Two cautions keep this from becoming sentimental. First, sleep is a description of the body, not a claim that the soul is unconscious; Jesus tells the thief he will be in paradise that day (Luke 23:43), and Paul expects to be with Christ at once (Phil 1:23). Second, calling death sleep does not deny how terrible it is. The one who says "he has fallen asleep" will be weeping at the tomb within thirty verses. Both are true at once, and refusing either one produces a Christianity that is either brutal or dishonest.

Application

  • About God His timing is never neglect. The delay in this chapter is caused by love and aimed at glory, and he says so out loud before anyone else understands it.
  • About you The sisters' prayer is worth copying: state the situation, state the relationship, and leave the response to him. Most of our praying is an attempt to specify the method.
  • Do Write down one prayer that God has not answered in the way or the time you asked. Beside it write verse 4 and verse 15, and name one way believing could grow if the answer keeps waiting.
