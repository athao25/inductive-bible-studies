John 8:48-59: Before Abraham Was, I Am

~25 min · Passage: John 8:48-59 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 8:48-59 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: the insults they use, the promise attached to keeping his word, how the Abraham argument develops, what Abraham is said to have seen, the wording of verse 58, and their immediate reaction.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Two insults in one breath (v. 48). "Are we not right in saying that you are a Samaritan and have a demon?" Racial slur plus accusation of possession.
  • He answers only the second (v. 49). "I do not have a demon, but I honor my Father, and you dishonor me." The slur is left unanswered.
  • He is not defending his own reputation (v. 50). "Yet I do not seek my own glory; there is One who seeks it, and he is the judge."
  • An enormous promise (v. 51). "If anyone keeps my word, he will never see death." Keeping, again, rather than merely hearing.
  • They take it literally (vv. 52-53). "Abraham died, as did the prophets... Who do you make yourself out to be?" The misunderstanding pattern one last time in this chapter.
  • Glory from the Father (v. 54). "It is my Father who glorifies me, of whom you say, 'He is our God.'"
  • A blunt charge of ignorance (v. 55). "But you have not known him. I know him. If I were to say that I do not know him, I would be a liar like you."
  • Abraham saw his day (v. 56). "Your father Abraham rejoiced that he would see my day. He saw it and was glad."
  • They compute his age (v. 57). "You are not yet fifty years old, and have you seen Abraham?"
  • The claim, in seven words (v. 58). "Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I am." Note the tense shift: Abraham came into being; I am.
  • The reaction is instant and legal (v. 59). "So they picked up stones to throw at him." Stoning was the penalty for blasphemy (Lev 24:16). Nobody thought he had merely claimed old age.
  • He leaves untouched (v. 59). "But Jesus hid himself and went out of the temple." The hour again.

Interpretation

Why is verse 58 the loudest claim in the Gospel so far?

Jesus said to them, "Truly, truly, I say to you, before Abraham was, I am." John 8:58, ESV

Three lines of evidence converge, and they are worth having at your fingertips. First, the grammar. If Jesus meant only that he predated Abraham, the natural Greek would be "before Abraham was, I was". Instead he contrasts a verb of becoming, used of Abraham, with a verb of being, used absolutely of himself: Abraham came into existence, I simply am. Second, the background. In Exodus 3:14 God names himself "I AM WHO I AM" to Moses, and Isaiah repeatedly has God say "I am he" as the badge of exclusive deity (Isa 41:4; 43:10-13; 46:4; 48:12). Jesus has already used that absolute "I am" at 8:24 and 8:28, so verse 58 is the climax of a pattern rather than an isolated saying. Third, and most decisively for a plain reader, the reaction. They reach for stones, and the law prescribed stoning for blasphemy. Hostile witnesses interpreted the sentence for us, and Jesus does not stop them to explain a misunderstanding, which is what an honest man would do if they had misheard him. Add verse 56, where he says Abraham rejoiced to see his day, and you have a claim to preexistence, to deity, and to being the substance of what Abraham was promised. This verse is where the course's teachers converge with real energy: MacArthur, White, and Durbin all treat John 8:58 as one of the strongest deity texts in Scripture, and White in particular has spent decades on how it is handled in translation debates. If you are ever pressed on whether Jesus claimed to be God, this verse plus 5:18, 10:30-33, and 20:28 is a complete answer.

What did Abraham see?

Genesis never records Abraham being shown the incarnation, so what does verse 56 mean? Most likely it points to the promise itself: "in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed" (Gen 12:3), and supremely to Genesis 22, where Abraham is stopped from sacrificing his son and says "God will provide for himself the lamb" (Gen 22:8), then names the place "The LORD will provide". Abraham saw the day of Christ in the promise, the way you can see a person in a portrait. Hebrews 11:13 says the patriarchs "greeted" the promises from far off. It is a useful correction for anyone who reads the Old Testament as a separate religion: the gospel was preached in advance to Abraham (Gal 3:8), and the man rejoiced. That also frames the tragedy of this scene. The people insisting hardest on their descent from Abraham are the ones failing to share his single most characteristic act, which was believing God.

Application

  • About God He is the eternally existing one, and he stood in a temple courtyard letting men gather stones rather than soften the claim.
  • About you Verse 51 offers you something no philosophy can: "if anyone keeps my word, he will never see death." Physical death remains, and yet the believer will not see it in the sense that matters, which is what 11:25-26 will spell out.
  • Do Learn John 8:58 well enough to quote it, and read Exodus 3:13-15 with it this week. Write one sentence on why the tense of the verb matters.
