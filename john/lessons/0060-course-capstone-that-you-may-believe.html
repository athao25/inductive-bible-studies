Capstone: That You May Believe

~40 min · No new passage · The whole Gospel, and what comes next

Sixty lessons, twenty-one chapters, seven signs, seven "I am" sayings, and one purpose statement. Today you gather it, test it, and decide what to do with it.

Say it without looking

  1. Tell the story of John's Gospel in one paragraph, from the prologue to the beach.
  2. List the seven signs and the seven "I am" sayings.
  3. Recite all ten memory verses in order.
The paragraph, if you want a model
In the beginning the Word was with God and was God, and the Word became flesh and dwelt among us. He came to his own and they did not receive him, though John bore witness and the signs began: water into wine, a healed son, a paralytic raised, five thousand fed, a sea walked, a blind man given sight, and a dead friend called out of a tomb. He taught that a man must be born again, offered living water to a Samaritan woman, called himself bread, light, door, shepherd, resurrection, way, and vine, and claimed the divine name outright before Abraham. The world's response hardened until the council resolved to kill him. Then the hour came. He washed his disciples' feet, gave a new commandment, promised another Helper, prayed for everyone who would ever believe, and was arrested, tried, and crucified, saying "It is finished". On the first day of the week the tomb was empty and the cloths were folded, and he called Mary by name, showed Thomas his hands, and restored Peter over breakfast. These things are written that you may believe.

The exam question

But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name. John 20:31, ESV

John did not write to give you a hobby, and this course did not exist to make you a person who knows facts about a first-century document. The book states its own aim, and it is a person and a verdict: that you believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that you have life in his name. Everything you have studied for sixty lessons stands behind that sentence. The prologue said who he is; the signs showed it; the "I am" sayings claimed it; the cross accomplished it; the resurrection proved it; the purpose statement asks you for a verdict on it. So take the last five minutes of this course and answer it honestly rather than devotionally. Do you believe this? If yes, say so out loud, and tell someone this week. If you are not sure, that is not a shameful place to be at the end of a study; it is exactly where Thomas was one week before the highest confession in the Gospel. Take it to your teacher, and keep sitting where Jesus shows up.

Check yourself

What to study next

  • 1 John. Written by the same man, working out the same themes for a church: light, love, life, and assurance. Five short chapters, and the natural sequel to this course.
  • Isaiah, or Matthew. Both are already built in this repository, with the same method and format. Isaiah will deepen everything John quotes; Matthew will give you the King and the kingdom from a different angle.
  • Genesis, Exodus, and Psalm 22. If John taught you anything, it is that the Old Testament is about Christ. Go read the texts he kept quoting.
  • Read John again, straight through, in one or two sittings. Nothing in this course substitutes for that. You will be startled how much you now see.
Your next step
Pick one: start 1 John this week, begin the Isaiah or Matthew course in this repository, or reread John cover to cover. Then set a review rhythm for the ten memory verses, one per week on a ten-week cycle. Decide today rather than "sometime", and write the date down.
Primary source
Keep the habit going: one MacArthur sermon a week from the John archive at gty.org would take you back through the whole book at preaching pace. James White on John 1:1, John 6, and John 8:58 remains the best material if you want the deity texts pressed carefully, and Jeff Durbin is worth your time on applying this to conversations with people who do not yet believe.
Church prompt
Tell someone at church that you have finished a study of John, and offer to walk one other person through it. Teaching a book is the fastest way to learn it twice, and the course is built to be handed on.

Questions welcome, always, and the end of a course is a good time to ask the ones you kept postponing. Bring them to your teacher this week rather than filing them away.
