Lesson 0059 · Unit 8: Chapters 18-21

John 21:15-25: Do You Love Me

~25 min · Passage: John 21:15-25 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 21:15-25 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: how many times Peter is questioned and what changes each time, the three commands, what is predicted about Peter's death, what Peter asks about John and the answer, and the two sentences that close the book.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • "Simon, son of John" (v. 15). Not Peter, the rock name, but the birth name, three times. Restoration starts at the beginning.
  • "Do you love me more than these?" (v. 15). An echo of the boast in 13:37 and Mark 14:29, where Peter claimed he would stand when others fell.
  • Three questions for three denials (vv. 15-17). The pattern is unmistakable, and it happens beside a charcoal fire.
  • Two Greek words for love. Jesus uses agapao in the first two questions and phileo in the third; Peter answers with phileo all three times. Whether John intends a distinction is debated, and the passage works either way (see below).
  • Peter is grieved at the third asking (v. 17). The third question is what stings, because three is the number of his denials.
  • He appeals to Christ's knowledge (v. 17). "Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you." No more boasting, no comparison with others.
  • Three commissions (vv. 15-17). Feed my lambs, tend my sheep, feed my sheep. Restoration is straight into responsibility.
  • They are Christ's sheep, not Peter's. "My lambs", "my sheep", spoken by the good shepherd of chapter 10 to an undershepherd.
  • A prediction of martyrdom (v. 18). "You will stretch out your hands, and another will dress you and carry you where you do not want to go."
  • John explains it (v. 19). "This he said to show by what kind of death he was to glorify God." Peter was crucified, by early and consistent tradition.
  • "Follow me" (v. 19). The same two words with which the whole thing started, now spoken to a man who knows what they cost.
  • Peter's comparison instinct returns (vv. 20-21). "Lord, what about this man?"
  • The answer is a rebuke and a release (v. 22). "If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? You follow me!"
  • A rumor corrected (v. 23). The saying spread that John would not die, and John himself sets the record straight. An author correcting a story about himself is a mark of honesty.
  • The witness identified (v. 24). "This is the disciple who is bearing witness about these things, and who has written these things, and we know that his testimony is true."
  • A closing hyperbole (v. 25). "The world itself could not contain the books that would be written." An affectionate exaggeration from a man who ran out of paper before he ran out of Jesus.

Interpretation

How does Christ restore a failure?

He said to him the third time, "Simon, son of John, do you love me?" Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, "Do you love me?" and he said to him, "Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you." John 21:17, ESV

Notice the shape of it, because it is a pattern worth knowing when you or someone you love has failed badly. He does it privately but not secretly; the other disciples are nearby, and the denial had been public. He does not minimize: three questions for three denials, at a charcoal fire, with the old boast quoted back in "more than these". He does not humiliate either; there is no lecture and no recounting of the courtyard. He asks one question, and it is about love rather than performance. And he ends by giving Peter work: three commissions, immediately. Restoration in this Gospel is not a period of probation but a return to responsibility, which is a striking thing to give a man with that record. Then note what has changed in Peter. In chapter 13 he compared himself favorably to others and trusted his own resolve. Here he refuses to compare and appeals only to what Christ knows about him. That is the difference between confidence in yourself and confidence in his knowledge of you, and it is the only kind that survives a courtyard.

Does the change of Greek verbs matter?

Two readings are respectable. On one, the shift is significant: Jesus twice asks for agape, the highest word, Peter can only claim philia, warm personal affection, and on the third question Jesus comes down to Peter's word, meeting him where he is. That reading has real pastoral beauty. On the other, John uses the two verbs interchangeably throughout his Gospel, since the Father is said both to agapao the Son (3:35) and to phileo the Son (5:20), and the beloved disciple is described with both verbs, so the variation is stylistic. Notice also that the passage varies "lambs" and "sheep" and "feed" and "tend" in the same way, which suggests John simply enjoys synonyms. The course does not insist on either reading, and here is the reason it does not matter much: the text itself tells you what grieved Peter, and it was not the vocabulary. Verse 17 says he was grieved "because he said to him the third time". Three, not the choice of verb, is what hurt, and three is what heals.

Why the exchange about John?

Jesus said to him, "If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? You follow me!" John 21:22, ESV

Peter has just been told he will die by crucifixion, and his immediate response is to ask what will happen to somebody else. That is so recognizable it is almost painful. The answer is one of the most useful sentences in the New Testament for the Christian life: what is that to you? Follow me. Comparison is a thief in both directions, breeding resentment when someone's road looks easier and pride when it looks harder. Christ assigns different paths, and the only assignment you are given is your own. Note also what John does with the rumor in verse 23. A saying had circulated that he would not die, and rather than enjoy the mystique, he prints the correction and quotes what Jesus actually said. Combined with the testimony claim in verse 24 and the earlier one in 19:35, it is the posture of a man who cares more about accuracy than about legend, which is precisely what you want in a witness.

Application

  • About God He restores by asking about love, and he gives work to people who have failed him. The sheep entrusted to Peter are still called "my sheep", which is the safety of every congregation.
  • About you If you have failed badly, this passage is your map: no minimizing, no groveling, one question about love, and then a job to do.
  • Do Answer verse 17 out loud, in your own words, as your own sentence. Then name one specific way you will "feed his sheep" in the coming month, and do it.
