Lesson 0046 · Unit 7: Chapters 13-17

John 14:15-31: Another Helper

~25 min · Passage: John 14:15-31 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 14:15-31 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: how love and obedience are related, the name given to the Spirit, what he will do for their memory, the kind of peace offered, and what is said about the ruler of this world.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Love shows itself in obedience (v. 15). "If you love me, you will keep my commandments." A statement of fact, not a bargain.
  • "Another Helper" (v. 16). Another of the same kind, which implies Jesus was the first. The Greek word Paraclete means one called alongside: advocate, counselor, comforter, helper. No single English word covers it.
  • Forever (v. 16). Unlike the Old Testament pattern of the Spirit coming upon particular people for particular tasks, this gift is permanent.
  • The Spirit of truth (v. 17). His work is tied to truth throughout these chapters.
  • The world cannot receive him (v. 17). "Because it neither sees him nor knows him."
  • A shift in preposition (v. 17). "He dwells with you and will be in you." Presence becomes indwelling.
  • Not orphans (v. 18). "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you." The word choice is tender and deliberate.
  • Life derived from his life (v. 19). "Because I live, you also will live."
  • Obedience and manifestation (v. 21). "Whoever has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me... and I will love him and manifest myself to him."
  • Judas (not Iscariot) asks the missionary question (v. 22). "How is it that you will manifest yourself to us, and not to the world?"
  • The Father and Son make their home (v. 23). "We will come to him and make our home with him." Indwelling is Trinitarian, not the Spirit alone.
  • The Spirit's teaching ministry (v. 26). "He will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you."
  • A different kind of peace (v. 27). "Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you. Not as the world gives do I give to you."
  • A hard sentence about their love (v. 28). "If you loved me, you would have rejoiced, because I am going to the Father, for the Father is greater than I."
  • Told in advance again (v. 29). "That when it does take place you may believe."
  • The ruler of this world has nothing in him (v. 30). No foothold, no claim, no leverage.
  • "Rise, let us go from here" (v. 31). They leave the room; chapters 15 to 17 may be spoken on the way.

Interpretation

Who is the Helper, and what does he do?

And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper, to be with you forever, even the Spirit of truth. John 14:16-17, ESV

Notice first that the Spirit is a "he", not an it. Jesus uses personal pronouns and describes personal actions: teaching, reminding, testifying, convicting, guiding. The Spirit is a divine person, not an energy, which matters practically because you cannot grieve a force or lie to a power (Eph 4:30; Acts 5:3-4). Notice second the word "another", meaning another of the same kind as Jesus himself. Whatever Christ was to them in person, the Spirit will be to them permanently. That is why his departure is gain rather than loss, a point he will make explicitly at 16:7. Then take verse 26 seriously in its first application: the promise to bring "to your remembrance all that I have said to you" was given to the apostles, and it stands behind the reliability of the Gospels you are reading. That is not a promise that you will recall Scripture perfectly without study. But there is a second application for every believer, since the Spirit's ongoing work is to illuminate and apply what Christ has said, which is exactly why prayer belongs at the start of Bible reading. And note the constraint that keeps this from becoming a license for private revelation: the Spirit reminds them of what Jesus said. Any voice claiming the Spirit while adding to or contradicting the word of Christ has identified itself as something else.

Is "the Father is greater than I" a denial of deity?

No, and the surrounding context makes it clear. In the same discourse Jesus says "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9), "I am in the Father and the Father is in me" (14:11), and earlier "I and the Father are one" (10:30). A writer who intended to deny his deity would not stack those sentences around this one. Two explanations, which are compatible. First, greater in position during the incarnation: the Son has taken the form of a servant (Phil 2:6-8), and he is speaking as the sent one on the way to a cross, so the Father in unclouded glory is in the greater position at that moment. Second, greater in the order of relations: the Son is eternally from the Father and is sent by him, which is a difference of relation, not of nature. The word he chose matters too. He said greater, not better; the Greek for "better" would have implied superiority of nature, and he does not use it. Now feel the pastoral edge of the verse. He rebukes them gently for grieving his departure: if they loved him rather than their own comfort in having him, they would be glad he was going home to glory. That is a searching test for our own prayers. How much of what we call love for Christ is love for what his presence does for us?

Application

  • About God Father, Son, and Spirit all indwell the believer (vv. 17, 23). The Christian is not left with a memory of Jesus but with the presence of God.
  • About you Verse 27 offers peace that does not depend on circumstances, given by a man hours from arrest. That is either absurd or the most valuable thing on offer.
  • Do Begin your Bible reading this week with one sentence of prayer asking the Spirit to teach and remind, per verse 26. Do it every day for seven days and notice whether the reading changes.
