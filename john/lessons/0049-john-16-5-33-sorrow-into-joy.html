Lesson 0049 · Unit 7: Chapters 13-17

John 16:5-33: Sorrow into Joy

~30 min · Passage: John 16:5-33 (ESV, read first!) · Memory verse 9 activates

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 16:5-33 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: why his going is to their advantage, the three things the Spirit convicts the world of, what the Spirit does with truth and with Christ's glory, the "little while" riddle and the picture he uses, what changes about their praying, and the final sentence.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Sorrow has filled their hearts (v. 6). They are too absorbed in grief to ask where he is going.
  • "To your advantage that I go away" (v. 7). A startling claim: they are better off with the Spirit than with Jesus physically present in one place.
  • Three convictions (vv. 8-11). The Spirit convicts the world "concerning sin and righteousness and judgment", each with a stated reason: sin, because they do not believe in him; righteousness, because he goes to the Father; judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
  • They cannot bear it now (v. 12). "I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now." Revelation is paced.
  • Guidance into all truth (v. 13). "He will guide you into all the truth... whatever he hears he will speak, and he will declare to you the things that are to come."
  • The Spirit's aim is Christ's glory (v. 14). "He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and declare it to you." A test for anything claiming to be Spirit-led.
  • The riddle (vv. 16-19). "A little while, and you will see me no longer; and again a little while, and you will see me." They repeat it among themselves five times, baffled.
  • Childbirth (v. 21). "When a woman is giving birth, she has sorrow... but when she has delivered the baby, she no longer remembers the anguish, for joy that a human being has been born into the world."
  • Joy no one can take (v. 22). "I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you."
  • A new arrangement for prayer (vv. 23-24). "In that day you will ask nothing of me... whatever you ask of the Father in my name, he will give it to you. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full."
  • The Father himself loves you (v. 27). "For the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me and have believed that I came from God." Christ does not have to talk the Father into loving them.
  • From figures to plainness (vv. 25, 29). They finally say, "Ah, now you are speaking plainly."
  • Their confidence is premature (vv. 30-32). "Now we know..." and he answers, "Behold, the hour is coming... when you will be scattered, each to his own home, and will leave me alone."
  • Never alone (v. 32). "Yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me." Hold this beside Matthew 27:46 and the cry of dereliction.
  • The summary sentence of the discourse (v. 33). "I have said these things to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world."

Interpretation

How is his departure an advantage?

Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you. John 16:7, ESV

Face the oddity of it. Would you not rather have Jesus here, visible, sitting across the table? He says no, and the reason is not sentimental. In the flesh he was in one place at a time; through the Spirit he indwells every believer everywhere at once. Before the cross the disciples had his presence; after Pentecost they had his presence, his finished work, and his power in them. It is worth noticing what makes the exchange possible: he must go to accomplish the cross and the resurrection before the Spirit is given (compare 7:39). So the advantage is not merely a change of mode but the arrival of everything the cross secures. Then verses 8 to 11 describe the Spirit's work in the world, and the verbs matter. He convicts, which means he presses a case until people know they are guilty. Not the church, not our persuasiveness. Our job is witness (15:27); the conviction belongs to him, which should relieve every Christian who has ever felt responsible for producing conversion in another person.

What is the joy that no one can take?

So also you have sorrow now, but I will see you again, and your hearts will rejoice, and no one will take your joy from you. John 16:22, ESV

The childbirth image is chosen with care. It does not say the pain is small or unreal; labor is agony. It says the pain is productive and bounded, and that what comes out of it changes how the whole episode is remembered. Applied first to the disciples, the sorrow is the crucifixion and the joy is the resurrection appearance, three days later. Applied to the church, the pattern holds for the whole age: tribulation now, joy at his return, with Romans 8:18 saying the same thing in different words. The specific promise is that this joy is unremovable, because its ground is outside the world's reach. Circumstantial joy can be taken by anyone with the power to change your circumstances. Joy grounded in a risen Christ who will see you again cannot be confiscated. Then verse 33 closes the discourse with the balance the New Testament never abandons: peace in him, tribulation in the world, and the reason to take heart, which is not that things will get easier but that he has already overcome the world. The verb is past tense, spoken before the cross. He is that certain.

Memory verse 9

I have said these things to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world. John 16:33, ESV

Say it aloud three times today, once tomorrow, and again at week's end. Learn all three clauses; most people quote the last one without the middle one, which is where the honesty is. Schedule at memory verses.

Application

  • About God The Father himself loves you (v. 27). The Son does not persuade a reluctant Father; the love is original to both.
  • About you If you feel responsible for convincing someone, verse 8 reassigns the job. Witness faithfully and leave the convicting to the Spirit.
  • Do Name your current tribulation in one sentence and write verse 33 underneath it in three parts: peace in him, trouble in the world, and the reason to take heart. Read it every morning this week.

Check yourself

Before next lesson
Read John 17 in your ESV, twice, slowly, and jot five observations. It is the longest recorded prayer of Jesus. Watch the three sections: he prays for himself (1-5), for the disciples (6-19), and for future believers (20-26). Note what he asks for each, and notice how often the word "given" appears.
Primary source
In your MacArthur Study Bible (Olive Tree), read the notes on 16:7-11 (the Spirit's conviction), 16:13-14 (guide into truth, glorify me), and 16:33 (overcome the world). Five minutes. Ask one question: how does the note explain "righteousness" in verse 10?
Church prompt
Ask someone at church whether they have ever felt responsible for another person's conversion. Read John 16:8 together and talk about what changes when the convicting is not your job.

Questions welcome, always: "what does convicting 'concerning righteousness' mean?", "does verse 23 promise anything I ask?", "how was he not alone at the cross?" Ask your teacher.
