Unit 7 Recap: The Upper Room

~30 min · No new passage · Consolidating John 13-17

Five chapters in one night, spoken to eleven men who would all abandon him within hours. This is the densest teaching in the Gospel, and it is where most Christians go when they need something to hold onto. Today you consolidate it.

Say it without looking

  1. Summarize each chapter from 13 to 17 in one sentence.
  2. Name the Helper's four ministries from chapters 14 to 16.
  3. Recite memory verses 7, 8, and 9 (14:6; 15:5; 16:33), then all nine in order.
Check your answers
(1) Roughly: 13, he washes their feet and gives the new commandment while the betrayer leaves; 14, do not be troubled, he is the way to the Father and will send another Helper; 15, abide in the vine, expect the world's hatred; 16, his going is gain, sorrow will turn to joy, he has overcome the world; 17, he prays for himself, for the eleven, and for everyone who would believe through their word. (2) He indwells forever (14:16-17), teaches and reminds (14:26), testifies about Christ and guides into all truth (15:26; 16:13), and convicts the world (16:8).

Recall: the whole unit

The unit in one frame

The Book of Glory opens with a towel. Knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, Jesus washes the feet of men who are about to fail him, including the one already arranging his arrest. He gives a new commandment whose standard is his own love and whose visibility is the church's badge. Then the discourse: do not let your hearts be troubled, because there is room in the Father's house, because he is the way to it, and because the Father and the Son will make their home in the believer through another Helper who stays forever. Chapter 15 supplies the image for the whole Christian life, a branch that bears fruit only by staying attached, and then warns that the world which hated the vine will hate the branches. Chapter 16 answers the grief directly: his departure is gain, the Spirit will convict and guide, and the sorrow will turn out to be labor pains. And chapter 17 is the prayer, in which everything the discourse promised is asked for out loud, ending with the request that his people be with him to see his glory.
