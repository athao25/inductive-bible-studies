Lesson 0018 · Unit 3: Chapters 5-6

John 5:30-47: The Four Witnesses

~25 min · Passage: John 5:30-47 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 5:30-47 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: how many witnesses Jesus names, what he calls the Baptist, what he says about searching the Scriptures, why he says they cannot believe, and what he says Moses wrote.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The legal frame (vv. 31-32). "If I alone bear witness about myself, my testimony is not true." Deuteronomy 19:15 required two or three witnesses. Jesus argues by their own rules and produces four.
  • Witness one: John the Baptist (vv. 33-35). "He was a burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to rejoice for a while in his light." A lamp, not the light (1:8), and their interest was seasonal.
  • Jesus does not depend on it (v. 34). "Not that the testimony that I receive is from man, but I say these things so that you may be saved." The motive of the whole argument is stated: rescue.
  • Witness two: the works (v. 36). "The works that the Father has given me to accomplish... bear witness about me." Greater than the Baptist's, and the works are given, not self-generated.
  • Witness three: the Father (vv. 37-38). "And the Father who sent me has himself borne witness about me." Then the indictment: you have never heard his voice, seen his form, or kept his word abiding in you.
  • Witness four: the Scriptures (v. 39). "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me."
  • Diligence is not the problem (v. 39). They searched intensively. The failure is not a lack of Bible study but of arriving where the study points.
  • The refusal is volitional (v. 40). "Yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life." Not "cannot understand"; will not come.
  • He is not fishing for approval (v. 41). "I do not receive glory from people."
  • The diagnosis (v. 42). "I know that you do not have the love of God within you."
  • The irony of credentials (v. 43). "If another comes in his own name, you will receive him." Those who reject the one sent by God will welcome the self-appointed.
  • The mechanism of unbelief (v. 44). "How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God?" Their reputation economy makes faith impossible.
  • Moses becomes the accuser (vv. 45-46). "There is one who accuses you: Moses, on whom you have set your hope. For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me."
  • The closing dilemma (v. 47). "But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my words?"

Interpretation

How can you study the Bible and miss Christ?

You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life. John 5:39-40, ESV

This is the most sobering pair of verses for people who do what you are doing right now. The men Jesus addresses were serious students. They counted letters, memorized paragraphs, and organized their days around the text. And they treated the Scriptures as the destination rather than the road, as if eternal life were stored in the studying itself. Two failures are worth naming. The first is treating Bible knowledge as a form of merit, a spiritual balance that accumulates. The second is subtler: reading the Bible as a book about principles for living rather than a book that testifies about a person. Jesus' claim is that all of it points to him, which is what he says again on the Emmaus road (Luke 24:27, 44). That is the difference between reading Scripture and coming to Christ through Scripture. Test yourself with a simple question at the end of a study session: did I meet anyone today, or did I only gather material? Note too that Jesus does not say their study was wrong; he says it was incomplete in the one way that matters. Keep searching. Just arrive.

Why can they not believe?

How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God? John 5:44, ESV

Verse 44 explains verse 40. Their unbelief is not evidential, because the evidence is stacked four deep. It is a matter of what they live on. If your sense of worth is funded by the opinions of people in your circle, then following a man those same people are moving to kill becomes literally unthinkable; the cost is not a bill you can pay. This is why Jesus links faith with seeking "the glory that comes from the only God". Faith requires a transfer of the account that your identity draws from. And notice the twist at the end of the chapter: Moses, their supreme authority, is turned from defense counsel into prosecutor. They believed they were defending Moses against a Sabbath-breaker. Jesus says Moses wrote about him, so the case against Jesus is a case against Moses. Where did Moses write of him? The seed of the woman (Gen 3:15), the lamb God provides (Gen 22), the Passover (Ex 12), the bronze serpent (Num 21), the prophet like Moses (Deut 18:15-18), and the whole sacrificial system that pictured a substitute. Read the Pentateuch with those markers and the accusation in verse 46 will make sense to you.

Application

  • About God He does not leave his Son unattested. Four witnesses, and the Father himself among them. Unbelief is never for lack of testimony.
  • About you The reputation economy of verse 44 runs in every workplace and every church. Ask whose approval you are actually spending your life to keep, and whether it would survive a conflict with Christ.
  • Do Read Genesis 22 and Deuteronomy 18:15-18 this week and write one line under each: how does this testify about Jesus? That is verse 46 practiced rather than admired.
