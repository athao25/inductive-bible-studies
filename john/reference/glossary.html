Reference

Glossary

Every technical term used in the lessons, defined once, used consistently. Grows as the course grows.

Study method

TermDefinition
Inductive studyDrawing conclusions out of the text by observation, rather than bringing conclusions to it. Three ordered steps: observation, interpretation, application.
ExpositionExplaining a passage's God-intended meaning in its context and pressing it on the hearer. "Expositional" study works through a book verse by verse rather than hopping between topics.
Grammatical-historical methodInterpreting a text by its grammar (what the words and sentences say) and its history (what the original author and audience meant and understood).
ContextThe material surrounding a passage: the paragraph, the section, the book, and the whole Bible. The first question of interpretation.
Cross-referenceAnother passage of Scripture that quotes, echoes, or explains the one you are studying. Scripture interprets Scripture.
AllusionAn unmarked reference to an earlier text that the author expects you to recognize. John's favorite tool: he rarely announces his Old Testament sources the way Matthew does; he assumes them (Genesis 1 under John 1:1, the Passover lamb under 1:29).

The Gospels

TermDefinition
Gospel"Good news": the announcement of salvation through Jesus Christ. Also the literary form of the four books that narrate his life, death, and resurrection.
Synoptic GospelsMatthew, Mark, and Luke, which "see together": they share much material and order. John stands apart; about ninety percent of his content has no Synoptic parallel, and he writes to supplement, not repeat.
SignJohn's word for Jesus' miracles: not raw power displays but pointers that reveal his glory and summon belief (2:11, 20:30-31). Seven public signs structure chapters 1-12; the resurrection is the eighth and greatest.
"I am" sayingsSeven predicate claims (bread, light, door, shepherd, resurrection, way-truth-life, vine) plus the absolute "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58), which takes up God's self-naming in Exodus 3:14. Together they are Jesus' self-revelation in John.
Logos (the Word)Greek for "word, reason, message". John's title for the eternal Son (1:1, 14): God's full self-expression, who was with God, was God, made all things, and became flesh. To hear the Word is to hear God speaking.
Book of Signs / Book of GloryThe two halves of John: chapters 1-12 (public signs, mounting belief and unbelief) and chapters 13-20 (the upper room, cross, and resurrection, where the Son is glorified). The hinge is 12:23.
The hourJohn's name for the appointed time of Jesus' death and glorification. "Not yet come" at 2:4, 7:30, 8:20; "has come" at 12:23, 13:1, 17:1. The cross runs on the Father's schedule, not his enemies'.
"The Jews" (in John)In John's usage, usually shorthand for the Judean religious leadership hostile to Jesus (e.g., 1:19, 5:18), not the Jewish people as such: Jesus, the Twelve, and the author are all Jewish, and "salvation is from the Jews" (4:22). Read the phrase by its context.
Paraclete (Helper)Greek parakletos, "one called alongside": advocate, counselor, helper. Jesus' name for the Holy Spirit in the upper room (14:16, 26; 15:26; 16:7), "another Helper" of the same kind as himself.
Son of ManJesus' favorite self-title, from Daniel 7:13-14: the human figure who receives everlasting dominion. In John he is the ladder between heaven and earth (1:51) and the one "lifted up" to draw all people (3:14, 12:32-34).

Theology

TermDefinition
Reformed theologyThe doctrinal stream of the Protestant Reformation: Scripture alone as final authority, salvation by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone, and God's absolute sovereignty in creation and salvation.
Incarnation"En-flesh-ment": the eternal Son taking a full human nature without ceasing to be God (1:14). One person, two natures; the center of John's prologue and of the Christian faith.
Deity of ChristThe doctrine that Jesus is fully God: "the Word was God" (1:1), "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58), "my Lord and my God" (20:28). John's Gospel is its clearest biblical witness, and all three course teachers defend it.
TrinityOne God in three persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, each fully God, distinct in relation. John supplies much of the raw material: the Word with God and God (1:1-2), the Son sent by the Father, the Spirit sent by both (14:26, 15:26).
Regeneration (new birth)God's sovereign act of giving spiritual life to the spiritually dead: being "born again / born from above" (3:3), "born of God" (1:13), "born of water and the Spirit" (3:5, echoing Ezek 36:25-27). Not self-produced: "not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God".
MessiahHebrew "anointed one" (Greek: Christ). The promised Davidic king. Andrew's first confession: "We have found the Messiah" (1:41). John writes so you will believe Jesus is he (20:31).
Lamb of GodThe Baptist's title for Jesus (1:29, 36): the sacrifice God himself provides, gathering the Passover lamb (Ex 12), Abraham's ram (Gen 22:8), and the Servant led like a lamb to slaughter (Isa 53:7), "who takes away the sin of the world".
Eternal lifeNot merely unending existence but the life of the age to come, possessed now: "whoever believes in the Son has eternal life" (3:36), defined as knowing the Father and the Son (17:3).
Effectual calling / drawingThe Father's sovereign, successful drawing of sinners to the Son: "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him" (6:44), and "all that the Father gives me will come to me" (6:37). A point where MacArthur, White, and Durbin all agree, argued most famously by White from John 6.
Perseverance of the saintsThe doctrine that those truly given to the Son are kept by him and never finally lost: "I give them eternal life, and they will never perish" (10:28), "I should lose nothing of all that he has given me" (6:39). Tested at the fruitless branches of 15:2, 6.
Common graceGod's non-saving goodness shown to all people without distinction. Relevant to prologue language like "the true light, which gives light to everyone" (1:9): general revelation illumines all, yet saving belief is born of God (1:12-13).
Textual criticismThe discipline of establishing the original wording of Scripture from thousands of manuscripts. Meets this course head-on at John 7:53-8:11, a passage absent from the earliest manuscripts; the lesson there handles it honestly rather than skipping it.
TypologyGod-designed patterns where earlier persons, events, or institutions (types) foreshadow and are fulfilled by later, greater realities (antitypes), supremely Christ: the tabernacle (1:14), the ladder (1:51), the bronze serpent (3:14), the manna (6:32-35), the temple itself (2:19-21).
EschatologyThe doctrine of last things. John's accent falls on "realized" eschatology: judgment and life arrive now, in belief or unbelief (3:18, 5:24), while the bodily resurrection "on the last day" remains future (6:39-40, 11:24). The millennium debates of the Matthew course barely surface here.
