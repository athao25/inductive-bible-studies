Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • "In the beginning" (v. 1). A deliberate echo of Genesis 1:1. John opens his Gospel on the Bible's first page, and the Word is already there when it starts.
  • Three clauses, each weight-bearing (v. 1). The Word was in the beginning (not: came to be); the Word was with God (distinction: face to face with another); the Word was God (identity of nature). Distinction and deity in a single verse.
  • "He was in the beginning with God" (v. 2). John immediately repeats the "with", as if he knows how much will hang on it.
  • Creation total, and negated twice (v. 3). "All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made." Nothing that was made was made without him, which puts the Word himself on the unmade side of the line.
  • Life and light are his (vv. 4-5, 9). Not things he received; things that were "in him".
  • The witness is not the light (vv. 6-8). John the Baptist is "sent from God" to bear witness; the contrast marks the Word off from even the greatest of God's servants.
  • "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (v. 14). Literally "tabernacled": the glory that filled the tent now camps in a human nature. "We have seen his glory", the very thing Isaiah said God gives to no other.
  • "Grace and truth" (vv. 14, 17). The pair echoes "steadfast love and faithfulness", God's self-description at Sinai (Ex 34:6).
  • "The only God, who is at the Father's side" (v. 18). No one has ever seen God; the only God, at the Father's side, "has made him known", literally exegeted him. The prologue ends where it began: one called God, distinct from one called Father, and the two face to face.

Interpretation

Why "the Word was God" cannot be softened

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. John 1:1, ESV

Every denial of the Son's deity has to do something with this verse, and the standard move (printed in the Jehovah's Witnesses' translation as "the Word was a god") fails on the Greek that is actually there. In the third clause, "God" stands before the verb without the article, which in Greek is the normal way to make it the predicate, stating what the Word is rather than who; the construction stresses nature, not identity. That grammar does two jobs at once, and both matter to this course. It refuses "a god": John, the monotheist who will quote Isaiah's courtroom scenes later in this Gospel (John 12:39-41), does not believe in junior deities, and "a god" would shatter the Shema this course just spent a unit fixing. And it refuses "the Word was the Father": if John had written the article, he would have collapsed the Word into the very God he was just said to be with. The sentence is built with a jeweler's precision to say exactly what Nicaea would later defend: all that God is, the Word is; and the Word and God the Father are not the same person. Distinction ("with God") plus full deity ("was God"), the doctrine's second and third pillars, in the Bible's most famous opening line.

Verse 3 closes the only escape hatch

Suppose someone grants the Word is exalted but insists he is the first and highest creature. Verse 3 is waiting: "all things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made." John says it positively, then negatively, sealing every gap: whatever belongs to the category "made" was made through the Word, so the Word does not belong to that category. Now bring forward the thread from Unit 1: YHWH "alone stretched out the heavens" and made all things "by myself" (Isa 44:24). If God created alone, and all things were created through the Word, the Word is not God's helper but stands within the "alone". This is why White's summary of the prologue is blunt: "The Word is eternal. The Word has always existed. The Word is not a creation." And verse 14 then delivers the thunderclap the whole Gospel unpacks: that uncreated Word became flesh and pitched his tent among us. The prologue does not argue that a man became God, which is impossible; it announces that God the Word became man, which is the gospel.

Application

  • About God "The Word was with God": before anything existed, there was fellowship, a face-to-face fullness. Verse 18's "at the Father's side" is where the Son has always been.
  • About you "No one has ever seen God"; the only God at the Father's side has exegeted him (v. 18). Everything you can know of God, you know through the Son. There is no back door around him.
  • Do Memory verse 3 activates today: John 1:1. Learn it with the clauses distinct: was, was with, was. Review Deuteronomy 6:4 and Isaiah 44:6 once each, aloud.
