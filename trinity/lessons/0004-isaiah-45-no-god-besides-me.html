Isaiah 44-45: No God Besides Me

~25 min · Passage: Isaiah 44:6-8; 45:5-7, 21-23 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?

Write down at least five observations: the titles God takes for himself, the count of "no other / none besides me" statements, the challenge issued to the idols, what God claims to do in 45:7, and the oath of 45:23.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on these passages include:

  • The stacked titles (44:6). "The LORD, the King of Israel and his Redeemer, the LORD of hosts." Name, kingship, redemption, and command of heaven's armies, all in one introduction.
  • "I am the first and I am the last" (44:6). Nothing before him, nothing after him, no room beside him. Hold this clause carefully; it returns at the end of the lesson.
  • "Besides me there is no god" (44:6). Then again in 44:8, 45:5, 45:6, 45:21, 45:22. Count them; the repetition is the argument. This is the most concentrated monotheism in the Bible.
  • The courtroom challenge (44:7-8). "Who is like me? Let him proclaim it... let them declare what is to come." The test of deity is declaring the future and bringing it to pass. The idols are summoned to testify and have nothing to say.
  • "You are my witnesses" (44:8). Israel's role in the trial: witnesses to the only God. "Is there a God besides me? There is no Rock; I know not any."
  • Cyrus, unnamed here but named in context (45:1-5). These speeches are wrapped around a pagan king whom God appoints by name, generations early, precisely to prove the point of the challenge.
  • Light, darkness, well-being, calamity (45:7). No dualism: there is no second, dark power running half the world. One God stands behind the whole of history.
  • A righteous God and a Savior (45:21). "There is no other god besides me... and there is none besides me." Deity and salvation are fused: if only God is God, then only God can save.
  • The universal invitation and oath (45:22-23). "Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth... To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear." The strictest monotheism in Scripture issues, in the same breath, the widest invitation.

Interpretation

What does exclusive deity mean here?

Thus says the LORD, the King of Israel and his Redeemer, the LORD of hosts: "I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god." Isaiah 44:6, ESV

Isaiah's setting is a lawsuit against idolatry: Israel, facing Babylon's impressive gods, is tempted to think YHWH one power among several. God's answer is not "I am the strongest" but "I am the only". The marks of exclusive deity in these chapters are worth listing, because Unit 2 will ask whether the New Testament gives them to anyone else: the name YHWH; "the first and the last"; creator of all things (44:24, "who made all things, who alone stretched out the heavens"); sovereign declarer of history; the only Savior (43:11, "besides me there is no savior"); the one to whom every knee will bow. These are not honors God distributes; they are the definition of being God, and he explicitly refuses to share them: "I am the LORD; that is my name; my glory I give to no other" (42:8).

Why this lesson sits in a Trinity course

Because these are the verses the doctrine must satisfy, and the verses the New Testament then does something astonishing with. Keep a bookmark here; the course will return three times. "To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear" (45:23) is quoted by Paul in Philippians 2:10-11, with the knees bowing "at the name of Jesus" and the tongues confessing that "Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father", a passage you will study in lesson 9. "I am the first and I am the last" (44:6) is spoken in Revelation 1:17 and 22:13 by the risen Jesus. And "besides me there is no savior" (43:11) stands behind the apostles' insistence that salvation is in no one else (Acts 4:12). The New Testament writers knew exactly what these chapters claim, and they applied the claims to Jesus anyway, while still confessing one God. Either that is blasphemy, or it is the strongest possible statement of who Jesus is. James White calls this the "Jehovah of Hosts" argument: the Old Testament's most jealous, least shareable language is precisely the language the New Testament uses for Christ. Note it now; weigh it when the texts arrive in Unit 2. Nothing in this lesson yet proves the Trinity. It sets the stakes, and the stakes could not be higher.

Application

  • About God He declares the end from the beginning and stands alone behind light and dark days alike (45:7). There is no rogue power writing any chapter of your life.
  • About you "Turn to me and be saved, all the ends of the earth" (45:22). Exclusive deity is not bad news; it is why the invitation can be universal and the salvation certain.
  • Do Memory verse 2 activates today: Isaiah 44:6. Learn it word for word, especially "I am the first and I am the last"; you will need the exact clause when you reach Revelation. Review Deuteronomy 6:4 aloud once.
