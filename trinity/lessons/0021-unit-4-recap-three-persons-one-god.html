Unit 4 Recap: Three Persons, One God

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating the whole doctrine so far

This recap is different: it consolidates not just a unit but the entire case, because with Unit 4 the doctrine is complete. Today you assemble the three pillars, state the doctrine in your own words, and answer the question every thoughtful person eventually asks of it. Lesson 22 will then show you what happened historically when parts of this were denied.

Recall: the unit

The doctrine, assembled

Watch how the three pillars force the conclusion, because this is the argument you should now be able to make unaided. Scripture teaches, unbendably, that there is one God (Unit 1). Scripture also teaches that the Father is God, the Son is God, and the Spirit is God, each fully, with the unshareable marks (Units 2-3), and that the three are really distinct, speaking to and sending one another, present together in one scene, named together under one name (Unit 4). Three lines of teaching, all clearly biblical; the doctrine of the Trinity is nothing but the refusal to surrender any of them. Every alternative buys simplicity by selling a text: tritheism keeps the three and sells the Shema; Arianism keeps the oneness and sells John 1, Colossians 1, Hebrews 1; modalism keeps both oneness and deity and sells the Jordan, the sendings, and every prayer the Son ever addressed to the Father. The church did not choose mystery over clarity; it chose all of Scripture over a tidy fraction of it.

"But how can it be?": the grammar that keeps you honest

The question deserves a straight answer, and the course now has the tools. The doctrine never asserts that God is one and three in the same sense: one in Being (what God is), three in person (who God is). No contradiction, but no full comprehension either, because there is nothing else like the one Being of God for the analogy to lean on. Hence every popular illustration fails in a heretical direction, and you should retire them knowingly: water, ice, and steam is modalism (one substance, successive modes); the egg's shell, white, and yolk is partialism (three parts making a whole); the sun, its light, and its heat makes two persons impersonal emanations of the first. The honest position is the one Scripture models: state what is revealed, refuse what is denied, and worship at the edge of what is not explained. As lesson 2 said and Unit 4 showed, you were never asked to solve God; you were invited to know him, and the three persons you now know by name have been, this whole course, teaching you (the Spirit), interceding for you (the Son), and delighting in you (the Father).
