Lesson 0008 · Unit 2: The Son Is God

John 5:16-30: Equal with God

~25 min · Passage: John 5:16-30 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 5:16-30 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: what Jesus answers when accused over the Sabbath, why verse 18 says they sought all the more to kill him, the works the Son shares with the Father, the claim of verse 23, and whose voice raises the dead.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • "My Father is working until now, and I am working" (v. 17). Challenged over a Sabbath healing, Jesus does not cite an exception clause; he cites his Father's ceaseless providence and places his own work beside it.
  • The hearers understood the grammar (v. 18). "This was why the Jews were seeking all the more to kill him... he was even calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God." Hostile witnesses are often the clearest commentators; they heard the claim exactly.
  • Jesus does not correct them (vv. 19ff). Where a misunderstanding occurs in John, it gets corrected (see 21:23). Here Jesus answers the charge by expanding it.
  • "Whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise" (v. 19). Not imitation at a distance; the Son does the same works, in the same way. No creature could survive this sentence.
  • Raising and judging, transferred (vv. 21-22). "As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will." And "the Father... has given all judgment to the Son." Resurrection and final judgment: the two works Scripture reserves for God alone.
  • Equal honor demanded (v. 23). "That all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him."
  • Hearing the Son's voice gives life now (vv. 24-25). "The hour is coming, and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live."
  • "Life in himself" (v. 26). "As the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself." Underived, self-existent life, the kind only God has, possessed by the Son in the Father's own way.
  • The Son does nothing "of his own accord" (vv. 19, 30). The passage that claims the most for the Son also shows him entirely oriented to the Father: he does what he sees, seeks the Father's will, not his own.

Interpretation

Equality claimed, not retracted

This was why the Jews were seeking all the more to kill him, because not only was he breaking the Sabbath, but he was even calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God. John 5:18, ESV

Watch the shape of the argument, because it is the same shape you will meet at John 10 and at the trial. Jesus says something; the hearers state its implication, "making himself equal with God"; and Jesus, given every opportunity to say "you misunderstand me", instead raises the stakes: the Son does whatever the Father does, gives life to whom he will, will judge every human being, and must be honored just as the Father is honored. Verse 23 alone settles the question this unit is asking. In the vocabulary Unit 1 fixed, honor "just as" the Father receives is worship, and Isaiah's God gives his glory to no other. Either verse 23 commands idolatry, or the Son stands on the Creator's side of the line. There is no third shelf, no place for an exalted creature receiving almost-divine honor; that is precisely the category Isaiah's courtroom scenes exist to demolish.

Equal, yet from the Father: both truths at full strength

Now the balancing truth, and it matters for the rest of the course. This same passage says the Son "can do nothing of his own accord" (v. 19), that judgment is "given" him (v. 22), that life in himself is "granted" (v. 26), that he seeks the will of him who sent him (v. 30). Arians old and new read these as proofs of a lesser being. Read them again: verse 19 does not say the Son does less than the Father, it says he does everything the Father does, likewise; the impossibility of independent action cuts both ways, for the Father and Son work inseparably. What these verses actually reveal is the eternal order of the persons: the Son is from the Father, sent by the Father, doing the Father's will, eternally the Son and never the Father. Equality of nature, distinction of person, order of relation, with neither truth trimmed to make room for the other. Hold this passage as the pattern: whenever you meet a text where the Son obeys, is sent, or receives (and Unit 2 has more coming, including "the Father is greater than I", John 14:28), the question is never whether he is equal to the Father in deity, which verse 23 has settled, but as what he speaks and acts: as the eternal Son in his relation to his Father, and, in the incarnation, as the servant who took our flesh. Lesson 22 will give this its formal grammar.

Application

  • About God The Father and the Son work inseparably: the hand that made you and the hand that judges and raises you are one work of one God. Nothing the Son promises can be vetoed elsewhere.
  • About you Verse 24 is the hinge of assurance in this Gospel: hearing and believing means having eternal life now, and not coming into judgment. The one who will judge you is the one who already gives you life.
  • Do Honor the Son "just as" the Father this week in the most concrete way: address prayer to Jesus at least once, deliberately, and notice whether it feels strange. If it does, verse 23 is the correction. Review John 1:1 aloud.
