Ephesians 1:3-14: The Shape of Salvation

~25 min · Passage: Ephesians 1:3-14 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Ephesians 1:3-14 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now (in Greek it is one unbroken sentence; read it aloud once to feel that). Write down at least five observations: what the Father did "before the foundation of the world", every occurrence of "in him / in Christ / in the beloved", what we have "through his blood", what the Spirit is called in verses 13-14, and the refrain that recurs at verses 6, 12, and 14.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • One long doxology (v. 3). "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing." The sentence is praise before it is theology; Paul is singing, not outlining.
  • The Father's work: before time (vv. 4-6). "He chose us in him before the foundation of the world", "he predestined us for adoption to himself as sons through Jesus Christ", "to the praise of his glorious grace". Election, predestination, adoption: the Father's initiative, in love, before anything existed.
  • The Son's work: at the cross (vv. 7-12). "In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses." The plan of the Father is accomplished in history by the Son, in whom all things will be united (v. 10), in whom we have obtained an inheritance.
  • The Spirit's work: in you now (vv. 13-14). Having heard and believed, "you were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it".
  • "In him", relentlessly (vv. 3-14). Ten times and more. Every blessing of the Father and every sealing of the Spirit reaches you in union with Christ. Nothing arrives outside him.
  • The refrain (vv. 6, 12, 14). "To the praise of his glorious grace... to the praise of his glory... to the praise of his glory." Three movements, one goal, marked off like verses of a hymn: Father (praise), Son (praise), Spirit (praise).
  • "Guarantee" (v. 14). Arrabon: down payment, engagement pledge, first installment of the very thing itself. The Spirit in you is not a promise of God's presence; he is the presence, in advance.
  • Purpose everywhere (vv. 5, 9, 11). "According to the purpose of his will... the mystery of his will... the counsel of his will." Salvation runs on intention, not improvisation.

Interpretation

Salvation has trinitarian architecture

In him you also, when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and believed in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it, to the praise of his glory. Ephesians 1:13-14, ESV

Stand back from the sentence and see its shape: the Father chooses, predestines, and adopts, before the foundation of the world; the Son redeems, forgives, and unites, through his blood, in the fullness of time; the Spirit seals and guarantees, in the believer, until the day of possession. Past, accomplished, applied; planned by the Father, purchased by the Son, sealed by the Spirit; and each movement closes with the same refrain of praise. This is not a passage about the Trinity that Paul paused to write; it is what salvation looks like when a trinitarian simply describes it. That is the deepest kind of evidence this course has met: the doctrine is load-bearing in the gospel itself. Pull out any person and salvation as the New Testament tells it collapses. No Father, and there is no eternal love or plan behind your believing; you are saved by late accident. No divine Son, and the redemption price is a creature's blood, which Hebrews spent a chapter denying could avail. No divine Spirit, and the seal on you is not God's own presence but a token, and the guarantee guarantees nothing. White's line from lesson 18 belongs here with full force: the Trinity is revealed "in the very action of the Triune God in redemption itself".

One work, three grips, and the security of it

Notice what this architecture does for assurance, because Paul wrote it as doxology, not diagram. Your salvation is held at three points that are one grip: a choice made before the foundation of the world that cannot be undone by anything inside time; a redemption accomplished at the cross, finished and priced in blood, not maintained by your performance; and a seal applied within you that is itself "the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it". The persons do not divide the work as contractors divide a site; each act is the one God's act, done in the manner proper to each person, which is why the passage can sweep it all into "him who works all things according to the counsel of his will" (v. 11). John 10's two hands have become three grips on one inheritance. And the response the passage models is the response the doctrine has been driving toward since lesson 2: not solving, but blessing. "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ": the correct posture before the Trinity is doxology with your feet on exegesis, which is precisely what this passage is, and what lesson 24 will send you out to practice.

Application

  • About God Before the foundation of the world, the Father set his love on you in the Son. Your story did not begin with your believing; it began in the eternal counsel of the three.
  • About you The Spirit in you is the down payment of your inheritance, God's presence in advance. Assurance is not a mood; it is a seal.
  • Do Reread verses 3-14 and mark F, S, or Sp beside each blessing. Then turn the three movements into three sentences of prayer, ending each with the refrain: "to the praise of his glory." Review Matthew 28:19.
