Unit 1 Recap: One God

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating Deuteronomy 6, Isaiah 44-45, Genesis 1

Three passages, one pillar. Before the course says a word about the deity of the Son or the Spirit, the foundation is poured, and today you retrieve it, connect it, and say it in your own words. Retrieval feels harder than rereading; that difficulty is the point.

Say it without looking

Before any quiz, take paper and try these three from memory. Then check yourself against the answers below.

  1. Recite Deuteronomy 6:4 and Isaiah 44:6 (memory verses 1 and 2).
  2. List the marks of exclusive deity that Isaiah's courtroom scenes assign to YHWH alone. There were at least five.
  3. In one sentence each: what does Genesis 1:26 honestly show, and what does it not prove?
Check your answers
(1) "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one." "Thus says the LORD, the King of Israel and his Redeemer, the LORD of hosts: 'I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god.'" (2) The name YHWH; "the first and the last"; sole Creator ("who alone stretched out the heavens"); sovereign declarer of the future; the only Savior; the one to whom every knee will bow; glory shared with no other. (3) Shows: within the one God there is speech, counsel, an "us", on the Bible's first page. Does not prove: three persons; the seed waits for John 1 to name the Word and the Spirit who were there.

Recall: the whole unit

The unit in one frame

Watch what has been established and how deliberately. The Shema (Deut 6:4-9) gave the confession: YHWH is one, and therefore claims all the heart. Isaiah's courtroom (44-45) gave the definition: to be God is to be first and last, sole Creator, declarer of history, only Savior, receiver of every knee, and these marks are explicitly unshareable, "my glory I give to no other". Genesis 1 gave the seed: the one Creator creates by his word and his Spirit, and deliberates as "us" without ever becoming two gods. Pillar 1 is now fixed: there is one God, and only one, and nothing later in this course may bend that. And the unit has quietly loaded the scales for everything that follows: if the New Testament hands Isaiah's unshareable marks to Jesus of Nazareth and to the Spirit, while its writers keep praying the Shema, only one conclusion will hold all the texts at once. That is precisely what Units 2 and 3 will show happening, passage by passage.
