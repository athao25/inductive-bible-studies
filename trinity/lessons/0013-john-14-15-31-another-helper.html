John 14:15-31: Another Helper

~25 min · Passage: John 14:15-31 (ESV, read first!)

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • "Another Helper" (v. 16). Greek has two words for "another": another of a different kind, and another of the same kind. John uses the second, allos: one like the Helper they already have, Jesus himself.
  • "Helper" is a personal office (v. 16). Parakletos: advocate, counselor, one called alongside. It is a courtroom word for a person who stands with you, applied to Jesus himself in 1 John 2:1. Winds and forces do not hold this office.
  • "To be with you forever" (v. 16). The first Helper stayed three years; the second has no departure date.
  • "The Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive" (v. 17). Known or unknown, received or refused: the language of personal relationship, "you know him, for he dwells with you and will be in you".
  • Personal pronouns pile up (vv. 17, 26). Him, he, whom. And the Spirit's actions are the actions of a someone: he dwells, he will teach you all things, he will bring to your remembrance.
  • Sent by the Father, in the Son's name (v. 26). "The Helper, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name." In 15:26 Jesus adds, "whom I will send to you from the Father... who proceeds from the Father". Both true; hold both.
  • "I will come to you" (v. 18). The Spirit's coming is so intimately the Son's that Jesus can say his arrival means "I will not leave you as orphans".
  • Three persons in two verses (vv. 23, 26). "My Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him"; then the Father sending the Spirit in the Son's name. The whole doctrine is in the room.
  • The result is peace (v. 27). The trinitarian promise lands as "let not your hearts be troubled, neither let them be afraid".

Interpretation

The Spirit is a person, not a power

"And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Helper, to be with you forever, even the Spirit of truth..." John 14:16-17, ESV

Unit 3 must establish two things about the Spirit, and this passage carries the first: he is a someone, not a something. The evidence is not one word but the whole grain of the discourse. The Spirit holds a personal office (Helper, the office Jesus held); he is "another of the same kind" as the Son, and the Son is not an impersonal force; he teaches, reminds, dwells, testifies (15:26), and can be known or not known; and John repeatedly uses the masculine pronoun of him even though the Greek noun for spirit is neuter, letting the Spirit's personhood override the grammar. Later revelation will add that the Spirit speaks in the first person ("Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul", Acts 13:2), forbids and redirects missionaries (Acts 16:6-7), decides ("it has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us", Acts 15:28), can be lied to, and can be grieved (Eph 4:30). Every one of those is nonsense of an electric current. Why does it matter? Because, as this unit will keep insisting, you cannot pray to a force, be comforted by an influence, or grieve a battery. A depersonalized Spirit produces a Christianity in which God is functionally distant and "power" is something to acquire rather than Someone to obey.

"Another of the same kind": the argument hiding in one word

Now the quiet weight of allos. The disciples' Helper for three years has been the divine Son, God in flesh at their table. Jesus promises "another Helper" of the same kind as himself, to do for them everything he has done, forever. If the Son is God (Unit 2's verdict) and the Spirit is another of the same kind, the conclusion is already leaning into Unit 3's second claim before we argue it: the Spirit stands where the Son stands, on the Creator's side of the line. Notice also the trinitarian choreography that this chapter takes as ordinary: the Son asks, the Father gives, the Spirit comes; the Father sends in the Son's name (14:26), the Son sends from the Father (15:26). Distinct persons, one seamless action, no negotiation and no rivalry, and the disciples' comfort depends on all three: the Son's advocacy above, the Father's love toward them, the Spirit's presence within them. Also mark 14:28, "the Father is greater than I", because honest study does not skip it: spoken by the incarnate Son on the way to the cross, it describes the position he occupies as sent servant (and the Father as sender), not the nature he possesses, which this same Gospel has already fixed (1:1, 5:23, and "I and the Father are one", 10:30). Lesson 22 gives this distinction its formal name.

Application

  • About God God's answer to troubled hearts is not a technique but persons: the Son who will not leave you an orphan, the Father who loves you, the Spirit who is in you forever.
  • About you If you are in Christ, the Helper is not an occasional visitor. "He dwells with you and will be in you", and his teaching ministry runs through the word he inspired.
  • Do Memory verse 6 activates today: John 14:26. Learn it, and this week catch yourself once saying or thinking "it" of the Spirit; replace it with "he", out loud. Review Hebrews 1:3.
