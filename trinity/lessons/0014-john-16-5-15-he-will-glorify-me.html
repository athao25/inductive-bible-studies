Lesson 0014 · Unit 3: The Spirit Is God

John 16:5-15: He Will Glorify Me

~25 min · Passage: John 16:5-15 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read John 16:5-15 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: why Jesus says his departure is to the disciples' advantage, the three things the Spirit will convict the world concerning, how each is defined, what the Spirit will and will not speak, and what verse 14 says he will do with what belongs to Jesus.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • "It is to your advantage that I go away" (v. 7). A staggering claim: the Spirit's presence with them will be better than the incarnate Son's continued presence beside them. The exchange is only an "advantage" if the one who comes is not a lesser substitute.
  • The Helper's coming depends on the Son's going (v. 7). "If I go, I will send him to you." The cross, ascension, and Pentecost are one connected mission.
  • He convicts the world (vv. 8-11). Concerning sin ("because they do not believe in me"), righteousness ("because I go to the Father"), and judgment ("because the ruler of this world is judged"). Searching the conscience of the whole world is not a creature-sized work.
  • "He will guide you into all the truth" (v. 13). Addressed to the apostles in the room: the promise behind the New Testament you are holding.
  • "He will not speak on his own authority" (v. 13). The same shape you met in John 5:19 and 30 of the Son. The pattern of the sent one, again, and by now you know what it does and does not mean.
  • "He will glorify me" (v. 14). The Spirit's self-effacing mission: to take what is the Son's and declare it. A floodlight, aimed at Christ.
  • The full circle of possession (vv. 14-15). "All that the Father has is mine; therefore I said that he will take what is mine and declare it to you." Father's, Son's, Spirit's declaration: everything flows through all three.
  • "He... he... he" (vv. 13-14). John again sets masculine pronouns against the neuter noun "Spirit". The grammar bends to the person.

Interpretation

Why the exchange is an advantage

"Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away, for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you." John 16:7, ESV

Stand in the disciples' sandals: how could anything be better than Jesus staying? The answer measures the Spirit. The incarnate Son, having taken a real human body, is in one place at a time, beside you at most; the Spirit dwells in every believer at once, forever (14:16-17). And his coming is not a downgrade of presence but the mode by which the Son and the Father themselves make their home in you (14:23), because the persons do not come severally, like separate visitors. Only if the Spirit is fully God, of the same kind as the Son, does "to your advantage" hold; sending a creature, however exalted, to replace God-with-us would be loss, not gain. The verse also quietly confirms the Spirit's distinct personhood one more time: one person goes, another comes, and one sends the other. You cannot send yourself to replace yourself.

The floodlight and the test of the Spirit

Verse 14 is the most practically important sentence in this unit: "He will glorify me, for he will take what is mine and declare it to you." The Spirit's mission is deliberately self-effacing, aimed away from himself and at the Son; J. I. Packer's image is a floodlight trained on a building. This gives you a permanent test with two edges. First, any movement, experience, or teacher claiming the Spirit while sidelining Christ, his word, his cross, fails the test on its face; the Spirit does not freelance ("he will not speak on his own authority") or compete with the Son for attention. Second, and less noticed: wherever Christ is being genuinely exalted, seen, treasured, and believed, there the Spirit is at work, since no one can say "Jesus is Lord" except in the Holy Spirit (1 Cor 12:3). The Spirit's shyness about his own glory explains why he is the "forgotten" person of the Trinity, and it also means that honoring him is not achieved by an anxious campaign to talk about him more, but by yielding to his mission: glorifying the Son he glorifies. And do not miss verse 15's ontology hiding under the mission: "all that the Father has is mine", says the Son, and the Spirit declares from that one common store. Three persons drawing on one undivided fullness: the doctrine, again, assumed in passing.

Application

  • About God The Spirit in you is not a diminished substitute for Christ beside you; he is God in you, and by him the Father and Son make their home with you. You live on the better side of the exchange.
  • About you Conviction of sin (v. 8) is not the Spirit against you but the Spirit for you: nobody comes to the Savior without first being shown the need.
  • Do Apply the floodlight test to your own week's inputs, honestly: do the teachers, feeds, and songs you take in magnify Christ, or themselves? Review John 14:26 and Deuteronomy 6:4 aloud.
