Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • "Hear, O Israel" (v. 4). The confession begins with an imperative to listen. Israel's faith starts with revelation received, not speculation achieved. The Hebrew word is shema, which gave the confession its name.
  • "The LORD our God, the LORD is one" (v. 4). LORD in capitals renders the divine name, YHWH, God's personal name from Exodus 3. The claim is not merely that Israel has one God among the world's many, but that the God who bears this name is one.
  • Love is commanded (v. 5). The first and greatest commandment, as Jesus himself will rank it (Matt 22:37-38), follows directly from who God is. Doctrine in verse 4, devotion in verse 5, and the order is not reversible.
  • "All... all... all" (v. 5). Heart, soul, might. Exclusive deity claims exclusive, total love. One God means undivided allegiance; two gods could split the heart.
  • The words go on the heart first (v. 6). Before they go on doorposts and gates, they are to be on your heart.
  • Teach them diligently to your children (v. 7). The confession is transmitted at home, in the ordinary postures of a day: sitting, walking, lying down, rising.
  • Bind, write, post (vv. 8-9). Hand, forehead, doorframe, gate: the one God is to saturate sight and habit. Whether Moses meant it literally or figuratively, the point is total permeation.

Interpretation

What does "the LORD is one" assert?

"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one." Deuteronomy 6:4, ESV

In its context, standing at the head of Moses' exposition of the first commandment, the Shema asserts at least this: YHWH is the only God there is, and he is not divisible into local Baals or rival powers. Deuteronomy says it explicitly two chapters earlier: "the LORD is God; there is no other besides him" (4:35, 39). This is the monotheism that made Israel strange in the ancient world, and it is pillar 1 of the doctrine you are studying. Fix this now: the doctrine of the Trinity does not soften this verse, negotiate with it, or quietly outgrow it. It is built on it. Any account of Father, Son, and Spirit that ends with three gods has not developed the Shema; it has denied it, and the New Testament writers, who prayed the Shema, would have been the first to say so.

Does "one" leave room for what comes later?

Two careful notes, made with restraint. First, the Hebrew word for "one" here, echad, is the ordinary word for one, and it can number things with internal complexity: the evening and the morning are "one day" (Gen 1:5); a man and his wife become "one flesh" (Gen 2:24). Be honest about what this does and does not prove: it does not teach the Trinity, and this course will not pretend it does. It simply means the Shema counts God as one without commenting on whether that oneness is simple or rich, and so it leaves the question of the persons exactly where it should be left, to later revelation. Second, notice what the New Testament does with this verse. When Paul confesses "there is no God but one", he immediately writes: "one God, the Father... and one Lord, Jesus Christ, through whom are all things" (1 Cor 8:4-6), placing Jesus inside the "one LORD" language of the Shema itself, using its own vocabulary. And when a scribe asks Jesus for the greatest commandment, he answers by reciting this passage in full (Mark 12:29-30). The Shema is not the Trinity's rival. It is the room the doctrine lives in.

Application

  • About God There is one God, bearing one name, admitting no rivals. Everything else this course will say must fit inside that sentence, and does.
  • About you "All your heart" is the corollary of "the LORD is one". Divided worship is practical polytheism; whatever splits your allegiance is functioning as a second god.
