The Big Picture of the Trinity

~25 min · Texts today: Deuteronomy 6:4; Matthew 28:19; 2 Corinthians 13:14 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
Read the three verses above in your ESV, slowly, twice each. They are the doctrine in seed form: the oldest confession of Israel, the baptismal formula, and the apostolic benediction. Write down what each one asserts, and one thing that surprises you when they are set side by side.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back at lesson 1:

The doctrine in one sentence

Before the aerial tour, the destination. James White's definition, which this course will use throughout because it is short enough to memorize and precise enough to defend:

The Forgotten Trinity
"Within the one Being that is God, there exists eternally three coequal and coeternal persons, namely, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit." — James R. White, The Forgotten Trinity, ch. 2, "What Is the Trinity?"

Every word is load-bearing. One Being: monotheism is kept, not compromised; there are not three gods. Three persons: Father, Son, and Spirit are really distinct; they speak to one another, love one another, send one another. Coequal and coeternal: none is a junior god, none had a beginning. And eternally: God did not become triune at Bethlehem or Pentecost; this is what God has always been. Notice also the crucial distinction hiding in the grammar: Being answers the question "what is God?" (there is one answer), person answers "who is God?" (there are three). The doctrine never says God is one and three in the same sense, which would be a contradiction. It says one in one sense, three in another.

Three pillars, five units

The whole doctrine rests on three lines of biblical evidence, and the course is simply those pillars taken in order:

  • Pillar 1: There is one God, and only one. Unit 1 plants this where Israel learned it: the Shema (Deut 6:4), Isaiah's courtroom speeches, and the opening page of Genesis. Nothing later in the course is allowed to bend this.
  • Pillar 2: There are three divine persons. Units 2 and 3 take the Son and the Spirit in turn: five passages where the New Testament calls the Son God and gives him God's works, then four where the Spirit is shown to be a person, and God. The Father's deity no one disputes, so it needs no unit of its own; you will meet it on every page.
  • Pillar 3: The persons are coequal, coeternal, and distinct. Unit 4 reads the passages where all three stand together in one frame: the Jordan, the Great Commission, and the long sentence of Ephesians 1. Unit 5 draws the boundary lines the church drew, and ends where the doctrine always meant to land: prayer, worship, and assurance.

Hold the three pillars and the classic summary follows with a kind of inevitability: one God; the Father is God, the Son is God, the Spirit is God; the Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Spirit, the Spirit is not the Father. Drop a pillar and you fall into one of the old errors: deny pillar 1 and you have three gods (tritheism); deny pillar 2 and the Son or Spirit becomes a creature (Arianism); deny pillar 3's distinctions and the three become masks of one person (modalism). Lesson 22 maps all of these; from here on you can already see where each goes wrong.

Seed, stem, flower

Expect the evidence to arrive the way it arrived in history: progressively. The Old Testament's burden is pillar 1, one God against all the nations' many, though even there you will find unexplained plurals, a Word and a Wisdom and a Spirit of God with odd independence, an Angel of the LORD who speaks as God. The doctrine is in seed. The incarnation and Pentecost force the question, and the New Testament writers, monotheist Jews to a man, begin saying things like "the Word was God" and "why is it that you have lied to the Holy Spirit? You have not lied to man but to God", without ever once suggesting they had abandoned the Shema. The doctrine flowers. The creeds of the fourth century add no new revelation; they fence what was already there against those who denied it. That sequence, seed to flower to fence, is the shape of this course.

Two rails for the whole course

  • Mystery is not contradiction. A contradiction asserts and denies the same thing in the same sense; the Trinity never does. But a God small enough for a creature's full comprehension would be a poor God. Expect to end each unit knowing more and still standing before something you cannot see the bottom of. That is called worship.
  • Revealed, not invented. Nobody sat down and devised this doctrine; it is unlike anything the philosophers produced, and the church accepted it under protest of its own tidy-mindedness, because the texts kept insisting. Your job is the Berean one: examine whether these things are so.

Application

  • About God God has eternally been Father, Son, and Spirit, which means love, fellowship, and giving are not things God started doing after creation; they are what God is. He did not create you out of loneliness.
  • About you You were baptized (or will be) into one name borne by three persons. The doctrine is not a puzzle appended to your faith; it is the grammar of it.
  • Do Memorize White's one-sentence definition this week, word for word. It is the course's working tool, and you will be asked to retrieve it in nearly every lesson from here on.
