Lesson 0001 · Foundations

How to Study a Doctrine

~25 min · Passage: 2 Timothy 2:15; Acts 17:10-12 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
Read 2 Timothy 2:15 and Acts 17:10-12 in your ESV before going further. Two short texts. Write down: what the approved worker does with the word of truth, and what the Bereans did with Paul's preaching, how often, and with what result.

What a doctrine is

A doctrine is the Bible's teaching on one subject, gathered from everywhere the Bible speaks of it and stated carefully. The word simply means "teaching". You already hold doctrines whether or not you have studied any: everyone who says "God hears my prayers" is making a doctrinal claim about who God is and how he relates to his people. The question is never whether you will have doctrine; it is whether yours will be assembled from the text with care or absorbed from the surrounding air without it.

Book study and doctrine study are the same discipline pointed in different directions. A book study walks one text and receives every subject it raises. A doctrine study fixes one subject and walks every text that raises it. The danger of the second is obvious: once you know what you are looking for, it is easy to start finding it wherever you look, wrenching verses out of their setting to prop up a system. That is called proof-texting, and this course is built to avoid it.

The method, unchanged

The safeguard is that every lesson here is still an inductive study of a single passage. The three steps do not change:

  • Observation: what does the text say? Read the passage twice, in context, and write down what is actually there before anyone tells you what it means.
  • Interpretation: what did it mean to its author and first readers? Context first, grammar and history next, Scripture interpreting Scripture always.
  • Application: what does it demand of you? A doctrine you can define but do not worship by is not yet learned.

The only addition is a fourth movement across the course: correlation. After the passages have each spoken in their own context, we set them beside one another and state what they teach together. That is all systematic theology is when it is done honestly: the sum of sound exegesis, not a substitute for it. The order matters absolutely. Passages first, summary after. The creed at the end of this course will not tell the texts what to say; the texts will show you why the creed says what it does.

Why the Trinity, and why it is worth the work

The doctrine of the Trinity is the Christian answer to the largest possible question: who is God? Its whole content can be stated in three lines that every unit of this course exists to establish: there is exactly one God; the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit is each fully that one God; and the Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Spirit, the Spirit is not the Father, three distinct persons. Hold all three lines and you have the doctrine. Drop any one and you have an old and named error, which lesson 22 will map.

Be warned of one thing at the start: the word "Trinity" is not in the Bible. That bothers some people, and it should not. The word is a label coined to summarize what the texts jointly and unavoidably teach, the way "monotheism" and "omniscience" are labels. The question this course trains you to answer is not "is the word there?" but "is the teaching there?", and to answer it with your finger on the passages.

The Berean posture

Now these Jews were more noble than those in Thessalonica; they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so. Acts 17:11, ESV

Notice both halves. The Bereans received the preaching with all eagerness: no folded arms, no cynicism. And they examined the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so: no gullibility either. Eager and careful at once. That is the posture for a doctrine course, especially this doctrine, where the church has thought hard for two thousand years and where your teacher, your favorite author, and this course itself must all submit to the same test: is that what the text says?

How each lesson runs

  • Read first. Every lesson opens by telling you the passage. Read it twice in your ESV and write at least five observations before you continue. The lesson then checks your observations, never replaces them.
  • Recall quizzes. Each lesson begins by retrieving the previous one, because retrieval is what fixes memory. Recap lessons close each unit.
  • Memory verses. One anchor verse per stretch of the course, listed in memory verses. The first activates in lesson 3.
  • Notebook. The notes panel on every lesson takes your observations, interpretations, and applications, and compiles them in My Notebook.

Practice: observe before you conclude

One exercise before lesson 2. Read this verse slowly and write three observations, nothing clever, just what is there:

Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Matthew 28:19, ESV

Things a careful reader notices: "name" is singular, not "names". Three are named under it, joined by "and... and", set side by side without ranking. And the setting is baptism, the entrance of every disciple, not a seminar for advanced students. Do not interpret yet; lesson 19 will walk this passage properly. Just notice how much was sitting on the surface, waiting for observation. That is the whole method in miniature.

Application

  • About God He has spoken, and he approves the worker who handles what he said rightly (2 Tim 2:15). Careful study is not academic pride; it is reverence.
  • About you You already have a doctrine of God. This course will test it against the text, and parts of it may need rebuilding. Decide now to let the passages win.
  • Do Set your time and place for this course, the same discipline as a book study. Write one sentence in your notebook: what do you currently believe the word "Trinity" means? Keep it; you will grade it yourself in lesson 24.

Check yourself

Before next lesson
Lesson 2 is the aerial view of the whole doctrine. No new passage to master, but reread Matthew 28:19 and read Deuteronomy 6:4, and write one honest question you have about the Trinity, anything from "how is this not a contradiction?" to "does it matter for my prayers?" The course exists to answer it.
Primary source
This course reads alongside James R. White, The Forgotten Trinity (Bethany House), chapter by chapter; get a copy this week. Start with chapter 1, "Why the 'Forgotten' Trinity?". White's opening charge sets the tone for the whole course: "If you are a Christian, you are a theologian. You have no choice." Also read the MacArthur Study Bible note on 2 Timothy 2:15: what does "rightly handling" require that casual reading does not?
Church prompt
Ask someone at church this week: "If a neighbor asked you what the Trinity is, what would you say in two sentences?" Do not correct anything; just listen. You are collecting the starting point this course is built to improve on, including your own.

Questions welcome, always: "is doctrine divisive?", "why trust the creeds at all?", "what if two passages seem to disagree?" Ask your teacher.
