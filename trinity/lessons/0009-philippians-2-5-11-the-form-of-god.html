Philippians 2:5-11: The Form of God

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The setting is humility, not christology (vv. 1-5). Paul is telling a beloved church to count others more significant than themselves, and reaches for the highest theology in his letters as the working example: "have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus". The passage may be an early hymn; either way, this is what the first churches sang and assumed.
  • "Though he was in the form of God" (v. 6). The participle is present, existing in the form of God, before any downward step is taken.
  • "Equality with God" is his to reckon with (v. 6). "He did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped": you cannot decline to exploit what you do not have. The not-grasping presumes the possessing.
  • "Emptied himself" is immediately defined (v. 7). Not by subtraction of deity, but "by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men". The emptying is an adding: form of God, plus form of servant.
  • The steps go down, and down, and down (vv. 7-8). Servant; born as man; humbled; obedient to death; "even death on a cross", the bottom rung of the ancient world.
  • "Therefore God has highly exalted him" (v. 9). The Father answers the descent with exaltation and bestows "the name that is above every name".
  • Isaiah 45:23, quoted at the summit (vv. 10-11). "At the name of Jesus every knee should bow... and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord." You bookmarked this verse in lesson 4; here is where the bookmark lands.
  • "To the glory of God the Father" (v. 11). The universal worship of Jesus does not compete with the Father's glory; it constitutes it.

Interpretation

What "form of God" and the emptying actually say

...who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, by taking the form of a servant, being born in the likeness of men. Philippians 2:6-7, ESV

"Form" here is not shape or appearance in our casual sense; it is the manner of existence that expresses what something truly is, which is why the same word structures both halves: the one existing in the form of God took the form of a servant, and no one thinks the second form was a costume. He was really God; he became really a servant. The old "kenosis" mistake, that the Son emptied himself of deity, of omniscience or omnipotence, runs aground on the passage's own grammar: Paul tells you how he emptied himself, "by taking". The incarnation is not God minus something; it is God plus humanity, majesty veiled in flesh rather than abandoned. Notice too what this passage does to the mind: the eternal Son's equality with God is the presupposition of his humility, not its contradiction. Precisely because he was in the form of God, his refusal to exploit it and his descent to a slave's death reveal what God is actually like. The cross is not out of character for God; it is the character of God, displayed.

The summit: Isaiah's oath at Jesus' feet

Now finish the argument this course set up in lesson 4. Isaiah 45:22-23 is the most jealously monotheistic oath in Scripture: "I am God, and there is no other... To me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear." Paul, a Pharisee who could recite that chapter, takes God's own unbreakable oath and locates its fulfillment "at the name of Jesus", with every tongue confessing that Jesus Christ is Lord, the very word his Greek Bible used for YHWH. There are only two ways to read this. Either Paul is announcing the most audacious blasphemy in Jewish history, or he is telling you that the man from Nazareth is included in the identity of the one God of Isaiah, so that bowing to him is the bowing Isaiah swore would come, "to the glory of God the Father". The last phrase seals the trinitarian shape: the Son receives YHWH's worship, and the Father is glorified by it, one God, no rivalry, two persons in one doxology. Add the quiet third: this hymn lives two paragraphs from "the fellowship of the Spirit" (2:1). The church that sang this had not worked out Nicaea's vocabulary, but it was already worshiping within the doctrine.

Application

  • About God Equality with God was never a thing to be grasped or hoarded, because giving is what God is. The descent from the form of God to the cross is God's own self-portrait.
  • About you "Have this mind among yourselves": Paul applies the deepest doctrine in this course to how you treat the people in your church this week. Doctrine that does not bend your pride has not been learned.
  • Do Memory verse 4 activates today: Philippians 2:10-11. Learn it alongside Isaiah 45:23 so the quotation is permanent in your mind. Say John 1:1 once, from memory.
