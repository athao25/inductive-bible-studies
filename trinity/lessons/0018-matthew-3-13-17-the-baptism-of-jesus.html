Lesson 0018 · Unit 4: Three Persons, One God

Matthew 3:13-17: The Baptism of Jesus

~25 min · Passage: Matthew 3:13-17 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Matthew 3:13-17 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: why John tries to prevent the baptism, Jesus' stated reason for it, what opens, what descends and in what manner, whose voice speaks, and what the voice says about the one in the water.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • John's protest (v. 14). "I need to be baptized by you, and do you come to me?" The baptizer of repentant sinners knows this candidate does not fit the line he is standing in.
  • "To fulfill all righteousness" (v. 15). Jesus is not repenting; he is identifying, stepping into the place of the sinners he came for, at the head of the line.
  • The heavens were opened (v. 16). The Old Testament longing, "Oh that you would rend the heavens and come down" (Isa 64:1), answered at a riverbank.
  • Three persons, three locations, one moment (vv. 16-17). The Son coming up from the water; the Spirit of God descending like a dove and coming to rest on him; the Father's voice from heaven. Simultaneous, distinct, and interacting.
  • The Spirit descends "like a dove" (v. 16). Gentle, visible, and personal, coming "to rest on him", the anointing Isaiah promised: "the Spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him" (Isa 11:2; 42:1).
  • The voice speaks about the Son (v. 17). "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased." One person speaks, another is spoken of, a third rests upon him. No sleight of hand can make this one person.
  • The sentence is stitched from Scripture (v. 17). "My Son" (Ps 2:7, the royal Son), "beloved" (Gen 22:2, Abraham's Isaac), "in whom I delight" (Isa 42:1, the Servant). The Father speaks his identification of Jesus in the Old Testament's own words.
  • This launches the ministry (4:1). Immediately, "Jesus was led up by the Spirit into the wilderness". The public work begins from this trinitarian commissioning and proceeds under it.

Interpretation

The doctrine, visible

And when Jesus was baptized, immediately he went up from the water, and behold, the heavens were opened to him, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and coming to rest on him; and behold, a voice from heaven said, "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased." Matthew 3:16-17, ESV

Units 2 and 3 argued from statements; this scene simply shows you. At one moment, in one place, the Son stands in the water, the Spirit descends and rests, and the Father speaks from heaven, and the three are irreducibly distinct: the voice is not talking to itself about itself while also descending on itself. This is why the old error called modalism, the idea that Father, Son, and Spirit are one person wearing three successive masks, has always broken first on this passage; a one-person God cannot occupy this scene. The old catechisms loved to say it plainly: here the whole Trinity manifests itself. And notice what the three are doing, because it is one action, not three: the Father commissions and delights, the Spirit anoints and empowers, the Son submits and stands with sinners, one saving mission beginning in a single frame. What the upper room described as choreography (the Father sending in the Son's name, the Son sending from the Father), the Jordan displays in public, with the heavens open.

Why the persons are met here, and not in a diagram

It matters that the clearest trinitarian text in the Gospels is a narrative, not a formula. God did not reveal the Trinity by publishing a definition; he revealed it, as White insists, "not merely in words but instead in the very action of the Triune God in redemption itself". You meet the persons the way the first disciples did: watching the Son obey, the Spirit descend, the Father delight. This has a practical edge the church has always drawn for baptism itself: the voice that says "my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased" speaks over the Son as he stands in sinners' water, taking their place; and everyone united to the Son by faith is baptized into that same name (Matt 28:19, next lesson) and hears, in him, the same verdict. The Father's delight, the Son's standing-in, the Spirit's resting: your assurance has trinitarian architecture. The doctrine you have spent seventeen lessons building is not scaffolding around the gospel. It is the gospel's own frame, visible at the Jordan before a single sermon of the ministry is preached.

Application

  • About God The Father's first public word in the Gospels is delight in his Son. Eternal love between the persons is not speculation; you have heard it spoken aloud.
  • About you If you are in Christ, the verdict spoken over him at the water's edge is the verdict you stand under: beloved, and pleasing, for his sake.
  • Do Read the scene once more slowly and pray to each person in turn, briefly, in the roles the scene gives them: to the Father who delights, the Son who stood in your place, the Spirit who rests and empowers. Review John 14:26.
