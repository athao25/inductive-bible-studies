Lesson 0016 · Unit 3: The Spirit Is God

1 Corinthians 2:6-16: The Depths of God

~25 min · Passage: 1 Corinthians 2:6-16 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read 1 Corinthians 2:6-16 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: what no eye has seen nor ear heard, how those things reach us (v. 10), what the Spirit searches, the human-spirit analogy of verse 11, and why the natural person cannot understand.

Recall first

Retrieval before new input. Without looking back:

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • Hidden wisdom, decreed "before the ages for our glory" (v. 7). The gospel is not an improvisation; it was God's secret plan from eternity, unknown to "the rulers of this age" who crucified the Lord of glory (v. 8).
  • "The Lord of glory" (v. 8). A title for YHWH ("the King of glory", Ps 24), given to the crucified one, in a paragraph about something else. Unit 2's pattern, appearing even here.
  • Revelation "through the Spirit" (v. 10). What no eye saw and no heart imagined does not stay hidden; God reveals it, and the channel is the Spirit.
  • "The Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God" (v. 10). The word is active, present: he plumbs the deepest things of God as his native element.
  • The analogy (v. 11). Who knows a person's thoughts except that person's own spirit within? "So also no one comprehends the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God." Your inner life is accessible to you alone; God's inner life is comprehended by the Spirit.
  • "That we might understand" (v. 12). The Spirit received is the Spirit "who is from God", given so that believers actually understand what God has freely given.
  • Words taught by the Spirit (v. 13). The apostles impart revelation "in words not taught by human wisdom but taught by the Spirit": a claim about the very sentences of Scripture.
  • Natural and spiritual (vv. 14-15). The natural person does not receive the things of the Spirit; they are folly to him, "because they are spiritually discerned". Understanding Scripture is a moral and spiritual event, not merely an intellectual one.
  • The Isaiah question, answered strangely (v. 16). "Who has understood the mind of the LORD?" (Isa 40:13). Paul's answer: "we have the mind of Christ." The mind of YHWH and the mind of Christ are interchangeable in his hands.

Interpretation

The Spirit knows God the way God knows himself

...these things God has revealed to us through the Spirit. For the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God. For who knows a person's thoughts except the spirit of that person, which is in him? So also no one comprehends the thoughts of God except the Spirit of God. 1 Corinthians 2:10-11, ESV

Follow the analogy precisely, because it is the unit's final and deepest argument. Your spirit is not your assistant, your emissary, or your best friend; it is you on the inside, which is exactly why it alone knows your unspoken thoughts. Paul says the Spirit of God stands to God as your spirit stands to you: on the inside of the divine life, comprehending "the depths of God", the interior fullness no creature can reach; Isaiah asked "who has measured the Spirit of the LORD?" expecting the answer no one, and Paul's teaching agrees: none instructed him, for he is not outside what he knows. A creature, however exalted, knows God from without, by revelation and gift; the Spirit knows God from within, comprehensively, as his own depths. Omniscient knowledge of God's own inner life is not a delegable skill; it is a divine perfection. The syllogism of Acts 5 said the Spirit is God; this passage shows the texture of it: he is God knowing himself. And yet, once more, he is not simply identical with the Father: the Spirit is "of God", searches, is sent, is received; person distinct, being one. Pillar 2 now stands complete for the Spirit as it does for the Son.

Why you understand your Bible at all

Now read the passage for its purpose, which is not speculative. Paul's argument runs: the depths of God are closed to eye, ear, and heart; the Spirit alone comprehends them; and that very Spirit has been given to believers "that we might understand the things freely given us by God", speaking through apostolic words he himself taught. This is the doctrine of the Trinity underwriting your morning Bible reading. If the Spirit were a creature, revelation would be secondhand at every point, a report about God carried by a courier who has himself never been inside; because he is God, Scripture is God's self-knowledge graciously worded for creatures, and illumination is God himself opening your eyes to what he wrote. It also explains verse 14 without elitism: the difference between the natural and the spiritual reader is not IQ but indwelling. Which returns this unit to where it began, and to the course's method itself: every "read it twice in your ESV first" in these lessons is an act of dependence on the Spirit of God. Pray before you read, not as a ritual, but because the author lives in you.

Application

  • About God God is not ultimately hidden. The one who alone comprehends his depths has been given to you, and he wrote a book.
  • About you If Scripture has ever moved from folly to wisdom, from dull to alive in your hands, you have experienced the Spirit's deity firsthand: only God can show you God.
  • Do For the next week, pray one sentence before every Bible reading: "Spirit of God, you search the depths of God; open my eyes." Then read. Review memory verses 1-6; the unit recap will demand all of them.
