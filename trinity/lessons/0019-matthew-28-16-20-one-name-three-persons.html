Matthew 28:16-20: One Name, Three Persons

~25 min · Passage: Matthew 28:16-20 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Matthew 28:16-20 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write down at least five observations: what the eleven did when they saw him and what some did even then, the scope of the authority Jesus claims, the four commands or participles of the commission, the grammar of "the name", and the last promise of the book.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • "They worshiped him, but some doubted" (v. 17). Matthew's honesty; and note what Jesus does about the worship: receives it, as he did at 14:33 and 28:9. After Unit 1 you know what accepted worship means.
  • "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me" (v. 18). Daniel 7:14's dominion, claimed by the risen Son of Man. "Given" marks the exaltation of the crucified mediator (cf. Phil 2:9), not a creature's promotion into deity.
  • "Therefore" (v. 19). The mission to all nations rests on the total authority. Discipling the world is not ambition; it is deduction.
  • "Baptizing them in the name" (v. 19). Singular. Not "in the names of", and not "in the name of the Father, and in the name of the Son...". One name.
  • "Of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (v. 19). Three, each with the article in the Greek, "the Father, the Son, the Holy Spirit", set side by side without ranking, sharing the one name. A curious trio if one is God, one an exalted creature, and one an impersonal force.
  • Entry rite, not advanced seminar (v. 19). This name is for every disciple of every nation at the front door of the faith. The Trinity is not a specialist's doctrine; it is the first thing said over a Christian.
  • "Teaching them to observe all that I have commanded" (v. 20). Baptism first, lifelong instruction after; the church's whole program in a participle.
  • "I am with you always" (v. 20). The book that opened with "Immanuel, God with us" (1:23) closes with the risen Jesus promising exactly that, personally, to the end of the age, everywhere at once. Only God keeps this promise.

Interpretation

The weight of the singular

"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit..." Matthew 28:19, ESV

In Scripture, "the name" is never a mere label; it is the revealed identity, presence, and authority of God: the name placed on Israel in blessing (Num 6:27), the name that dwells in the temple, the name YHWH proclaimed to Moses. To be baptized "in the name" is to be brought under that identity and given over to that ownership. And here the one name, singular, is jointly borne by three, each marked out distinctly: the Father, the Son, the Holy Spirit. The grammar quietly performs the whole doctrine. One name: one God, one Being, the Shema untouched. Three articled persons: really distinct, none absorbed into another, none an appendix to another. Try any alternative and the sentence breaks. Three gods would need three names. One person in three modes would not need three articles, and the Jordan has already ruled it out. A God, a creature, and a force could not stand under one divine name without blasphemy, the very blasphemy Unit 1 taught Israel to die rather than commit. The baptismal formula is White's definition in liturgical form, centuries early: within the one name that is God's, three coequal persons.

What baptism into this name means, and why the promise can hold

Now put the commission's three great statements together, because they stand or fall as one. "All authority in heaven and on earth" is God's authority (Dan 7:14 gives dominion to the Son of Man precisely so that "all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him"). "The name" is God's name, shared by Son and Spirit. "I am with you always, to the end of the age" is God's omnipresence, promised in the first person by the man on the mountain. Matthew's Gospel is framed by it: "God with us" at the birth (1:23), "I am with you" at the end. Every disciple of every nation for twenty centuries has been baptized into this identity, which means every Christian you know, whatever they could articulate, was claimed at the font by the triune God: brought under the Father's fatherhood, united to the Son's death and resurrection, indwelt by the Spirit. The doctrine of the Trinity is therefore not one item on the church's syllabus; it is the name on the church's front door, and the reason the mission cannot fail: the authority is total, the name is one, and the presence is permanent.

Application

  • About God The triune name is not a formula to recite but an identity to live under: Father over you, Son beside you, Spirit within you, one God, always with you.
  • About you If you are baptized, that name is on you. Much of the Christian life is learning to be what your baptism already declared.
  • Do Memory verse 7 activates today: Matthew 28:19. Learn it with the grammar deliberate: the name (singular), of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Review Deuteronomy 6:4 beside it; hold both without strain, because the text does.
