Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The before-picture is bleak (vv. 11-12). Separated from Christ, alienated from Israel, strangers to the covenants, "having no hope and without God in the world". Five clauses of distance.
  • "But now in Christ Jesus" (v. 13). The hinge, and the means: "brought near by the blood of Christ". Doctrine of the Trinity or not, nearness to God is purchased, not self-arranged.
  • "He himself is our peace" (v. 14). Peace is a person before it is a feeling. He made peace (v. 15) and he preached peace (v. 17), to far and near alike.
  • One new man, one body (vv. 15-16). The dividing wall down, both reconciled "to God in one body through the cross".
  • Verse 18, the sentence this lesson is named for. "For through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father." Three persons, three prepositions, one movement of prayer.
  • "Access" (v. 18). The word for being ushered into a king's presence. Not correspondence with the palace; standing in the throne room.
  • From strangers to household (v. 19). "No longer strangers and aliens... members of the household of God." The far-off end up not in the lobby but in the family.
  • The temple, again, and the Trinity in it (vv. 20-22). Built on the apostles and prophets, Christ the cornerstone, "a holy temple in the Lord... a dwelling place for God by the Spirit". The passage ends where verse 18 pointed: God dwelling with his people, trinitarianly.

Interpretation

The doctrine of the Trinity is a description of prayer

For through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father. Ephesians 2:18, ESV

Here is the whole course in eleven words. To the Father: prayer's destination, the person Jesus taught disciples to address, "Our Father in heaven". Through him, the Son: prayer's ground and route, the mediator whose blood brought the far-off near, which is why Christians pray "in Jesus' name", not as a closing formula but as a plea's entire legal standing. In one Spirit: prayer's enabling atmosphere, the indwelling person who helps our weakness, since "we do not know what to pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us" (Rom 8:26). Every Christian who has ever prayed clumsily and been heard has used this verse, knowingly or not. And now run the course's test one last time: if the Son were a creature, praying "through him" would hang your access on creaturely merit; if the Spirit were a force, "in one Spirit" would mean praying in a mood. The doctrine is not an intellectual tax on prayer; it is the reason prayer works. This is what the church means by the shape of its worship: to the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit, and it is also why praying directly to the Son (Acts 7:59) and to the Spirit is right and good, since each person is fully the one God, while the ordinary grain of Scripture's own prayers runs along verse 18's route.

One access, one household, one temple

Do not miss what verse 18 is doing in its paragraph: it is Paul's argument for church unity. Jew and Gentile, the ancient world's deepest religious division, "both have access in one Spirit to the Father", and therefore are one household, one new man, one temple under construction. The Trinity grounds the church's oneness: because access runs through the same Son in the same Spirit to the same Father, the people who pray that way belong to each other, whatever their history. Jesus prayed for exactly this the night before the cross: "that they may all be one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you... so that the world may know that you sent me" (John 17:21-23). The unity of the church is meant to be the world's visible evidence of the invisible unity of the persons, which makes division in the church not merely a management failure but a false statement about God. And the closing image gathers the whole Bible's story: the God whose Spirit hovered at creation, whose glory filled the tabernacle, who tabernacled among us in the Word made flesh, now dwells, by the Spirit, in a temple built of people, with access thrown open. The doctrine you can now define is the floor plan of the house you live in.

Application

  • About God The Father is not reluctantly accessible. The Son's blood and the Spirit's indwelling exist because the Father willed the far-off brought into the household (1:5).
  • About you Your weakest prayer travels the strongest route ever built: through the Son, in the Spirit, to the Father. Pray more, and more plainly, on that basis.
  • Do Memory verse 8, the last, activates today: Ephesians 2:18. This week, pray once deliberately along its route, naming each person and their role. And do one concrete thing to repair or strengthen unity with another believer; verse 18 makes it a doctrinal act.
