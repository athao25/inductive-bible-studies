Acts 5:1-11: Lying to the Holy Spirit

~25 min · Passage: Acts 5:1-11 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Acts 5:1-11 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now (glance at 4:32-37 for the setting). Write down at least five observations: what Ananias and Sapphira actually did with the money, what Peter says filled Ananias's heart and to whom he lied (v. 3), how Peter renames the offense in verse 4, whether the sin was in the keeping or the lying, and what came upon the whole church.

Observation check

Compare your list. Strong observations on this passage include:

  • The backdrop is Barnabas (4:36-37). A real act of generosity, publicly honored. Ananias and Sapphira want Barnabas's reputation at a discount.
  • The property was theirs (v. 4). Peter says so explicitly: unsold, it remained theirs; sold, the proceeds were at their disposal. The sin was never the keeping; it was the performance.
  • "Why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit?" (v. 3). The offended party, named first, is the Spirit.
  • "You have not lied to man but to God" (v. 4). Peter restates the same offense with a new name. Lied to the Holy Spirit; lied to God. One sin, two designations, no gap between them.
  • You can lie to him (vv. 3-4). Lying requires a someone on the receiving end, a person who can be deceived, or in this case, cannot be. And the Spirit knew: the lie was detected before it was finished.
  • "Test the Spirit of the Lord" (v. 9). Sapphira's version of the sin echoes Israel's wilderness sin of putting YHWH to the test (Deut 6:16).
  • Judgment is immediate and divine (vv. 5, 10). No apostle pronounces a sentence; Ananias and Sapphira fall dead before God, as Nadab and Abihu and Uzzah did.
  • "Great fear came upon the whole church" (vv. 5, 11). The first use of the word "church" in Acts arrives wrapped in the fear of God.

Interpretation

The plainest syllogism in the doctrine

But Peter said, "Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit...? You have not lied to man but to God." Acts 5:3-4, ESV

Unit 3's second claim, the Spirit is God, is nowhere stated more economically than here. Peter names the offense twice in two sentences: a lie to the Holy Spirit; a lie to God. Not "to God's representative", not "to a power from God", but to God, as the direct object of the deception. The equation is made in passing, under no doctrinal pressure, in a narrative about money and hypocrisy, which is precisely what gives it its evidential weight: Peter is not arguing the Spirit's deity, he is assuming it, the way you assume what everyone in the room already knows. Add the supporting grain of the passage: the Spirit is set opposite Satan as the one being contended against (v. 3), he searches hearts and detects the lie no human could catch (cf. 1 Cor 2:10-11, next lesson), testing him is renamed with the wilderness formula reserved for testing YHWH (v. 9), and the judgment that answers the offense is God's own, unmediated. Elsewhere the New Testament completes the same pattern quietly: believers are God's temple because the Spirit dwells in them (1 Cor 3:16), the Lord who spoke Isaiah's words is the Spirit who spoke them (Acts 28:25-27), and blasphemy against him is the gravest possible (Matt 12:31-32). None of these texts is about the Trinity; all of them assume it.

Why the severity, and what it teaches about the Spirit's presence

Readers flinch at this passage, and should feel its weight rather than explain it away. The pattern is biblical and precise: judgment falls hardest at inaugurations, when God's presence newly arrives among his people. Fire from the LORD took Nadab and Abihu at the tabernacle's dedication (Lev 10); Uzzah died as the ark came to Jerusalem (2 Sam 6); and here, at the founding of the church, death answers the first hypocrisy. The common thread is holiness at close quarters: the nearer God dwells, the more seriously his presence must be taken. And that is the passage's contribution to this unit beyond the syllogism: the one who dwells in the church, whose presence made the community so weighty that a staged donation became a capital offense, is the Spirit. He is not the church's atmosphere or its shared enthusiasm; he is the holy God in residence, which is exactly what "your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit" (1 Cor 6:19) claims for every believer. The fear that fell on the church (v. 11) was not servile terror; it was the recalibration that follows discovering who, exactly, is in the house.

Application

  • About God The Spirit searches hearts and cannot be deceived. Every unspoken motive is already known, which is terrifying to hypocrisy and deeply comforting to honesty.
  • About you The sin here was religious image management: wanting to appear more devoted than you are. It remains the church's most respectable sin, and this passage is God's opinion of it.
  • Do Identify one place your public spiritual image outruns your private reality, and close the gap this week in the honest direction, before God who indwells you. Review John 14:26.
