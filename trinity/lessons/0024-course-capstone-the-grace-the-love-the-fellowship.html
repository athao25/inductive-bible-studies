Capstone: The Grace, the Love, the Fellowship

~30 min · Texts: 2 Corinthians 13:14; John 17:20-26 · And everything behind you

The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all. 2 Corinthians 13:14, ESV

Paul's last line to a difficult church, and the church's benediction ever since: three persons, three gifts, one blessing, spoken over ordinary congregations for twenty centuries. You now know what it asserts and what it cost to keep. Today you finish the course the way the doctrine deserves: retrieval, an honest self-grading, and worship.

First: grade your lesson 1 sentence

Take out the sentence you wrote in lesson 1 defining the Trinity. Grade it against the three pillars: did it keep one God? three fully divine persons? real distinction without ranking of nature? Most students find their old sentence was quietly modalist ("God in three forms") or vaguely partialist ("three parts of God"). Write the corrected sentence beneath it, in your own words, and date it. That pair of sentences is the course's measurement of itself.

Recall: the whole course

The course in one frame

Unit 1 poured the foundation: one God, exclusively, jealously, with marks he swears to share with no one. Unit 2 watched the New Testament hand every mark to the Son, in five independent witnesses, while the writers went on praying the Shema. Unit 3 did the same for the Spirit: a person, not a power; God, not a gift. Unit 4 saw the three together, at the Jordan, in the baptismal name, in the architecture of salvation, distinct persons in one seamless work. Unit 5 walked the boundary lines, learned why the creeds say "begotten, not made" and "of one Being", and found the doctrine's destination in eleven words: through him, in one Spirit, to the Father. The conclusion, in White's sentence you memorized in lesson 2: within the one Being that is God, there exists eternally three coequal and coeternal persons, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. You did not receive that sentence from a council. You watched it rise out of the texts, which is the only place it ever came from.

The last passage: inside the fellowship

"Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory that you have given me because you loved me before the foundation of the world." John 17:24, ESV

Read John 17:20-26 once more, slowly, because it takes you somewhere no other passage in this course has: inside the conversation of the persons, on the night before the cross, with you in it by name, "those who will believe in me through their word" (v. 20). Here is the love "before the foundation of the world" between the Father and the Son; here is the Son asking that believers be brought into that love, "that the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them" (v. 26). This is why the doctrine matters more than the sum of its proof-texts. If God were a solitary monad, love would be something God began to do when there was finally someone to love, and salvation would be employment in his service. Because God is triune, love is what God eternally is, and salvation is adoption into a fellowship older than the universe: the Father loving you with the very love he has for his Son, the Son bringing you where he is, the Spirit pouring that love into your heart (Rom 5:5). White ends his book insisting the doctrine is for devotion: "To know Christ truly is to know the Trinity, for God has not revealed himself in such a way as to allow us to have true and balanced knowledge of the Father outside of such knowledge of the Son." That is where this course has been going since the Shema: not to a diagram solved, but to a fellowship entered.

Where you go from here

  • Keep the verses. Eight anchors, Deuteronomy 6:4 to Ephesians 2:18, on the spaced schedule in memory verses. They are the doctrine in portable form.
  • Keep the method. Observation, interpretation, application, correlation last. Any doctrine you ever examine, examine this way; the doctrine map compresses this course for review and for teaching others.
  • Keep the posture. Berean and doxological at once: eager, careful, and worshiping past the edge of comprehension.
  • Teach it. You now hold the three-pillar argument, the answers to the old errors, and eight texts from memory. Within the month, walk one person through it; teaching is where this course either becomes yours or evaporates.

Check yourself: the final quiz

The benediction, received

End the course in prayer, using Paul's own order: thank the Lord Jesus Christ for grace, the descent you studied in Philippians 2; thank God the Father for love, the choosing before the foundation of the world; thank the Holy Spirit for fellowship, the indwelling that makes you a temple. Then say the benediction of 2 Corinthians 13:14 aloud over yourself and whoever you next worship beside. You know, now, what you are saying.
