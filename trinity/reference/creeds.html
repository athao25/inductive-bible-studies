Creeds

The church's fences around the doctrine, in full text. They add no revelation; every clause guards a text this course studied. Lesson 22 tells their story.

The Nicene Creed (325, expanded 381)

Properly the Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed; the form recited in most churches today. Traditional English translation.

We believe in one God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth, of all things visible and invisible.

And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all worlds; God of God, Light of Light, very God of very God; begotten, not made, being of one substance with the Father, by whom all things were made; who for us men and for our salvation came down from heaven, and was incarnate by the Holy Spirit of the virgin Mary, and was made man; and was crucified also for us under Pontius Pilate; he suffered and was buried; and the third day he rose again, according to the Scriptures; and ascended into heaven, and sits on the right hand of the Father; and he shall come again, with glory, to judge the living and the dead; whose kingdom shall have no end.

And we believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and Giver of Life; who proceeds from the Father and the Son; who with the Father and the Son together is worshiped and glorified; who spoke by the prophets. And we believe in one holy catholic and apostolic Church. We acknowledge one baptism for the remission of sins; and we look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come. Amen. Nicaea 325 / Constantinople 381
  • "One God... Maker": pillar 1 (lessons 3-6).
  • "God of God, Light of Light": Hebrews 1:3's radiance (lesson 11).
  • "Begotten, not made, of one substance": against Arianism (lesson 22); John 1:1-3, 14.
  • "By whom all things were made": John 1:3; Col 1:16 (lessons 7, 10).
  • "The Lord and Giver of Life... together worshiped and glorified": Unit 3 in creedal form.
  • "Who proceeds from the Father and the Son": "and the Son" (Latin filioque) is the Western addition, from John 15:26 with 16:7; the Eastern churches confess procession from the Father alone. A real division, but within the doctrine, not about it.

The Athanasian Creed (5th century), trinitarian section

Not by Athanasius, but the fullest creedal statement of the doctrine. Excerpted; traditional translation, lightly modernized.

And the catholic faith is this: that we worship one God in Trinity, and Trinity in Unity; neither confounding the persons nor dividing the substance. For there is one person of the Father, another of the Son, and another of the Holy Spirit. But the Godhead of the Father, of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit is all one: the glory equal, the majesty coeternal. Such as the Father is, such is the Son, and such is the Holy Spirit... The Father eternal, the Son eternal, and the Holy Spirit eternal; and yet they are not three eternals, but one eternal... So the Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God; and yet they are not three Gods, but one God... The Father is made of none, neither created nor begotten. The Son is of the Father alone; not made, nor created, but begotten. The Holy Spirit is of the Father and of the Son; neither made, nor created, nor begotten, but proceeding. And in this Trinity none is before or after another; none is greater or less than another; but the whole three persons are coeternal together, and coequal. Athanasian Creed (Quicunque Vult), excerpt
  • "Neither confounding the persons": against modalism. "Nor dividing the substance": against tritheism and partialism. The two rails in one sentence.
  • "None is greater or less": against subordinationism; John 5:23 (lesson 8).
  • Begotten / proceeding: the only distinctions the creed allows are the relations of origin (lesson 22, doctrine map).

How to use creeds as a Berean

  • Creeds are norms under the norm: authoritative summaries because and insofar as they state what Scripture states. The test is always Acts 17:11.
  • Use them as checklists, not sources: after your own exegesis, the creed tells you whether you have wandered from what the whole church has always found in these texts.
  • Recite them as confession, not recitation: every clause cost someone something. "Begotten, not made" is a war memorial (lesson 22).
