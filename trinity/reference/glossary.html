Reference

Glossary

Every technical term used in the lessons, defined once, used consistently.

Study method

TermDefinition
DoctrineThe Bible's teaching on one subject, gathered from everywhere the Bible speaks of it and stated carefully. Everyone holds doctrines; the question is whether they are assembled from the text with care.
Inductive studyDrawing conclusions out of the text by observation, rather than bringing conclusions to it. Three ordered steps: observation, interpretation, application.
CorrelationThe fourth movement added in doctrine study: setting passages beside one another and stating what they teach together, only after each has spoken in its own context. Honest systematic theology is the sum of sound exegesis.
Proof-textingBending verses out of their context to prop up a system: the peculiar danger of doctrine study, which this course guards against by keeping every lesson an inductive study of one passage.
Progressive revelationGod's revealing of himself in stages across redemptive history: seed in the Old Testament, flower in the New, fence in the creeds. Later revelation never contradicts earlier; it unfolds it.
Berean postureFrom Acts 17:11: receiving teaching with all eagerness while examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things are so. Eager and careful at once; the posture this course asks toward teachers, creeds, and itself.

The doctrine's grammar

TermDefinition
TrinityOne God in three persons: "Within the one Being that is God, there exists eternally three coequal and coeternal persons, namely, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit" (White). A summary label, like "monotheism": the word is post-biblical, the content is biblical.
Being / essence / substanceWhat God is: the one, undivided divine nature, with all its perfections. The "one" of the doctrine. Greek ousia, Latin substantia.
PersonWho God is: a distinct subject who speaks, loves, sends, and is addressed: the Father, the Son, the Spirit. The "three" of the doctrine. Not a separate being, and not a mere role or mode.
Homoousios"Of one Being/substance": Nicaea's word for the Son's full deity, chosen because every biblical phrase had been slipped by Arian reinterpretation. The one word the fourth century's battle was fought over.
Eternal generation ("begotten")The Son's eternal relation to the Father: of the Father, as radiance is of light, sharing his nature; never an event, never a beginning. "Begotten, not made."
ProcessionThe Spirit's eternal relation of origin: he "proceeds from the Father" (John 15:26), sent by Father and Son. The relations of origin (unbegotten, begotten, proceeding) are the only distinctions among the persons.
Inseparable operationThe persons never work apart: every divine act (creation, redemption, indwelling) is one act of the one God, done by all three, each in the manner proper to his person (John 5:19; 16:15).
AppropriationSpeaking of a work as especially the Father's (electing), the Son's (redeeming), or the Spirit's (sealing) because Scripture leads with that person there (Eph 1), while the work remains the undivided work of all three.
Economic / ontological TrinityThe Trinity in the economy: the persons as revealed in the work of salvation (sending, obeying, descending). The ontological (immanent) Trinity: the persons as they eternally are. The economy truly reveals, and never contradicts, the eternal relations; "sent" texts describe mission, not lesser nature.
MonotheismThe confession that there is exactly one God (Deut 6:4; Isa 44-45). Pillar 1 of the doctrine, kept, not compromised, by trinitarianism.
IncarnationThe eternal Son taking a full human nature without ceasing to be God (John 1:14; Phil 2:6-8). Addition of humanity, not subtraction of deity: one person, two natures.
Kenosis ("emptying")From Phil 2:7. Rightly: the Son emptied himself by taking the form of a servant. Wrongly (the "kenotic theory"): that he laid aside divine attributes, which the verse's own grammar refuses.

The errors

TermDefinition
Modalism (Sabellianism)One divine person appearing in three successive modes or masks. Dies at the Jordan (Matt 3:16-17), where three persons act at once. Modern heir: Oneness Pentecostalism.
ArianismThe Son as the first and greatest creature: "there was when he was not". Answered at Nicaea (325) from John 1:3, Col 1:16-17, Heb 1. Modern heirs: Jehovah's Witnesses.
Pneumatomachianism"Spirit-fighters": those who granted the Son's deity but called the Spirit a creature or force. Answered at Constantinople (381): "the Lord and Giver of life... together worshiped and glorified".
TritheismThree gods. No church taught it outright; careless analogies imply it. Dies on the Shema.
SubordinationismSon and Spirit divine but lesser in nature than the Father. Distinct from the true order of persons, which carries no inferiority (John 5:23).
PartialismFather, Son, and Spirit as three parts, each a third of God. Refuted by Col 2:9: in Christ dwells all the fullness. The egg illustration's error.

Words in the texts

TermDefinition
YHWH (the LORD)God's personal name, revealed at the bush (Ex 3:14-15), rendered "LORD" in capitals in English Bibles. The Greek Old Testament rendered it kyrios, "Lord", the title the New Testament gives to Jesus (Phil 2:11).
Shema"Hear" (Hebrew), the first word of Deut 6:4 and the name of Israel's confession: "The LORD our God, the LORD is one." Pillar 1's charter text.
EchadHebrew "one" (Deut 6:4). Can number complex unities (one day, one flesh) but does not by itself teach plurality in God; the honest reading leaves the persons to later revelation.
Logos (the Word)John's title for the eternal Son (John 1:1, 14): God's full self-expression, who was with God, was God, made all things, and became flesh.
Prototokos ("firstborn")Rank and heirship, not birth order: Israel (Ex 4:22) and David (Ps 89:27) are "firstborn" by appointment. Col 1:15's word; Greek for "first-created" (protoktistos) is a different word Paul did not use.
Parakletos (Helper)"One called alongside": advocate, counselor. The Spirit's personal office (John 14:16), held also by Jesus (1 John 2:1). "Another (allos, same kind) Helper."
Morphē ("form")Phil 2:6-7: the manner of existence expressing what something truly is. In the form of God; taking the form of a servant: really God, really servant.
Arrabōn ("guarantee")Down payment in kind, first installment of the thing itself: the Spirit as the beginning of the believer's inheritance (Eph 1:14).
"I am" (egō eimi)Jesus' absolute self-designation (John 8:58, "before Abraham was, I am"), taking up Ex 3:14 and Isaiah's "I am he" (Isa 43:10). His hearers reached for stones.
