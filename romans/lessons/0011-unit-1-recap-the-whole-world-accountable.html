Unit 1 Recap: The Whole World Accountable

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating Romans 1:1-3:20

Three groups, one verdict, and a courtroom that ends in silence. Today you retrieve the argument and say it in your own words. Retrieval is harder than rereading; the difficulty is what makes it stick.

Say it without looking

  1. Recite Romans 1:16-17 (memory verse 1), then 3:23-24 (memory verse 2).
  2. In four sentences, explain how Paul gets from "I am not ashamed of the gospel" to "every mouth stopped".
  3. Name the three groups Paul indicts in order, and the one thing that silences each.
Check your answers
(2) Something like: the gospel reveals God's righteousness by faith, but wrath is revealed too, so the gospel must be shown to be necessary. The pagan world knew God from creation and exchanged him for images, and was handed over. The moralist who condemns that world is condemned by his own standard, and the covenant member who has the law does not keep it. Therefore Israel's own Scriptures pronounce that none is righteous, and the law's work is to stop every mouth. (3) The pagan, silenced by creation's plain testimony (1:19-20); the moralist, silenced by the standard he invokes against others (2:1); the Jew, silenced by the Scriptures he was entrusted with (3:19).

The structure is a narrowing. Each defendant seems better positioned than the last, and each ends in the same place. The final one (the person with the Bible, the training, and the sign) is the hardest to convict and receives the longest treatment, because complacency there is the most durable. That is worth remembering when you read this unit as someone who has been in church for years.

Why this unit had to come first

Paul spends nearly three chapters proving something nobody enjoys hearing, and he does it before mentioning the love of God once. That order is a deliberate strategy, not a temperament. Verse 3:19 names the goal: every mouth stopped. The gospel of 3:21 is unintelligible to a person who is still talking, still comparing themselves favourably, still producing mitigations, still assuming that their advantages are the same thing as acquittal.

So the practical test of whether this unit has done its work is not whether you can recite the outline. It is whether the announcement coming tomorrow ("But now the righteousness of God has been manifested apart from the law") reads as electrifying rather than as familiar. If it reads as familiar, go back and sit in 3:19-20 for another day. There is no hurry, and there is no substitute.

Watch: the two words that open it, what is "manifested apart from the law" and yet witnessed by law and prophets, the four great nouns of verses 24-25, and the two purposes stated with "so that" in verse 26. Church prompt
Ask someone to explain why Romans begins with bad news. If they have never noticed, walk them through the three defendants. It is the clearest illustration in Scripture of why the gospel is good news rather than good advice.
