Lesson 0026 · Unit 3: Chapters 6-8

Romans 8:1-11: No Condemnation

~30 min · Passage: Romans 8:1-11 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Romans 8:1-11 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write at least five observations: the first word of the chapter and what it reaches back to, the two "laws" of verse 2, what the law could not do and why, what God did instead and to what end, the flesh-Spirit contrast running through verses 5-9, and the two resurrections mentioned in verses 10-11.

Recall first

Observation check

  • "There is therefore now no condemnation" (v. 1). Three load-bearing words. Therefore: because of everything since 3:21. Now: already, not at the last day. No: not less, none.
  • "For those who are in Christ Jesus" (v. 1). The only qualification, and it is a location rather than an achievement.
  • Two laws (v. 2). "The law of the Spirit of life… has set you free… from the law of sin and death." Two operating principles, one stronger than the other.
  • What the law could not do, and why (v. 3). "Weakened by the flesh": the failure is located in us, not in the law, exactly as chapter 7 argued.
  • God's action (v. 3). "By sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and for sin, he condemned sin in the flesh." Note "likeness": real humanity, not sinful humanity.
  • Condemnation relocated (v. 3). Sin was condemned: in the flesh, at the cross, which is why there is none left for those in Christ (v. 1).
  • The purpose (v. 4). "In order that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not according to the flesh but according to the Spirit." Note "in us", not merely "for us".
  • Two mindsets (vv. 5-8). Those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh; those according to the Spirit on the things of the Spirit. Mind here means orientation, not intellect.
  • The verdict on the flesh (vv. 7-8). Hostile to God, does not submit to God's law, "indeed, it cannot", and "those who are in the flesh cannot please God." Inability stated twice.
  • The dividing line (v. 9). "Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him." There is no category of Christian without the Spirit.
  • Trinitarian density (vv. 9-11). Spirit of God, Spirit of Christ, Christ in you, the Spirit of him who raised Jesus. Father, Son and Spirit in three verses.
  • A future promise (v. 11). "He who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through his Spirit who dwells in you." Bodily resurrection, guaranteed by the indwelling.

Interpretation

The verdict that answers chapter 7

There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. Romans 8:1, ESV

Set this sentence directly against the cry of 7:24 and you see what Paul is doing. The wretched man asks who will deliver him. The answer, announced in 7:25a and unpacked here, is that the deliverance is already legally secure: whatever the internal war looks like on a given morning, there is no condemnation. Not "less condemnation for those who try hard", not "no condemnation once sanctification is complete". None, now, for everyone in Christ.

The ground is given in verse 3, and it is worth stating precisely. God "condemned sin in the flesh": that is, the condemnation was not cancelled but relocated. It fell, in full, on the Son at the cross. This is why 8:1 is not sentimentality about God overlooking things. Nothing was overlooked; it was borne.

Then verse 4 states the purpose, and it goes further than most readers expect: "in order that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us". Not only credited to us, though it is; actually fulfilled in us, by the Spirit, as we walk according to him. What the law demanded and could not produce, the Spirit produces. That is the answer to "how can grace restrain sin better than law?": a question raised back at 6:14 and left hanging through chapter 7.

Doctrine checkpoint: two inabilities, one dividing line
Verses 7-9 contain some of the strongest statements in Scripture about human inability, and they are the backbone of the Reformed reading of Romans.
  • "The mind that is set on the flesh… does not submit to God's law; indeed, it cannot" (v. 7). Not "will not with difficulty" but cannot. The same verb of impossibility appears again in verse 8: "those who are in the flesh cannot please God."
  • Everyone is in one category or the other (v. 9). "You, however, are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if in fact the Spirit of God dwells in you." No third state, no partial membership.
  • The line is drawn by the Spirit's indwelling, not by experience or attainment (v. 9). "Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him." That sentence rules out any two-tier Christianity in which the Spirit is an optional upgrade for the eager.
Put these together and you have the reason salvation must be entirely of grace. If the unregenerate cannot submit or please God, then the move from flesh to Spirit cannot originate in the flesh. Someone must be given life before they can live. The letter has been heading here since 3:11.

The Spirit arrives

You, however, are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if in fact the Spirit of God dwells in you. Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him. Romans 8:9, ESV

Count the mentions: the Holy Spirit appears about twenty times in chapter 8, after being nearly absent from chapters 1-7. That distribution is not accidental. Chapter 7 showed what the Christian life looks like when attempted with law, effort, and sincerity but without the Spirit's power: knowledge without ability, delight without performance, and a groan at the end. Chapter 8 supplies what was missing.

Notice the interchangeable titles in verses 9-11: "Spirit of God", "Spirit of Christ", and then "Christ in you". Paul moves between them without comment, which tells you both that the Spirit is fully divine and that to have the Spirit is to have Christ present. The indwelling is not an influence but a person, and the same person who raised Jesus from the dead.

Which is why verse 11 can make the promise it does. If the Spirit who raised Jesus lives in you, then your own bodily resurrection is not a hope hanging in the air; it is guaranteed by a present possession. The body that verse 10 concedes is "dead because of sin" (mortal, decaying, subject to the whole catalogue of chapter 7's frustrations) will be given life. Salvation in Romans is never escape from the body. It is the redemption of it (8:23).

Application

  • About God He condemned sin rather than sinners in Christ. The severity and the mercy are the same act.
  • About you The accusations that arrive at 3 a.m. are answerable with one word from verse 1: now. Not "when you improve": now.
  • Do Begin memory verse 7 today (8:1), keeping "therefore". Then ask verse 5's question about the last week: what has your mind actually been set on? Write down one habit that is shaping the setting, and one change.

Church prompt
Ask someone whether they think a Christian can lack the Holy Spirit. Verse 9 settles it in one sentence, and it is worth having that sentence ready, kindly, for anyone who has been made to feel second-class.

Questions welcome, always: "what does 'likeness of sinful flesh' protect?", "if the flesh cannot please God, how does anyone ever believe?", "is 8:4 about our obedience or Christ's?" Ask your teacher; the second is answered by the necessity of new birth, and the third by the words "in us".
