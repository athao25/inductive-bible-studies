Lesson 0036 · Unit 4: Chapters 9-11

Romans 10:14-21: How Shall They Hear

~25 min · Passage: Romans 10:14-21 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Romans 10:14-21 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write at least five observations: the chain of questions in verses 14-15 and the direction it runs, the quotation about feet, the admission in verse 16, the summary statement of verse 17, the two questions "have they not heard?" and "did Israel not understand?", and the posture of God described in the final verse.

Recall first

Observation check

  • A chain running backwards (vv. 14-15). Call → believe → hear → preacher → sent. Paul starts at the end and works back to the origin.
  • It follows directly from verse 13. "Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved", so how does anyone come to call?
  • "How are they to hear without someone preaching?" (v. 14). Literally, "without someone proclaiming"; the preacher is a herald announcing a message that is not his own.
  • "How are they to preach unless they are sent?" (v. 15). The chain terminates in a sending, and the sender is God.
  • Isaiah 52:7 quoted (v. 15). "How beautiful are the feet of those who preach the good news!" In Isaiah, the messenger runs over the mountains to announce that the exile is over.
  • An honest concession (v. 16). "But they have not all obeyed the gospel." Preaching does not guarantee response, and Isaiah 53:1 is quoted to show that the prophets knew it.
  • Note "obeyed the gospel". Belief is described as obedience, matching "the obedience of faith" at 1:5.
  • The famous summary (v. 17). "So faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ."
  • Two excuses closed (vv. 18-21). "Have they not heard?": Psalm 19:4, they have. "Did Israel not understand?": Deuteronomy 32:21 and Isaiah 65:1-2, they did.
  • Gentiles as provocation (v. 19). "I will make you jealous of those who are not a nation." A theme that becomes central in chapter 11.
  • The boldest line (v. 20). Isaiah 65:1: "I have been found by those who did not seek me." Paul notes that Isaiah "is so bold as to say" it.
  • The last image (v. 21). "All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people." The chapter that began with sovereignty ends with outstretched hands.

Interpretation

The chain, and what it is for

How then will they call on him in whom they have not believed? And how are they to believe in him of whom they have never heard? And how are they to hear without someone preaching? And how are they to preach unless they are sent? Romans 10:14-15, ESV

Read the chain forwards and you have the entire logic of Christian mission: God sends, the sent preach, the hearers hear, the hearers believe, the believers call, and the ones who call are saved. Every link is necessary. Remove the preacher and nothing further happens.

Now set this beside chapter 9. Paul has just spent a chapter arguing that mercy depends on God's will rather than human exertion. The obvious objection, "then why bother preaching?", is answered here, in the very next movement, and the answer is not a compromise. God ordains the end and the means together. The elect are not saved without hearing; they are saved by hearing, and someone must be sent. This is why the same apostle who wrote Romans 9 crossed the empire on foot and planned to reach Spain.

Note also the quotation from Isaiah 52:7. The original setting is the announcement that the exile is over: a runner coming over the mountains with news of deliverance, and the watchers seeing his feet before they hear his voice. Feet are not usually beautiful. They are beautiful when they are carrying that message, and Paul applies the line to ordinary preachers of the gospel. Whatever your estimate of your own eloquence, the message you carry is the one Isaiah's runner was carrying.

Doctrine checkpoint: faith comes by hearing
Verse 17 is one of the most quoted verses about faith, and it repays precision.
  • "Hearing" (akoe) means the message heard, not the act of listening. Faith arises from the report itself, which is why the content of preaching matters more than the technique.
  • "The word of Christ" is most likely the message about Christ, spoken by his authority. Christ himself is heard when the gospel is preached: compare Ephesians 2:17, where the risen Christ "came and preached peace" to people he never physically visited.
  • It rules out two errors at once. Against the idea that sincerity in any religion suffices: faith comes from a specific message. Against the idea that experiences or signs produce faith: they may accompany it, but the instrument God uses is a word.
  • It sets the priority for the church. If faith comes by hearing, then whatever else a congregation does, it must ensure that the message is actually preached and actually heard. This is the biblical foundation of expositional preaching, and of your own daily habit of putting the text in front of yourself before anything else.
Note too that verse 16 stands in the way of any mechanical reading: "they have not all obeyed the gospel." Faith comes by hearing, and hearing does not always produce faith. The word is the means God uses; it is not a lever that works automatically. Which is why 9:16 and 10:17 belong in the same letter.

Two excuses, and the hands

But of Israel he says, "All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people." Romans 10:21, ESV

Paul closes the chapter by shutting two doors. Did they not hear? Psalm 19:4 says the message has gone out to the ends of the world, hyperbole for the gospel's spread, following the psalm's language about creation's witness. Did they not understand? Moses and Isaiah said this would happen; even Deuteronomy warned that God would provoke Israel to jealousy by "those who are not a nation". Ignorance is not available as a defence.

Then the final image, and it is deliberately jarring after chapter 9. Isaiah 65:1-2 in two halves: to the Gentiles, "I have been found by those who did not seek me", sovereign initiative, exactly matching 3:11's "no one seeks for God". And to Israel, "All day long I have held out my hands to a disobedient and contrary people": patient, persistent, unreciprocated invitation.

Hold those two halves together, because they are the whole unit in miniature. God finds people who were not looking for him, and God pleads with people who will not come. Romans does not resolve that into a tidy system, and neither should you. What it does is refuse to let either truth silence the other: sovereignty never becomes fatalism, and human responsibility never becomes the ground of salvation. The picture the chapter leaves you with is not a decree on a page but a pair of outstretched hands, held out all day long.

Application

  • About God He holds out his hands all day long. Whatever else you conclude about chapters 9-11, this is his posture toward the disobedient.
  • About you If faith comes by hearing, then your evangelism does not depend on your persuasiveness but on getting the message said. That should lower the pressure and raise the urgency at once.
  • Do Name one person who has not heard the gospel clearly from you. Ask God for one opportunity this month, and write down what you would actually say: Romans 10:9 gives you the content.
