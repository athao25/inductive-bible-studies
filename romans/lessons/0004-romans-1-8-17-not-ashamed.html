Lesson 0004 · Unit 1: Chapters 1:1-3:20

Romans 1:8-17: I Am Not Ashamed

~25 min · Passage: Romans 1:8-17 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Romans 1:8-17 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write at least five observations: what Paul thanks God for, how he describes his praying, how often he has planned to visit, what he hopes to give and to receive, the three "I am" statements in verses 14-16, and the Old Testament line that closes the paragraph.

Recall first

Observation check

  • Thanksgiving first (v. 8). Not for their giving or their leaders but for their faith, "proclaimed in all the world". Rome was the hub of the empire's road system; what happened there travelled.
  • "God is my witness" (v. 9). An oath. Paul is defending himself against a suspicion: that his long absence meant indifference toward a church he did not found.
  • "Without ceasing I mention you" (vv. 9-10). He has been praying for people he has never met, and praying specifically: that he might finally come.
  • "Often intended… but thus far have been prevented" (v. 13). Prevented by whom? Compare 15:22: "hindered from coming to you" by the work still unfinished elsewhere. Paul's frustrated plans are not failures of guidance.
  • He gives and receives (vv. 11-12). "That I may impart to you some spiritual gift" is immediately softened: "that we may be mutually encouraged by each other's faith." An apostle correcting himself mid-sentence toward humility.
  • "To Greeks and to barbarians, both to the wise and to the foolish" (v. 14). A merism: the two ends stand for everyone. And Paul calls it a debt, not a preference.
  • Three declarations (vv. 14-16). I am under obligation; I am eager; I am not ashamed. Debt, desire, and defiance in three short lines.
  • "For" four times over (vv. 15-17). Eager to preach, for I am not ashamed, for it is the power of God, for in it the righteousness of God is revealed. Each clause supplies the ground of the one before. This is the connector discipline in miniature.
  • "To the Jew first and also to the Greek" (v. 16). Not preference but sequence in redemptive history; the phrase returns at 2:9-10 with judgment attached to the same order.
  • "As it is written" (v. 17). Habakkuk 2:4. The theme is stated and then immediately grounded in Scripture, the pattern for the whole letter.

Interpretation

Why would anyone be ashamed?

For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek. Romans 1:16, ESV

Understatement is a rhetorical device: to say "not ashamed" in the capital of the world is to concede that shame was the expected response. Crucifixion was a slave's death, deliberately obscene; Cicero said the very word should be kept far from the ears of Roman citizens. Paul proposed to walk into that city and announce that a crucified Jewish carpenter is Lord and that the emperor is not. Every social instinct available said this was embarrassing.

His answer is not bravado but content. He is unashamed because of what the gospel is: dynamis theou, God's own power, operating for salvation. Note the logic carefully: the gospel does not merely point to the power; it carries it. That is why Paul's ambition is to preach (v. 15) rather than to demonstrate, argue, or impress. The instrument God has chosen to raise the dead is an announcement, which is why the argument returns at 10:14-17 to insist that faith comes by hearing.

And the reach is universal with one condition: "to everyone who believes". Those four words do two jobs at once. They knock down every wall of ethnicity and status: a point this congregation urgently needed. And they raise one door, narrow and open: faith. No one is excluded by category; no one enters by any other means.

Doctrine checkpoint: "from faith for faith" (1:17)
The Greek is literally "out of faith into faith", and it has been read several ways.
  • From God's faithfulness to our faith. Attractive, and true theologically, but the same word in the Habakkuk citation immediately following clearly means human faith.
  • From faith at the start to faith all the way through. Faith at conversion, faith for the whole Christian life, never faith and then something else. This is the reading that fits Paul's argument, and 1:5 with 16:26 supports it.
  • From one degree of faith to another. Possible, but it makes the phrase about growth in a paragraph that is about the source of righteousness.
Either way, the emphatic point is exclusivity: faith at every stage, and nothing beside it. Habakkuk 2:4 in its own context contrasts the puffed-up man with the righteous who lives by faithful trust while waiting for God to act against Babylon. Read Habakkuk 1-2 this week; it is a short book, and the whole of Romans grows out of one of its lines.

Mutual encouragement, and why an apostle needed it

that is, that we may be mutually encouraged by each other's faith, both yours and mine. Romans 1:12, ESV

Verse 11 begins as you would expect: the apostle comes to impart. Verse 12 corrects the impression before it hardens. He expects to be strengthened by the faith of people who have no office, no letters, and no missionary journeys behind them. It is a small sentence with large implications for how ministry works: gifts flow in both directions, and the man writing the most important theological document in history says so about a church he has never met.

Set this alongside verse 14, "I am under obligation". Paul's word is opheiletes, debtor. He does not owe the Greeks and barbarians anything they lent him; he owes them because Christ entrusted him with something that belongs to them as much as to him. That is a useful category for anyone who has received the gospel. What you hold is not a private possession but a deposit, and the debt is discharged only by delivery.

Application

  • About God He saves through a message. That should make you both bolder and humbler: bolder, because the power is not in your persuasiveness; humbler, for the same reason.
  • About you Where do you feel the pull of shame, not disbelief, but embarrassment? Name the room. Then answer it as Paul does, with content rather than nerve: what the gospel actually is.
  • Do Begin memory verse 1 today (1:16-17). Read it aloud ten times, then say it from memory five times, keeping the opening "For" attached. Then pray verse 12 for one person in your church: that you would be mutually encouraged this week, and arrange the conversation.

Watch: what is revealed and against what, what people do with the truth, what has been made plain and by what means, the two things "clearly perceived" in verse 20, and the exchange described in verse 23. Primary source
In your MacArthur Study Bible, read the notes on 1:16 (not ashamed, power, to the Jew first) and 1:17 (righteousness of God, from faith for faith). Then read Habakkuk 2:1-4 with Romans 1:17 open beside it.
Church prompt
Ask an older believer where they have felt ashamed of the gospel and what changed it. Most honest answers involve a specific relationship, not an abstract argument.

Questions welcome, always: "who prevented Paul from coming?", "does 'to the Jew first' still apply?", "if the gospel is God's power, why does anyone reject it?" Ask your teacher; the third question is chapters 9-11.
