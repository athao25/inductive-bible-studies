A renewed mind first produces an accurate estimate of yourself, then a recognition that you are one member among many in a body. Love without a mask follows: genuine, non-retaliatory, honouring, even toward enemies and rulers. Notice that the order runs outward: God, self, church, enemies, state, world. And every ring rests on the first. The reason you can bless a persecutor and defer to a scrupulous brother is that your body is already on the altar and your mind is being renewed.

The thing most readers miss

Romans is usually remembered for chapters 1-8 and argued about for 9-11. Chapters 12-16 are treated as the practical appendix. But look at what is actually in them: the vice list of 13:13 puts quarrelling and jealousy beside drunkenness; chapter 14 measures a dispute about food against the death of Christ; chapter 16 gives a page and a half to names. Paul evidently thought that a congregation's table manners, tax returns, and greetings were where eleven chapters of doctrine either proved themselves or did not.

Which raises the question this unit exists to press. If your grasp of justification, election and assurance has not changed how you speak about political leaders, whether you defer to a scrupulous believer, what you do when wronged, and whether you know the names of the people you worship beside, then something has been learned but not received. The doctrine was always headed for Monday.
