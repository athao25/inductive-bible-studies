Unit 3 Recap: No Condemnation, No Separation

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating Romans 6-8

Three chapters that begin with an accusation, "then let us sin!", and end with the most confident paragraph in the Bible. Today you retrieve the route between them.

Say it without looking

  1. Recite memory verses 6 (6:23), 7 (8:1), 8 (8:28-30) and 9 (8:38-39).
  2. In four sentences, explain why a justified person will not simply go on sinning.
  3. Name what chapter 7 has almost none of and chapter 8 has about twenty of, and say why that matters.

Recall: the whole unit

The three objections and their answers

ObjectionWherePaul's answer
"Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound?"6:1You died with Christ and were raised with him. Reckon it, refuse sin's orders, present yourself to God.
"Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace?"6:15Everyone is a slave of what they obey. Your obedience shows your owner, and the two masters pay differently.
"Is the law sin?"7:7By no means. The law is holy, righteous and good. Sin used the good command as a base of operations, which is exactly what a holy law is meant to expose.

Whatever you conclude about the identity of the "I" in chapter 7 (and careful believers differ) this contrast is the point Paul is making. Sanctification pursued by law produces the first column. Sanctification pursued by the Spirit produces the second.

Why chapter 9 follows chapter 8

The unit ends at the highest point in the letter and possibly in the New Testament: nothing in all creation can separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord. Then the next sentence is "I am speaking the truth in Christ; I am not lying… I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart" (9:1-2).

That is not a change of subject. It is the question chapter 8 provokes. If God's promises are unbreakable, then look at Israel: they had the covenants, the promises, the patriarchs, and the Christ came from them, and most of them are outside. Either the promises can fail, in which case chapter 8 is worthless, or something in our understanding of the promise needs correcting. Paul has staked the entire assurance of the church on the reliability of God's word, so he cannot leave the question alone.

So do not read chapters 9-11 as an excursus that a busy reader may skip. They are the defence of everything you have just learned to rest on. And notice how they end, not with a syllogism but with "Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God!" The unit that begins in anguish ends in worship.
