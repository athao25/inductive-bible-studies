Lesson 0010 · Unit 1: Chapters 1:1-3:20

Romans 3:9-20: None Is Righteous

~30 min · Passage: Romans 3:9-20 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Romans 3:9-20 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write at least five observations: the charge Paul says he has already made, how many times "no one" or "none" appears in verses 10-12, which parts of the body the quotations name and in what order, the two things verse 19 says the law accomplishes, and the reason given in verse 20 for why the law cannot justify.

Recall first

Observation check

  • "What then? Are we Jews any better off?" (v. 9). The diatribe again, and the answer is blunt: "No, not at all." Set it beside 3:1-2 and note the two different questions being asked: privilege versus standing.
  • "I have already charged" (v. 9). Paul summarises 1:18-3:8 as a legal indictment: "all, both Jews and Greeks, are under sin." Note the preposition: under, as under a power, not merely guilty of acts.
  • A chain of quotations (vv. 10-18). Six or seven Old Testament texts stitched together: Psalm 14:1-3 (or 53), Psalm 5:9, Psalm 140:3, Psalm 10:7, Isaiah 59:7-8, Psalm 36:1. A rabbinic technique called a catena, a chain.
  • The universal negatives (vv. 10-12). "None… no one… no one… not even one… all… no one… not even one." Seven denials in three verses. The repetition is the argument.
  • An anatomy lesson (vv. 13-18). Throat, tongues, lips, mouth, feet, eyes. The catalogue works down the body from throat to feet and ends at the eyes ("there is no fear of God before their eyes") which is the root of all the rest.
  • Speech dominates (vv. 13-14). Four of the six organs are organs of speech. Whatever else fallen humanity does, it talks, and Paul's chain says the talk is a grave, a poison, a curse.
  • "Whatever the law says it speaks to those who are under the law" (v. 19). An anticipated escape closed. The Jewish reader might have assumed those psalms described the wicked nations; Paul points out that Israel's own Scriptures were addressed to Israel.
  • Two purposes (v. 19). "So that every mouth may be stopped, and the whole world may be held accountable to God." A courtroom image: the defence rests, involuntarily.
  • The conclusion (v. 20). "By works of the law no human being will be justified in his sight, since through the law comes knowledge of sin." The law's function is diagnostic. It is a mirror, not a shower.

Interpretation

The verdict, and why it is stated in Israel's own words

as it is written: "None is righteous, no, not one; no one understands; no one seeks for God. All have turned aside; together they have become worthless; no one does good, not even one." Romans 3:10-12, ESV

Paul could have argued the point. Instead he quotes, and the choice is deliberate: the case against humanity is built entirely from Israel's own Scriptures, so the verdict cannot be dismissed as a Christian innovation. The chain also spans the canon: Psalms and Isaiah, worship and prophecy, and it culminates in a line about the fear of God, which is where wisdom begins (Prov 9:10). The last quotation names the missing foundation of everything above it.

Notice the three faculties denied in verse 11: none is righteous (the will), no one understands (the mind), no one seeks for God (the affections). This is what theologians came to call total depravity, and the label is regularly misunderstood. It does not mean that every person is as bad as they could be; verse 12 does not say people never do anything kind. It means sin has reached every faculty, so that no part of a person is left neutral and able to initiate a move toward God. Romans 8:7-8 will state the same thing as an inability: the mind of the flesh "does not submit to God's law; indeed, it cannot."

"No one seeks for God" deserves a pause, because it seems contradicted by every religious impulse in human history. Paul's answer is in chapter 1: people are intensely religious and are constantly seeking, but gods of their own manufacture, on terms they set. What no one does by nature is seek this God, on his terms. Which is why, when a person is found, the finding is credited elsewhere: "I have been found by those who did not seek me" (10:20, quoting Isaiah).

Doctrine checkpoint: what the law is for
Verse 20 gives the law's function in this argument: "through the law comes knowledge of sin." Reformed theology has traditionally spoken of three uses of the law, and it is worth having them straight, because most confusion about Paul and the law comes from mixing them up.
  • The civil use. Restraining evil in society, since even unbelievers are curbed by law and its penalties.
  • The pedagogical use (this passage). Exposing sin, stopping mouths, and driving the sinner to Christ. "Through the law comes knowledge of sin"; Galatians 3:24 makes it a guardian leading us to Christ.
  • The normative use. Guiding the believer in what pleases God, which is why 13:8-10 can say love fulfils the law and quote the commandments approvingly.
Romans 3:20 denies that the law can ever justify, not that it has value. Paul will insist at 7:12 that the law is "holy and righteous and good". A mirror is excellent at showing you your face and useless for washing it, and no one blames the mirror.

"Every mouth stopped"

Now we know that whatever the law says it speaks to those who are under the law, so that every mouth may be stopped, and the whole world may be held accountable to God. Romans 3:19, ESV

The image is a courtroom at the moment the defence gives up. The Greek verb means to be muzzled or fenced in: the defendant has run out of things to say. Everything since 1:18 has been aimed at this silence: the pagan silenced by creation's testimony, the moralist silenced by his own standard, the covenant member silenced by his own Scriptures.

And this is the necessary preparation for what comes next. The very next paragraph begins "But now" (3:21), and it is the hinge of the whole letter. A person still talking (still explaining, still comparing, still producing mitigations) is not yet ready to hear it. Grace is only good news to the silent. This is why the order of Romans cannot be rearranged, and why the honest place to be, at the end of this lesson, is with nothing to say.

One more observation before you go: verse 20 says "no human being will be justified in his sight". The phrase is from Psalm 143:2, where David prays "enter not into judgment with your servant, for no one living is righteous before you". David (the man after God's own heart) asks God not to put him on trial. That is the last word of the case for the prosecution, and it comes from the best man in the Old Testament.

Application

  • About God His standard does not bend, and his indictment is exact. The kindness in this passage is that it is honest: a doctor who softens the diagnosis is not being kind.
  • About you If verse 12 is true of you, then every scheme of self-improvement as a way to God is finished. That is not despair; it is the clearing of ground. You cannot be given a righteousness while you are still constructing one.
  • Do Begin memory verse 2 today (3:23-24), and learn both verses, never verse 23 alone. Then pray Psalm 143:2 back to God and stop talking for a minute afterwards.
