Lesson 0029 · Unit 3: Chapters 6-8

Romans 8:26-30: The Unbroken Chain

~30 min · Passage: Romans 8:26-30 (ESV, read first!)

Did you read it?
If you have not yet read Romans 8:26-30 twice in your ESV, stop and do that now. Write at least five observations: how the Spirit helps in weakness, what he does when we do not know what to pray, the two "we know" statements, the two qualifying phrases attached to verse 28, the five verbs of verses 29-30 and their order, and the tense of the last one.

Recall first

Observation check

  • "Likewise" (v. 26). It joins the Spirit's groaning to creation's and ours. Three groans, one chapter.
  • "The Spirit helps us in our weakness" (v. 26). The verb pictures taking hold of something alongside someone: the same word Martha uses of Mary in Luke 10:40.
  • The named weakness is prayer (v. 26). "We do not know what to pray for as we ought." Not a general lament about frailty but a specific admission about our ignorance in prayer.
  • "Groanings too deep for words" (v. 26). The intercession is the Spirit's, not ours; the groans are wordless.
  • "He who searches hearts" (v. 27). The Father, who reads the Spirit's mind directly. Intra-Trinitarian communication about you.
  • "According to the will of God" (v. 27). The Spirit's intercession is always aligned with God's will, which is exactly why it never fails.
  • "And we know" (v. 28). Certainty, not optimism.
  • Two qualifiers on verse 28. "For those who love God" and "for those who are called according to his purpose." The promise is not universal; it is covenantal.
  • "Good" is defined by verse 29. Not comfort or success but conformity "to the image of his Son". The next verse tells you what the good is.
  • Five links (vv. 29-30). Foreknew, predestined, called, justified, glorified. Each verb takes the same group as its object.
  • The chain has no leakage. "Those whom he… he also…" repeated four times, with no reduction at any stage.
  • "Glorified" is past tense (v. 30). Though verse 18 said the glory is still to be revealed. In God's purpose it is as good as done.
  • The purpose clause (v. 29). "In order that he might be the firstborn among many brothers." The goal of the whole chain is the Son's preeminence in a large family.

Interpretation

The prayer you cannot pray

Likewise the Spirit helps us in our weakness. For we do not know what to pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words. Romans 8:26, ESV

Paul's admission is more specific than it first appears. He does not say we are bad at praying, or that we lack discipline. He says we do not know what to pray for. Standing in front of a genuinely complicated grief, most Christians can produce words and cannot produce certainty that the words are the right ones. Should this end, or should it continue and be used? We do not know.

The answer is not better technique. It is that the Spirit takes hold alongside us and intercedes, and, crucially, his intercession is "according to the will of God" (v. 27). Where our request may be mistaken, his never is. So there is a second intercession running underneath your prayers, perfectly aimed, unfailingly heard. Note the pairing with 8:34, where Christ also intercedes: the Son pleads at the Father's right hand, the Spirit pleads from within the believer's weakness.

And "groanings too deep for words" completes the trio of the chapter. Creation groans under the curse; we groan awaiting our bodies; the Spirit groans in us as he intercedes. The Christian in a hard season is not outside the pattern of the age; they are inside it, and God himself is groaning with them.

Doctrine checkpoint: what "all things work together for good" actually promises
Probably the most quoted and most abused verse in the letter. Four observations restore it.
  • It does not say all things are good. It says they work together for good. The paragraph before it is about futility, bondage and groaning; Paul is not describing a world where nothing goes wrong.
  • It is limited by two clauses. "For those who love God, for those who are called according to his purpose." This is a promise for a defined people, not a general principle of optimism.
  • "Good" is defined by the next verse. Conformity to the image of the Son. Not health, safety, or a resolved situation: something better and considerably more costly.
  • Its guarantee is the chain of verses 29-30. Verse 28 is not wishful thinking because verses 29-30 name a purpose that God has been executing since before creation and will not abandon.
Said pastorally: never quote verse 28 at someone without verse 29. Alone it sounds like a claim that their loss was secretly fine. With verse 29 it says something harder and truer: that God is making them like his Son, and will not waste even this.

The chain, link by link

And those whom he predestined he also called, and those whom he called he also justified, and those whom he justified he also glorified. Romans 8:30, ESV

Theologians call this the golden chain. Five verbs, one subject throughout (God) and one object carried unchanged from end to end. Nobody enters in the middle, and nobody drops out along the way.

  • Foreknew. Not "knew in advance what they would do". The object is people, not facts: "those whom he foreknew". In biblical usage to know someone is to set your love on them (Amos 3:2; Jer 1:5), which is why 11:2 says "God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew."
  • Predestined. Marked out beforehand, and verse 29 says what for: to be conformed to the image of his Son. The destiny is not merely arrival in heaven but likeness to Christ.
  • Called. Not the general invitation: that goes out to many who never come. This calling always works, since everyone called here is also justified. See effectual calling.
  • Justified. The verdict of 3:21-5:21, now placed inside a larger purpose.
  • Glorified. Stated in the past tense though still future (8:18, 23). God speaks of it as accomplished because his purpose does not fail.

The doctrinal weight is unavoidable, and this course states it plainly: salvation from beginning to end is God's work, planned before creation and carried to completion by him. MacArthur, White and Durbin agree here without qualification, and the chain is the strongest single argument in Scripture for the perseverance of the saints. The number justified equals the number glorified.

But notice what Paul is doing with the doctrine, because this is where many discussions of predestination go wrong. He is not settling a debate; he is comforting sufferers. The chain sits between "the sufferings of this present time" (v. 18) and "if God is for us, who can be against us?" (v. 31). Its function is pastoral: the reason you can be certain that God will not waste your grief is that he has been working on this since before the world existed and has never yet lost anyone. Predestination in Romans is not a cold doctrine for arguing; it is the floor under a groaning believer.

Application

  • About God He plans, calls, acquits and glorifies without losing anyone in transit. His work is not a rescue attempt but a purpose.
  • About you The good God is producing in you is likeness to Christ. Measure this year against that goal rather than against comfort, and the assessment changes.
  • Do Begin memory verse 8 today (8:28-30), all three verses together, never verse 28 alone. Then pray for a situation you do not know how to pray about, say so honestly to God, and rest on verse 26.
