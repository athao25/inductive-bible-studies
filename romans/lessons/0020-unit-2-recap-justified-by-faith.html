Unit 2 Recap: Justified by Faith

~25 min · No new passage · Consolidating Romans 3:21-5:21

Two words opened this unit ("But now") and by the end of it you have a mercy seat, two witnesses, a peace treaty, and two men in whom the whole race stands. Today you retrieve it and say it in your own words.

Say it without looking

  1. Recite memory verses 3 (3:28), 4 (5:1) and 5 (5:8), then say 1:16-17 after them.
  2. In four sentences, explain how God can justify the ungodly without ceasing to be just.
  3. Name the two Old Testament witnesses of chapter 4 and what each proves.
Check your answers
(2) Something like: God put Christ forward as a propitiation, a mercy seat, whose blood turned away the wrath our sin deserved. That death vindicated God's justice for the sins he had passed over in earlier centuries and for those he forgives now. Therefore he is both just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus. And the righteousness we receive is not produced in us but credited to us, so it can be a gift rather than a wage. (3) Abraham, counted righteous in Genesis 15:6 before he was circumcised and before the law existed, proving that justification is by faith apart from works and apart from the sign; and David in Psalm 32, blessed as the man against whom the Lord will not count sin, proving that the same transaction covers the guilty.

Recall: the whole unit

Four pictures of one salvation

PictureWhere it comes fromWhat it saysText
JustificationThe law courtThe judge declares the guilty righteous, on another's account3:24, 28; 5:1
RedemptionThe slave marketRelease secured by the payment of a price3:24
PropitiationThe templeA sacrifice that turns away wrath; the mercy seat displayed3:25
ReconciliationPersonal relationshipsEnemies made friends, at the offended party's initiative and cost5:10-11

Four angles on one event. Notice that each answers a different problem: guilt needs a verdict, bondage needs a price, wrath needs a sacrifice, and hostility needs a reconciler. Any account of the cross that keeps only one of the four will eventually distort. Romans keeps all four in twenty-six verses.

The argument of the unit

TextMove
3:21-26"But now": righteousness apart from law, through faith, grounded in the propitiation, so that God is just and justifier.
3:27-31Therefore boasting is excluded, and since God is one, Jew and Gentile enter the same way.
4:1-8Proof from Abraham (counted, not earned) and David (sin not counted).
4:9-17Proof from the calendar: before circumcision, before the law. Hence the promise rests on grace and is guaranteed.
4:18-25What such faith looks like, and it is written for us, who believe in the God who raised Jesus.
5:1-11The results: peace, access, hope, endurance, the Spirit, and a love proved at the cross while we were enemies.
5:12-21The foundation underneath it all: two men, two acts, two reigns, and grace super-abounding.

What this unit settles, and what it leaves open

Settled: how a guilty person can be right with God. The answer is a righteousness from outside, credited by God, received by faith, purchased at the cross, and secured by the resurrection. Settled too: that this was always God's way, demonstrable from Genesis and the Psalms, and that it applies identically to Jew and Gentile.

Left open, deliberately: what happens to sin afterwards. Paul has just said that where sin increased, grace abounded all the more (5:20), and he knows exactly what that sounds like. He heard the caricature back at 3:8. So the next unit opens with the obvious objection, asked by Paul himself: "Are we to continue in sin that grace may abound?" The answer takes three chapters and ends with the most confident paragraph in the Bible.

One last thing to notice before you move on. The unit began with a courtroom and ended with a throne. Justification is a verdict, but it is not merely an acquittal that leaves you where you were. Those who receive the abundance of grace "reign in life" (5:17). Keep that phrase in view through chapters 6-8; it is the difference between being let off and being raised up.
