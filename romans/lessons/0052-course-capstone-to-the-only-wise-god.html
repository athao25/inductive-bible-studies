Lesson 0052 · Course capstone: Chapters 1-16

Capstone: To the Only Wise God

~40 min · No new passage · The whole of Romans in view

Fifty-one lessons ago you read two verses about not being ashamed of the gospel. Today you look back down the whole letter, test what has stayed, and decide what happens next. Take longer over this one than usual.

Recall: the whole letter

Say the letter in seven sentences

Without looking, write one sentence for each movement. Then compare.

A model answer
  1. 1:1-17. Paul, a slave of Christ set apart for God's gospel, is unashamed of it, because in it God's righteousness is revealed from faith for faith, to Jew and Greek alike.
  2. 1:18-3:20. God's wrath is revealed against all who suppress the truth: the pagan who exchanged his glory for images, the moralist condemned by his own standard, and the covenant member condemned by his own Scriptures, until every mouth is stopped.
  3. 3:21-5:21. But now, apart from the law, God has manifested a righteousness through faith in Christ, whom he put forward as a propitiation, so that he might be just and the justifier; proved from Abraham and David, issuing in peace, access and hope, and grounded in one man's obedience answering another man's fall.
  4. 6-8. Grace does not license sin, because those in Christ died and rose with him; the law could never break sin's power, but the Spirit does, making us sons and heirs who groan for the redemption of our bodies and cannot be separated from God's love.
  5. 9-11. God's word has not failed, because the promise never rested on descent: he has mercy on whom he wills, keeps a remnant by grace, hardens partially and temporarily, grafts Gentiles into a Jewish root, and will not repent of his gifts and calling.
  6. 12-15:13. Therefore, by those mercies, present your bodies, be renewed in mind, use your gifts for one body, love without a mask, submit to authorities, owe only love, and welcome the believer who differs, as Christ welcomed you.
  7. 15:14-16:27. Paul's priestly mission, his plans held under God's will, and a page of names: ending, as chapter 11 ended, in glory to the only wise God.

What Romans has given you that you did not have

  • A vocabulary. Justification, propitiation, imputation, adoption, sanctification, election, hardening, remnant, flesh. Not jargon: precision, so that you can say what you mean about God.
  • A method for arguments. Follow the connectors. Identify the speaker. Read the paragraph before and after. Chase the quotation to its source. You will use that on every book of the Bible.
  • A defence against two errors. Legalism, which seeks to establish its own righteousness (10:3), and antinomianism, which thinks grace makes holiness optional (6:1). Romans closes both doors permanently.
  • An anchor for hard days. 8:1 for the accusing morning, 8:28-30 for the incomprehensible loss, 8:38-39 for the hospital room, 5:8 when God's love cannot be felt.
  • A reason to be humble about other Christians. Chapters 9-11 remove every ground for superiority; chapter 14 removes the right to police another's conscience.
  • A worship instinct. Twice the letter's argument breaks into doxology, at 11:33-36 and 16:25-27. Doctrine that does not end there has not finished.
The three places this course argued and told you it was arguing
A capstone should be honest about its own commitments. This course reads Romans in the Reformed tradition, and on these points it took a position and showed its work rather than pretending there was no debate:
  • Romans 7:14-25. Read as the believer attempting sanctification by law rather than by the Spirit, which is why the Spirit is absent from the paragraph and floods chapter 8. MacArthur reads it as normal Christian experience; others as pre-conversion. All agree that law cannot deliver.
  • Romans 9. Read as unconditional election of individuals to salvation, with hardening as God's judicial confirming of rebels. The corporate dimension is real and does not replace the individual one. The objections Paul anticipates at 9:14 and 9:19 are the strongest evidence for this reading.
  • Romans 11:26. Read as pointing to a future large-scale salvation of Jewish people, held without dogmatism, and with the firm insistence that on any reading Israel is saved through Christ and not apart from him.
You are not obliged to agree. You are obliged to argue from the text, as Paul does, and to keep 14:1 in force toward those who land elsewhere.

The honest self-assessment

Answer these in writing, for yourself, not for anyone else.

  1. Can you explain justification to a non-Christian in two minutes, without jargon?
  2. Can you say why grace does not encourage sin, using Romans 6 rather than a warning?
  3. When you sinned last week, did you go to 8:1 or to self-punishment?
  4. Has your speech about political leaders changed since 13:7?
  5. Is there a believer you were despising in chapter 14 terms whom you have since welcomed?
  6. Has anyone heard the gospel from you during this course (10:14)?
  7. Does your grasp of election make you pray more or argue more (10:1)?
  8. Are your convictions on the disputed passages held from the text, and held charitably?

Where to go next

  • Read Romans again, straight through, twice a year. An hour each time. Nothing else you can do with sixty minutes will pay as well.
  • Take Galatians next if you want the same doctrine at white heat and half the length, or Ephesians for the same gospel with the church in view. If you have not done them, the Matthew, John and Isaiah courses are here, and the Trinity course walks one doctrine through its texts.
  • Teach it. The fastest way to find out what you actually understand is to explain a paragraph of Romans to somebody else. Start with 3:21-26.
  • Keep the reference pages. The book map, glossary and memory verses are built to outlive the course.

The last word

Now to him who is able to strengthen you according to my gospel and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of the mystery that was kept secret for long ages but has now been disclosed and through the prophetic writings has been made known to all nations, according to the command of the eternal God, to bring about the obedience of faith: to the only wise God be glory forevermore through Jesus Christ! Amen. Romans 16:25-27, ESV

The letter ends with a promise and a doxology. The promise is that God is able to strengthen you, which is what you will need, because knowing Romans and living it are not the same task and the second one takes the rest of your life. The doxology tells you what all of it is for: not your comfort, though comfort comes; not your certainty, though certainty comes; but glory to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, forever.

You have read the fullest statement of the Christian gospel that exists. The right response is the one Paul himself models twice: stop arguing for a moment, and worship. Then get up and present your body.
