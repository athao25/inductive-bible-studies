# Mission: Expositional Study of Romans (All 16 Chapters)

## Why
To know God more truly and to hold the gospel more firmly by working carefully, verse by verse, through the fullest statement of the Christian gospel in Scripture. Romans answers the questions a believer must be able to answer: why anyone needs saving, how God can acquit the guilty and stay just, what happens to sin after grace, whether God keeps his promises, and what all of it looks like on an ordinary Monday. The goal is a changed life and a settled assurance, not a stocked memory.

## Success looks like
- Can state the argument of Romans in seven sentences, movement by movement, without help
- Can explain justification, propitiation, imputation, adoption, sanctification and election in plain words
- Can trace the logic of any paragraph by its connectors ("for", "therefore", "but now", "much more")
- Can identify the diatribe objections and say who is speaking each line
- Has memorised the twelve key verses listed in reference/memory-verses.html
- Can answer both standard objections to free grace: that it licenses sin (ch. 6) and that it makes God unjust (ch. 9)
- Can hold Romans 9 and Romans 10 together without silencing either
- Treats a believer who differs on a disputable matter the way chapter 14 requires
- Regularly discusses what is being learned with people at church

## Constraints
- 20-30 minutes per day, daily cadence
- ESV is the study translation; owned tools are the MacArthur Study Bible and Olive Tree Bible Software
- Lessons stand alone: method foundations are retaught in lesson 0001, and every technical term is defined on first use and kept in the glossary
- Reformed framework. On this letter's central doctrines (justification by faith alone, imputed righteousness, total inability, unconditional election in chapter 9, the security of the believer in chapter 8) MacArthur, White and Durbin agree, and lessons say so plainly. Live checkpoints handled from the text: 2:6-16 (judgment according to works), 7:14-25 (who the "I" is), 8:29 (foreknew), 11:26 ("all Israel"), 13:1-7 (the limits of submission), 14 (which matters are disputable)
- Where MacArthur's dispensational framework shapes his reading of 11:25-27 more than the others', lessons say so rather than smoothing it over
- Workflow per session: read the passage in ESV first and jot observations, then open the lesson

## Out of scope
- Greek language study (English-tools word study only)
- Academic critical scholarship beyond briefly answering authorship, date and setting
- The New Perspective on Paul debate as a topic in its own right; where it touches a text (3:20 "works of the law", 4:4-5), the lesson argues from the passage rather than surveying the literature
- Systematic theology as a separate track; doctrine is taken up where Romans raises it
- Detailed eschatology beyond what 8:18-25, 11:25-27 and 13:11-12 require
